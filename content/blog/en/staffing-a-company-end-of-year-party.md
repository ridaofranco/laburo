A company end-of-year party looks like just another event, but it falls in the worst season for building a team: everyone wants to celebrate in the same weeks, on the same nights, with the same good people. This guide is for whoever organises it (production company, agency or HR) and wants to reach December with the staff confirmed.

## Why November and December are different

You don't need statistics to understand it, the logic of the calendar is enough:

- **Dates pile up.** Almost every company wants to celebrate before Christmas, and almost all prefer Thursday, Friday or Saturday. That leaves few possible nights for a huge number of events.
- **Different types of event compete.** Company parties, year-end closings, social events and season launches all share the same weeks. They all need waiters, bar and door staff.
- **The pool of experienced people doesn't multiply.** A good bartender can work one party a night. If they get three offers for the same Friday, they pick one, and usually it's the one that arrived first with clear conditions.
- **Public holidays shift the calendar.** A long weekend changes which nights the company wants and who is available to work.

The practical conclusion is just one: in peak season, confirming early is worth more than at any other time of year.

## The backwards timeline

The safest way not to be late is to build the timeline from the party date backwards. The order matters, because each step depends on the previous one.

### 1. Date and venue first

Without a date and a venue you can't ask anyone for a price or know how many people you need. Choose the night by looking at the holiday calendar (below) and book the venue by real capacity, not by "everyone will fit". The basics are in [choosing an event venue by capacity](/en/blog/choosing-an-event-venue-by-capacity).

### 2. Suppliers next

With the venue confirmed you know what it includes and what it doesn't: whether it has a kitchen, a fixed bar, its own sound, a cloakroom. Each supplier you add may or may not bring their own staff, and that defines how much staff you need to find yourself. Always ask in writing.

### 3. Staff, as soon as you have the above

With venue and suppliers closed you can write the headcount: how many people per role and at what hours. That's the moment to send the offers, not the week before. In December, every day you wait is a day another company confirms the same person.

### 4. The week before

Check who accepted and who didn't, send the final brief (call time, dress code, address, who to report to) and confirm the backup list.

## What roles a company party needs

The list changes with the format (dinner with a show, cocktail party, party with a DJ), but these positions almost always appear:

- **Reception and check-in.** They welcome guests, check the list and direct people.
- **Cloakroom.** In summer (December is summer in Argentina) it seems unnecessary, but there are handbags, backpacks from people coming straight from the office, and gifts. If the venue doesn't cover it, add it.
- **Waiters.** Table or tray service, depending on the format. The number depends on whether it's a served dinner or a cocktail party.
- **Bar.** Bartenders and bar backs. The bar is where a shortage of people shows most: a long queue can be seen from the whole dance floor. The guide on [hiring bartenders for a corporate event](/en/blog/hiring-bartenders-for-a-corporate-event) has the details.
- **Dishwasher,** if there's a kitchen or tableware to wash during the event.
- **Door.** Door security staff for entry and control during the night.
- **Technical crew.** Sound, lighting and video, especially if there are speeches, a company presentation or a show.
- **Guest coordination,** if there are assigned tables, awards or special guests.

For the maths of how many people per role, the method is in [how many staff for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event). For the December rush in general, read [booking event staff for December](/en/blog/booking-event-staff-for-december).

## Public holidays in November and December 2026 (Argentina)

According to the official calendar of national holidays on argentina.gob.ar:

| Date | What it is | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday 9 November | Visit of Pope Leo XIV | Fixed holiday |
| Tuesday 10 November | Visit of Pope Leo XIV | Holiday in Córdoba and Buenos Aires City |
| Wednesday 11 November | Visit of Pope Leo XIV | Holiday in Buenos Aires Province |
| Monday 23 November | National Sovereignty Day (20 Nov) | Movable holiday |
| Monday 7 December | Non-working day for tourism purposes | Tourism |
| Tuesday 8 December | Immaculate Conception | Fixed holiday |
| Friday 25 December | Christmas | Fixed holiday |

What to look at when choosing the night:

- **The weekend of 5 to 8 December** becomes a long one (Saturday, Sunday, tourism Monday and holiday Tuesday). Many people go away, and that hits both the company's attendance and staff availability.
- **The week of 21 December** ends with Christmas on Friday. A party that week competes with family celebrations.
- **The calendar can change by decree.** Check the official page before closing the date.

## Plan B for dropouts

In peak season dropouts weigh more, because replacing someone in the same week is harder than in March. Three things help:

1. **Count as covered only those who accepted.** An offer that was sent or seen is not a confirmed person.
2. **Keep a buffer in the critical roles:** door, bar and service.
3. **Have a short backup list** of people who have already worked with you, and tell them in advance they might be called.

The step by step for when the dropout has already happened is in [event staff no-show: what to do](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do).

You're one short. Two hours to go. The bar opens regardless.

That's the real situation, and it has two different answers: what to do **now**, and what you should have had in place **before** so that now wasn't a problem. Both matter, but the second one is what makes the difference.

## Now: the moves, in order

**1. Pin down exactly what fell through.** It's not "I'm one short": it's "I'm missing a barback at bar 2 from 9 p.m.". An empty position at teardown and one at the door have neither the same urgency nor the same solution.

**2. Reassign with what's already on site.** The fastest replacement is always someone already inside. If you're short in a critical role and have spare capacity in one that isn't, you've moved the problem to where it hurts least and bought time.

**3. Call someone who has worked with you.** Second stop, not a last resort. Someone whose work you've seen is an infinitely better bet than an unknown profile two hours before the event. That's why it pays to have that list marked beforehand: on LABURO you can mark profiles as favourites, with their experience and availability visible, precisely so you don't have to rebuild that list from memory at the worst moment.

**4. Only then, look outside.** And search properly: by role, by province and city, and by real availability. A detail that matters at this point is how much notice each person needs to take a job (some declare immediate availability, others need 24 or 48 hours' notice) and whether they have their own transport, which at the last minute and in an area with no public transport can decide whether they make it.

A detail that saves wasted calls: don't offer the shift to someone already assigned to another of your events that same night. LABURO's search lets you hide people who are already assigned.

**5. Send the offer in writing anyway.** Especially now. Role, event, when, where, pay and conditions. The person opens it on their phone and accepts with one click, and only then **are they confirmed for that event**. A replacement secured verbally over the phone, in a rush, is exactly the same problem you're trying to solve.

## Before: the three things that prevent the scene

### 1. That "confirmed" means confirmed

The number one gap isn't that people let you down: it's that **they were never as confirmed as you thought**.

An "ok" in a WhatsApp group, a read receipt, an unanswered message. All of that gets mentally logged as "they're in", and they're not.

A confirmation is an explicit acceptance of written terms: role, date, hours, place, how much is paid and when. On LABURO each person receives that offer with everything included and accepts or declines it from the link; the producer sees the real status of each one: **sent, seen, accepted, declined or expired**.

And offers **expire**. An expired offer isn't a pending "yes": it's an open spot. So that this isn't discovered too late, anyone with an offer close to expiring gets an automatic reminder before it expires. The point isn't to rush anyone: it's to turn silence into information before the day of the event.

### 2. That coverage is a number, not a feeling

If you haven't written down how many people you need per role, you can't know how many you're missing.

With the headcount entered (10 waiters, 4 at the bar, 5 at access), LABURO's board shows per event **how many spots are covered and how many are missing**, counting only accepted offers. Declined and expired ones appear as an open role, which is the signal to "search here again".

The practical difference: you find out you're two short on Tuesday, not on Saturday at seven in the evening.

### 3. That the person who can't come gives early notice

A good share of absences in this industry aren't people who vanish: they're people who didn't know who to tell, or who told you late because telling you was awkward.

That's why anyone with a confirmed shift gets a reminder the day before or on the day, with the role, the event and the hours, and with an explicit request: if something has come up and you can't make it, let us know right now on WhatsApp.

A no-show reported hours in advance can be covered. The same no-show discovered with the doors open can't. Making it easy and penalty-free to give notice is, in practice, the cheapest measure there is against empty positions.

## On the day: who actually arrived

Confirmed is not the same as present. At events where it's enabled, staff **clock in and out** from their dashboard, and the real hours are recorded.

That serves two purposes: so payroll isn't put together from memory, and so the gap shows up early. A call time earlier than when you actually need people exists precisely for this: so you know at 7 p.m. who has arrived, not at 8, when you've already opened.

## The no-show checklist

- Do I have the headcount written down by role and quantity?
- Am I counting as covered only those who accepted?
- Have I checked how many offers are expired or declined?
- Do I have a buffer at the door, till and bar?
- Do I have the people who've worked with me marked, and do I know how to reach them fast?
- Is the call time earlier than when I really need them?
- Do people know who to tell, and how, if they can't make it?

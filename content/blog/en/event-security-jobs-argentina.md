At the door of an event there is more than one job, and in the eyes of the law they are not all the same. Scanning a ticket, deciding who gets into a nightclub and protecting a person are different tasks, with different rules. This guide sets out which control roles exist in Argentina, what's expected and how to work with the public and with a radio. We summarise the legal side from two guides we have already written with the official text in hand.

> **Note:** this is general information to help you find your way, not legal advice. For your own case, check the regulation in force in your jurisdiction.

## Three things that get mixed up

- **Access control and registration.** Validating tickets, scanning a QR code, handing out wristbands or badges, managing the queue and directing people. It is most of the door work at conferences, trade shows and corporate events. It is not private security, nor admission and permanence.
- **Admission and permanence control.** The staff who decide who gets in and who stays at events and musical, artistic and entertainment shows. In Argentina it is regulated by **national Law 26,370**, implemented by **Decree 1824/2009**, with its own card and credential. It is explained in [the guide on Law 26,370](/en/blog/argentina-law-26370-door-staff).
- **Private security.** Guarding, escorting and protecting people and property. In Buenos Aires City it is provided by a licensed company, with guards entered in the official register. We cover it in detail in [private security at events in Buenos Aires](/en/blog/private-security-at-events-buenos-aires).

If the same person has to scan tickets and contain a situation, they will do both badly. That is why each task should be stated in the offer.

## What the law says, in short

What follows comes from those two guides, which quote the official text.

**Admission and permanence (Law 26,370).** Among other requirements, it asks for being over 18, two years of actual residence in Argentina, completed secondary school, a criminal record certificate, a psychological fitness certificate, the technical certificate obtained by passing the course and being on the payroll **as an employee**. The licence is issued by the authority in each jurisdiction and the professional card is valid for one year. The credential must be visible, at chest height on the left side. The law bans working armed, without a licence or under the influence of alcohol. Each province adheres to the law and sets up its own register, so the procedure depends on where you work.

**Private security in Buenos Aires City (Book VI of City Law 5688).** The guard has to be entered in the register, with requirements such as completed secondary school, being of age, psychophysical fitness and qualifying training, and must wear their credential visibly in places open to the public. For mass events and nightclubs, a 2024 regulation requires a specialisation course from the city's Higher Institute of Public Security. In escort and door work at live shows, the use of weapons is prohibited.

Beyond this, we don't give requirements: if someone tells you they can license you for something, ask them to show you the regulation.

## What's generally expected

Beyond the paperwork, every control role values the same things:

- **Punctuality.** The door is set up before opening: walk-through, briefing, who is on each entrance.
- **Friendly firmness.** Saying no, or that people have to wait, without raising your voice and without arguing.
- **Sustained attention.** The entrance rush demands pace; the rest of the night, keeping watch without getting distracted by your phone.
- **Knowing the venue.** Emergency exits, where the first aid point is, who to call for each thing.
- **Staying until the end.** The door is one of the last posts to close, and that has to be in the hours on the offer.

## Dealing with the public

Law 26,370 requires **equal and respectful treatment** and that the right of admission be based on **objective conditions**, not discriminatory or arbitrary ones. In practice:

- **The same rules for everyone.** What applies to one person applies to the next.
- **Explain the reason,** briefly and without getting into an argument: "we're at full capacity", "I can't let you in without a ticket".
- **Don't handle alone what's beyond you.** An aggressive person, someone feeling unwell, a fight: you call your lead, security or the first aid point.
- **Protect people's data.** What you see on a list or an ID document is not discussed.

## Radio communication

At many events the door is coordinated by radio. The basics:

- **Before opening,** confirm which channel your area uses, who your lead is and where the meeting point is if the radio fails.
- **Speak briefly:** who you are calling, who you are, where you are and what is happening. Wait for an answer before going on.
- **Don't tie up the channel** with chat. Someone may be waiting to report something urgent.
- **Don't say guests' personal details** over the radio.
- **Watch your battery** and report it before it dies, not after.

How radios are allocated by area at a show is in [concert and live show staffing](/en/blog/concert-and-live-show-staffing).

In your request, separate access, admission and security. To work out how many people the door needs, see [how many staff for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event). If you run a licensed security company in Argentina, you can be listed on LABURO as a supplier for free.

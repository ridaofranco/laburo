A venue inquiry is someone who has already looked at your listing, seen your photos and decided to write to you. What happens in your reply decides whether that person comes to see the venue or moves on to the next one on the list. This guide goes in order: what to answer first, what to ask back, how to offer the tour, how to state deadlines and the deposit, and how to follow up.

## How the inquiry reaches you on LABURO

When someone fills in the form on your listing in the LABURO venue directory, the inquiry arrives at the email address you entered when you listed. If you hit reply, the email goes to the person who asked, not to LABURO. Each inquiry is also saved in your dashboard, with a note of whether the email went out or not: if you ever don't see it in your inbox, it's there. If the person left their phone number, you can also message them on WhatsApp from the dashboard.

If you haven't built your own form, the inquiry always includes the same things:

- **Type of event** (wedding, quinceañera, birthday, corporate event, private party or other).
- **The day.**
- **The hours**, from what time to what time (optional, so it doesn't always come).
- **How many people** (required: without it you don't know if they'll fit).
- **How they picture the event**, in their own words.
- **An approximate budget**, if they chose to add one.
- **Name and email** of the person asking, and their phone number if they left it.

If you're always missing the same detail (whether they need a kitchen, whether they bring their own caterer, whether there will be minors), you can add it as a question in your form from the dashboard. On your listing, the person has already read that you'll send price and availability by email after reading the inquiry: that's what they're waiting for.

## The first thing your reply has to say

People who inquire usually write to several venues on the same day. The first lines of your email have to answer three things, in this order:

1. **Whether the date is free.** If it isn't, say so in the first sentence and offer the nearest free date, or the other slot on that same day if you have one.
2. **Whether the venue works for that number in that layout.** 120 people seated at tables is not the same as 120 at a standing cocktail with a bar. Say which layout gives you the number they asked for. If it doesn't, say that too: it's better to lose the inquiry today than to have an overcrowded venue on the day. The full capacity logic is in [choosing an event venue by capacity](/en/blog/choosing-an-event-venue-by-capacity).
3. **What's included and what isn't.** Furniture, sound, kitchen, cleaning staff, parking, setup times. If the client has to bring something or hire it separately, put it here and not in the contract.

After that comes the price, if you can already give it, or what you still need to know to give it. For the proposal itself (line items, terms, validity) read [how to reply to an event quote request](/en/blog/how-to-reply-to-an-event-quote-request).

## What to ask back

Asking questions is fine as long as each one helps you quote. The ones most often missing:

- **The exact hours**, if they weren't included, and whether they need to get in earlier to set up decoration or technical equipment.
- **The layout**: seated, standing, with a dance floor, with a stage.
- **Whether they're bringing their own caterer or suppliers**, and which ones.
- **Whether there will be minors**, at quinceañeras or birthdays.
- **How guests will get there**: by car, on a shuttle or by public transport.

Put all your questions in a single message, numbered. Five questions in one email get answered; five emails with one question each wear people out.

## How to offer the tour

If the date is free and the capacity works, the next step is for them to see the venue. Offer **two or three specific times** ("Tuesday 6 pm, Thursday 11 am or Saturday 10 am"), not "drop by whenever you like". If you can, make one of them similar to the time of the event: seeing a venue in the morning with daylight is not the same as at night.

For the tour, have ready what people usually ask about: the floor plan or measurements, where the bar goes, where suppliers come in, where the toilets and the emergency exit are, where to park. If your venue is in Buenos Aires City, they may also ask about your permit: what's worth knowing is in [event venue permits in Buenos Aires City](/en/blog/event-venue-permit-in-buenos-aires-city). How to run the visit itself is in [how to prepare an event venue tour](/en/blog/how-to-prepare-an-event-venue-tour).

## Booking deadlines and deposit, stated clearly

The part that causes most arguments later is the one least often written down at the start. In the same email where you give the price, or at the latest after the tour, put in writing:

- **Until when you hold the date** with no commitment, with an exact day.
- **How much the deposit is, how it's paid and from when the date is confirmed.**
- **What happens if they cancel**, and whether that changes depending on how much notice they give.
- **When the balance is paid.**
- **What's included in the price** and what's charged separately (extra hours, additional cleaning, extra furniture).

The terms are yours. What matters is that they're written down, with dates rather than "a few days before", and that they're the same for everyone.

## The follow-up

If you sent the price and didn't hear back, a follow-up message isn't a nuisance: very often the person is waiting for an answer from their boss, their family or another venue. Keep it short and with something new: "I'm still holding the date until Friday; after that I'll release it". If there's no reply, a second message a few days later is enough. Anything more stops being follow-up.

If they say no, ask why in a single line: price, date, capacity or location. That answer tells you what to change on your listing. And check from time to time that your listing says what you answer in your emails: [how to write an event venue listing](/en/blog/how-to-write-an-event-venue-listing) has the step by step.

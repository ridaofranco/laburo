If you have already worked as a barista in a café, you know how to grind, extract and steam milk. At an event you'll use all of that, but in different conditions: a station set up that day, guests who arrive in a block and a shift that looks nothing like one in a shop. This guide covers what changes, what's expected and how to fill in your profile so dates come your way.

## What changes compared with a café

In a café you know your machine, your grinder and your water. You dial in in the morning and adjust bit by bit for the rest of the day. At an event almost none of that is a given:

- **The station is mobile.** It might be a bar in a hotel lobby, a counter on a booth or a table in a function room. It is up to you to arrange everything so you can work comfortably without crossing paths with guests.
- **The machine may not be yours.** It is often supplied by whoever hires you or by a rental company. Arrive early to get to know it and dial it in.
- **Water and power rule.** Ask beforehand whether there is a socket nearby, how much load it can take and where the water comes from. It is the first thing that goes wrong at an event station.
- **Guests arrive in rushes.** At a conference, everyone leaves the room at the same time and you have fifteen or twenty minutes to serve a whole queue. Between rushes, you restock, clean and prepare for the next one.
- **The menu is short.** Usually a handful of options (espresso, cortado, milk coffee, a plant-based alternative). Whoever hires you decides it, not you.

## Where the role comes up

The formats that ask for baristas most often are:

- **Conferences, conventions and corporate days** with coffee breaks between talks. If that world interests you, see [conference and convention staff](/en/blog/conference-and-convention-staff).
- **Trade show booths**, where coffee is the excuse for visitors to come over and stay chatting with the brand.
- **Brand activations**, sometimes with a drink or a cup designed for the occasion.
- **Weddings and parties** with a coffee station after dinner.

Each one has a different pace: at a trade show the flow is steadier through the day, at a conference it is all or nothing.

## What's expected

**Consistent coffee at the peak.** The first cortado and the hundredth have to come out the same. If the queue rushes you and you start cutting corners, it shows in the cup.

**The station ready before opening.** Machine hot, grinder dialled in, cold milk to hand, cups or glasses lined up, sugar, sweetener, spoons and napkins in place. Ask what time you need to arrive to set up.

**Constant cleaning.** Purge the steam wand, clean the portafilter, dry the bar, clear the grounds. At an event lots of people are watching you at once, and a dirty station looks bad for the brand or company that hired you.

**Knowing what you serve.** If someone asks whether there is lactose-free milk or a plant-based option, you need to know before the station opens.

**Handling the queue.** They are someone else's guests. Patience, a friendly answer if something has run out and, if someone gets difficult, you tell your supervisor.

**Coordinating with the rest of the team.** Coffee often shares space with the bar, waiters or runners. If you want to see how they work next to you, read [event bartender jobs](/en/blog/event-bartender-jobs) and [event waiter jobs](/en/blog/event-waiter-jobs).

## The food handler certificate

In Argentina, article 21 of the Argentine Food Code requires a food handler certificate for anyone who is or could be in contact with food where it is prepared or sold. The only requirement is passing a safe food handling course of at least 7 hours; the certificate lasts 3 years and is valid nationwide. A barista prepares drinks and handles milk, so it is worth keeping it up to date and adding it to your profile. If you work outside Argentina, the name and requirements depend on your country or city.

How to get it and what the regulation says is in [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work).

## What to ask before accepting

Before confirming a date, make sure you are clear on:

- **The full hours**, including setting up and breaking down the station.
- **Who provides the machine, the grinder and the coffee**, and whether you need to bring anything of your own (jugs, tamper, cloths).
- **How many people are expected** and how many rushes there are in the day.
- **Clothing**: apron, colour of T-shirt or shirt.
- **The pay** and how it is paid.

If any of that isn't in the offer, ask before you accept. For everything else worth bringing, see [what to bring when working an event](/en/blog/what-to-bring-when-working-an-event).

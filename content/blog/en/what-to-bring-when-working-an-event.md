Half the problems of a shift have nothing to do with the work itself: the phone that dies in the middle of the night, the shoes that start hurting in hour three, the cold in the early morning after you took your jacket off for the photo. This guide is the list of what's worth having ready before you leave, the basics for any role and the extras that help depending on the position.

It isn't a list for bringing everything: it's so you can choose what goes in your bag depending on the event.

## First: reread the offer

Before you pack anything, check what you were asked for. On LABURO, the offer you accepted has the role, the event, when, where, the pay and the **conditions**, which is where whoever hires spells out anything specific: dress code, whether you need to bring something, whether there's somewhere to leave your things. If something is missing, ask before you leave, not at the door.

For the full day (what time to arrive, how check-in works), there's the guide to your [first day working at an event](/en/blog/first-day-working-at-an-event). For clothing, the guide to the [event staff dress code](/en/blog/event-staff-dress-code).

## The basics, for any role

- **ID.** You may be asked for it to get through the staff entrance or to collect your badge. Without it, sometimes you don't get in.
- **Charged phone, charger and power bank.** Your phone is your line to whoever coordinates, your way home and, in many positions, your work tool. A long shift with the phone on all the time eats the battery.
- **A warm layer.** Events change temperature: the air-conditioned room, loading outside, leaving in the early hours. Something light that fits in your bag is enough.
- **Water.** Ask whether there's water for staff; if you don't know, bring a bottle.
- **Food if the shift is long.** Not every event feeds the team. If it isn't clear, bring something that lasts hours without a fridge.
- **Spare comfortable shoes.** If you're asked to wear certain shoes for the look and you'll be standing for many hours, a second pair in your bag changes the end of your shift.
- **Plasters.** In case your shoes rub. They take up no space.
- **A pen and a bit of tape.** To write down a name, label a box, stick back a sign that came loose. Something always comes up.
- **A change of clothes or a spare T-shirt** if the job is physical or you might get stained.
- **Money or a card for the trip home**, with the route back planned before you leave. If the event finishes late, read [getting home after a late-night event](/en/blog/getting-home-after-a-late-night-event).

And a general rule: **bring as few valuables as possible**. Ask whether there's a place to leave your things during the shift and who looks after it.

## The uniform: whatever you're asked for, complete

If you're asked for all black, a branded uniform or a specific garment, bring it complete and in good condition. If the uniform is handed out on site, ask what to wear underneath and which shoes. If you have doubts about something (a visible tattoo, your hair, accessories), ask before you accept, not on the day.

## By role

### Bar and hospitality

- Whatever the person hiring you asks for the look: apron, shirt, hair tied back.
- Short nails and no rings if requested.
- If the position requires it, the relevant paperwork. The guide to the [food handler certificate](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work) explains what it is; the name and requirements depend on your country or city.
- If you have your own tools (corkscrew, bottle opener), ask whether you can bring them or whether the organisers provide them.

More about the trade in [event bartender jobs](/en/blog/event-bartender-jobs).

### Registration and reception

- A charged phone and a power bank are almost mandatory: many registration desks work with lists or scanners on the phone.
- A pen, a marker and tape for printed lists or last-minute changes.
- A warm layer if your post is at the entrance, which is usually the most exposed spot of the event.

What the job involves, in [what conference registration staff do](/en/blog/conference-registration-staff-role).

### Setup and teardown

- Closed, sturdy shoes, clothes that can get dirty and a warm layer for the early hours.
- Work gloves if the organisers don't provide them (ask).
- Water and food: setup usually starts early and finishes late.
- If the job requires protective equipment, who provides it is up to whoever hires you: ask beforehand.

What the trade requires, with rules and sources, is in [event setup and teardown: how to work safely](/en/blog/event-setup-and-teardown-work-safely).

### Promo staff, hostesses and hosts

- The look you were asked for, complete, and something for touch-ups during the shift.
- Spare shoes: this is the role where shoes for the look are most often required and where you spend the most hours standing.
- The brand brief read or saved on your phone, to go over what you have to say.

## A bag packed in advance

If you work often, it's worth keeping an event bag ready: power bank, charger, plasters, pen, tape, a spare T-shirt and a bottle. Before each event you add the specifics (uniform, food, a warm layer depending on the forecast) and off you go. Arriving with everything and without rushing is also part of what gets you called back, as explained in [how to get regular event work](/en/blog/how-to-get-regular-event-work).

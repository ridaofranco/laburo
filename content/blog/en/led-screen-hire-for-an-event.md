The screen is what the whole audience looks at at the same time. If it looks washed out in the sun, if the video stutters or if the speaker's presentation doesn't appear when they walk on stage, everyone sees the mistake. This guide is for production companies, businesses and organisers adding screens to a concert, a conference, a launch or a festival: when LED makes sense, what details to give the supplier, how it's rigged, where the power comes from and who operates it.

## LED or projector

Both work, for different things:

| Situation | Usually best |
|---|---|
| Outdoors in daytime or lots of ambient light | LED screen |
| Large stage, audience far away or at the sides | LED screen |
| Room with controlled light, audience close | Projector |
| Presentations and videos in a conference room | Projector, or LED if there's budget and strong light |
| Unusual formats (columns, strips, floors) | LED screen in modules |

An LED screen emits its own light and is built in modules, so it adapts to sizes and shapes. A projector needs relative darkness and a surface, and the image loses strength with ambient light. The supplier makes the final call with the venue details.

## What details to give the supplier

Without these, the quote is guesswork:

- **Indoors or outdoors.** And if outdoors, whether there's a roof or the screen is exposed.
- **Time and light.** An evening event isn't the same as one at midday with the sun in front.
- **Size of the venue.** Dimensions of the space and available height.
- **Audience distance.** How far away the front row is and how far the back row is. This determines the right resolution (what the supplier calls pixel pitch): the closer people watch, the finer it has to be.
- **Where the screen goes.** Flown from a structure, on a tower, standing on the floor, at the sides of the stage.
- **What will be shown.** Videos, presentations, live camera, sponsor graphics.
- **Setup and teardown dates.**

Ask for the quote to detail final size, resolution, rigging, power needed, technician hours included and what happens if it rains.

## Structure and anchoring

An LED screen is heavy and catches the wind. So:

- **The supplier decides how it's mounted**: flown (with a structure and rigging points), on towers or on a ground-supported base.
- **If it's flown from the venue**, the venue has to confirm how much weight its rigging points can take. Don't assume.
- **Outdoors**, ask how it's ballasted or anchored and at what wind speed it has to be lowered or switched off.
- **The area under and around it** stays clear during setup and teardown.

Requirements for structural calculations, permits and insurance depend on the venue and the municipality. Confirm them with the supplier, the venue manager and the local authority before closing.

## Power

The screen needs stable, dedicated power. Ask the supplier for the **consumption** and the **type of connection**, and pass it to the venue so it can confirm whether its installation can handle it without sharing a circuit with the kitchen or the lighting. Outdoors or in venues without enough mains power, the power comes from a generator: how to ask for one is in the guide on [event generator hire](/en/blog/event-generator-hire-how-to-size-it).

## Content and format

The most common mistake is sending the content on the day and finding it doesn't fit:

- **Ask the supplier for the final resolution and aspect ratio** of the assembled screen. A modular screen is rarely exactly 16:9.
- **Design for that size.** Videos and graphics are made at the screen's resolution, not a TV's.
- **File format**: ask which formats and codecs the video control plays.
- **Speaker presentations**: collect them beforehand, in a single folder, with names and order.
- **Content test**: run everything through the screen at rehearsal, in daylight if the event is in daylight.
- **Backup copy**: on a second computer or device, ready to use.

## Video operator and switcher

If the content changes during the event, someone has to run it. The **video/LED technician** or **switcher operator** sits at control: plays the videos, switches between the presentation and the live camera, brings up graphics on time and follows the event's running order with coordination. The supplier's technician makes sure the screen works; the operator handles what's shown and when. Sometimes they're the same person, but at shows, conferences with many speakers or broadcast events, it's best to have two.

What that role involves in detail is in the guide on [event video, LED and switcher technicians](/en/blog/event-video-led-and-switcher-technician). If it's a show, read [concert and live show staffing](/en/blog/concert-and-live-show-staffing).

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a projector for an outdoor daytime event.**
- **Asking for a size without giving the audience distance.**
- **Not asking how much weight** the venue can take for flying.
- **Plugging the screen** into a shared circuit.
- **Content made in 16:9** for a screen that isn't.
- **Nobody at control** when the speaker walks on.

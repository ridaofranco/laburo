Anyone who rents out tables, chairs, lounge sets or tents lives off a question that always arrives the same way: "Do you have this for that date, how much is it and can you deliver it?". This guide is about getting that question more often, with the details you need to answer fast. It doesn't talk about market prices: it covers what to show, how to state your stock and your terms, and how to mark your delivery areas.

## What the person hiring you is looking for

On the other side there is someone organising a wedding, a company party, a trade fair or a birthday at a country house. Before asking for a price they want to settle three things:

1. **Whether you have enough.** Do you have 200 matching chairs, or 80 of one model and 120 of another?
2. **Whether the style works.** A folding chair is not the same as a wooden one for a wedding.
3. **Whether you can get there.** Do you deliver to their area, and do you set it up yourself?

If your listing or your first reply answers those three things, you are already ahead of most.

## What to show: catalogue and photos

**A catalogue organised by family.** Tables (round, rectangular, high), chairs, lounge sets and poufs, bars, platforms, tents and gazebos, plus tableware and linen if you offer them. For each piece: size, material, colour and, for tables, how many people fit.

**Photos of your own jobs.** The whole piece, set up, at a real event. A dressed table with chairs around it says more than the same table alone in the warehouse. If a lounge set was used at an outdoor event, show it there. Avoid manufacturer images or other people's photos: clients recognise them and stop trusting you.

**The real condition.** If a batch of chairs shows signs of use, say so and show it. Someone organising a small event may prefer them if the price reflects it; someone organising a wedding will prefer to know beforehand.

## Your stock, stated honestly

The most expensive mistake in this trade is promising pieces that aren't there on the day. It happens when you count the whole warehouse instead of what is free for that date.

- **State the quantity available per date**, not the total. If you have 300 chairs but 100 are going to another event that Saturday, for that Saturday you have 200.
- **Set aside what is under repair.** Broken pieces don't get rented.
- **If the request exceeds your stock, say so in your first reply** and offer what you can cover. Mixing models without warning is worse than saying no.
- **One calendar, with statuses.** Inquiry, deposit paid, confirmed. If you note bookings in two places, sooner or later you will rent the same chair twice. There's more about managing your calendar in high season in [how event suppliers can prepare for the end-of-year season](/en/blog/event-suppliers-end-of-year-season).

## Your terms, clear before you quote

Almost every argument with furniture rental clients comes from something that wasn't written down. These are the ones worth settling before you send a price:

- **Delivery.** Is it included or charged separately? Does it depend on distance or volume? Are there floors to climb by stairs or lift?
- **Setup and teardown.** Do your people do it, or do you deliver to the door? If you set up, how long do you need and what time do you have to get in? Venues and country houses usually have loading times: ask for them.
- **Collection.** When do you pick up? If the event ends in the early hours, do you collect that night or the next day? Who looks after the pieces in the meantime?
- **Breakages, missing pieces and stains.** How are they charged? A list with the replacement value of each piece avoids arguing in the heat of the moment.
- **Deposit and balance.** How much the deposit is, what it secures (the date and the stock) and when the rest is paid.
- **Quantity changes.** Until when pieces can be added or removed at no cost.
- **Weather, for tents and outdoor jobs.** What happens if it rains or it's windy, and who decides whether to set up.

Write it once, in a short text, and paste it into every reply. Also put it in the details of each service on your listing: people will ask anyway, so it's better if they read it first.

## Your delivery areas

For furniture, the area matters as much as the price: a lounge set that travels 80 kilometres costs something different.

On LABURO, each service you add has its own **provinces** marked, and the directory filter looks at those, not at your company's province. Mark only where you really deliver. If within a province there are areas you don't reach or where delivery costs change, say it in the service details in plain words: "we deliver to the north and west; south with an extra delivery charge".

## How people find you on LABURO

People searching the LABURO supplier directory filter by **category**, by **province** or by **keyword**. Three categories fit your business: **Furniture**, **Tents** and **Tableware and linen**. The category filter has to match exactly, which is why it pays to add one service per thing you rent: one for furniture, one for tents, one for tableware.

In each service you can add a **starting price**, with its unit ("per piece", "per table and 10 chairs set", "per event"). It's optional and doesn't commit you, but it filters: the people who write to you are within your range. The listing makes clear that prices are a guide and that you send the real quote.

Use your client's words in the description: "Tiffany chairs", "white lounge set", "high tables", "Bedouin tent", "gazebo". The keyword search looks at your name, your short tagline, your description and the title and details of each service.

## When the request arrives

When someone requests a quote from your listing, it reaches you by email and you see it in your dashboard, under your inquiries. In your inquiry settings you can add the questions you won't quote without: event address, number of guests, indoor or outdoor, setup time. That way you don't have to go back and forth with messages.

Reply with your availability for that date, the price broken down (pieces, delivery, setup) and your terms pasted underneath.

If you're thinking of getting into events, or you're already in and want to move into a better position, this is the real picture: which positions exist, which are requested most often and what makes one worth more than another.

You won't find rates here. You will find the factors that move them, which is what helps you decide where to grow.

## The 11 families of positions

In LABURO trades are grouped like this, and this is the actual list used to find you:

1. **Bar and food service.** Bartender, bar back, waiter, runner, cook, kitchen assistant, dishwasher, barista, sommelier, maître d', food truck operator.
2. **Access and check-in.** Access control, check-in staff, scanner or ticketing operator, door security, cloakroom, wristbands and credentials.
3. **Production and logistics.** Production assistant, field producer, stage manager, show caller, setup and teardown, loading and unloading, storekeeper, props, logistics coordinator.
4. **Technical.** Sound, lighting, video and LED, switcher operator, stagehand, rigger, electrician, backline.
5. **Audiovisual.** Photographer, camera operator, drone operator, editor, streaming.
6. **Service and experience.** Promoter, hostess, receptionist, host, sampling, brand ambassador, entertainer, guest coordinator.
7. **Sales.** Salesperson, cashier, merchandise sales.
8. **Cleaning and maintenance.**
9. **Health and safety.** Doctor, nurse, emergency team, first aider.
10. **Transport.** Driver, shuttle driver, valet parking.
11. **Other profiles.** Translator or interpreter, volunteer coordinator, live community manager, announcing and booth.

## The ones requested most often

You don't need statistics to understand this: look at what **any** event with an audience has, whether it's a wedding, a festival or a conference.

It has a **door** people come in through. It has something to **drink or eat**. It has something to **build and take down**. And it has something to **clean**.

That's why bar and food service, access and check-in, setup and teardown, and cleaning are the families that come up most. They're the natural way into the trade: there's more volume, you learn on the job and they don't require a prior certification.

The downside is the same: **they're the ones the most people can cover**, and where the difference between "one more" and "someone reliable" matters most.

## The ones requested less, but worth something different

Rigger, electrician, sound, lighting or LED technician, drone operator, doctor, nurse, driver, translator. These positions appear at fewer events, but when they do **there aren't twenty candidates available on a Saturday night**.

There the calculation flips: fewer opportunities, more weight in each one. And an advantage you don't see at first: these are profiles that get called back, because finding a last-minute replacement is much harder.

## The six factors that make a position worth more

This is stable, and it works for any sector and any event:

1. **Responsibility for money or safety.** Till, access, health, rigging. If a mistake of yours costs money or puts someone at risk, the position carries more weight.
2. **Certification or licence.** An electrician, a driver with the right licence, a first aider. The paperwork isn't red tape: it's what allows you to be assigned.
3. **Your own tools.** Equipment, vehicle, instruments. It changes what you bring.
4. **Scarcity.** How many people in your area can do exactly that, on that day, in that time slot.
5. **Autonomy.** Being sent alone without needing anything explained. It's what gets noticed fastest and paid fastest.
6. **The time slot.** Late-night teardown, holidays, events outside your city. The shifts nobody likes are, almost always, the ones with the least competition.

All six can be worked on. None of them is luck.

## How to move up without inventing experience

The route that works is sideways and short, not a leap:

- From **bar back** to **bartender**.
- From **loading and unloading** to **stagehand** and from there to **backline** or **technical**.
- From **check-in staff** to **scanner operator** and from there to **access coordination**.
- From **runner** to **production assistant**.

You move up from the inside. That's why the shifts nobody wants (setup, teardown, late night) are the best real way in: there's more room, and it's where people see you work.

One detail that matters: when you set up your profile, mark **the positions you can really do**. Marking twenty trades doesn't make you appear in more useful searches, it makes you appear in the ones you don't fit. If you want to open up to a new position, add it and explain it in the detail of your experience ([here's how to write it](/en/blog/event-staff-profile-what-to-include)).

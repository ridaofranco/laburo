At an event with a stage, everyone looks at the same spot. If the riser wobbles, if the speaker can't be seen from the back or if the roof isn't up before the rain, it shows straight away. This guide is for production companies, businesses and organisers who need to hire a stage or a riser: what to ask for, what information to give and what to check before choosing a supplier.

## Riser or stage: what you really need

Not every event needs the same thing.

- **Low riser**: a presentation in a venue, a toast, a few words from the company owner, a DJ at a party. It is enough that people at the back tables can see who is up there.
- **Medium stage**: a band, a short fashion show, an awards night. You need room for gear, comfortable steps and, if it is outdoors, a roof to think about.
- **Large stage with a roof**: a concert or a festival. Here the structure is planned together with sound, lighting and screens, because they hang from it or rest on it.

Before asking for prices, write down in two lines what will happen on stage and how many people need to see it. That answer shapes everything else.

## What your request should include

To get a good quote (and to be able to compare), give every supplier the same information:

1. **Date, set-up time, event time and take-down time.**
2. **Address and type of venue**: hall, open-air site, square, rooftop, indoors or outdoors.
3. **Rough dimensions**: width, depth and height. If you don't know them, tell them what goes on stage and ask for a recommendation.
4. **What happens on top**: speakers, a band, a DJ, a fashion show, dancing. Two people at a lectern is not the same as a group jumping around.
5. **The ground it stands on**: grass, earth, concrete, wooden floor. It changes how it is levelled and what protection is needed.
6. **How to get there**: whether a truck can get in, whether there are stairs or a lift, how far the unloading point is.
7. **What else you need**: roof, steps, ramp, handrails, skirting (the fabric that hides the structure), carpet or black flooring.

More on putting together a request you can compare is in [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers).

## What to ask before you choose

With two or three quotes in hand, ask about what is usually not written down:

- **What the price includes**: transport, set-up, take-down, steps, handrails, skirting. Ask for it line by line.
- **How many people they send to build it and how long it takes.** You need it for the day's schedule.
- **Whether someone from their crew stays during the event** or leaves after set-up and comes back at the end.
- **What happens if it rains or gets windy**: whether the roof holds, whether they have side panels, when they would advise not using the stage.
- **What technical documentation they can provide** if the venue or the local authority asks for it.
- **How far ahead you need to confirm** and what happens if the date changes.

## Getting on and off the stage safely

What people forget most is not the stage itself: it is how you get on and off it.

- **Solid steps with a handrail**, placed where they don't clash with the audience or the cables.
- **A ramp** if someone in a wheelchair needs to go up or if heavy gear goes up on a trolley.
- **Handrails on the edges** that don't face the audience, especially on a high stage or with people moving around on it.
- **Marked edges** with tape or light so nobody misses a step in low light.
- **Tidy cables**, covered where people walk.

The specific requirements depend on the venue, the size of the structure and the event's permit. Confirm them with the venue manager and whoever handles health and safety before you sign off the plan.

## Outdoors: think about the weather

If the event is outside, the stage is the first thing the weather hits. Ask for a roof if there will be gear or people on stage for hours, ask about side panels for wind and agree with the supplier who decides to stop if conditions turn bad. For the rest of the team, see the [rain plan for outdoor event staff](/en/blog/rain-plan-for-outdoor-event-staff). If it is a festival, [outdoor festival staffing](/en/blog/outdoor-festival-staffing) also helps.

## Set-up is a team too

The supplier builds their structure, but more people work around the stage: whoever unloads gear, runs cables, sets out chairs and takes everything down at the end. It is worth knowing in advance what the supplier's crew does and what you have to cover. How that crew works and stays safe is in [event set-up and teardown](/en/blog/event-setup-and-teardown-work-safely).

If a screen goes on stage, add what the guide on [LED screen hire for an event](/en/blog/led-screen-hire-for-an-event) covers: the structure and the screen have to be planned together.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking for "a stage" with no dimensions** and without saying what goes on it.
- **Comparing prices that don't include the same things** (one with transport and set-up, another without).
- **Leaving the steps until last** so they end up in the middle of a walkway.
- **Not asking about the ground** and finding a slope or soft grass on the day.
- **Building with no margin** before the sound check.

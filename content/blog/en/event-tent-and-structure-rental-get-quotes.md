Nobody chooses a tent or a stage from a photo. Whoever organises an event wants to know whether their guests will fit, whether it can be set up at their site and what happens if it rains. If your listing and your quote answer those three things before they're asked, the request comes to you.

## Where to list yourself

On LABURO, **Tents** and **Stage and structures** are separate categories (there are also **Barriers**, **Rigging** and **Furniture**). If you rent tents and also build stages or platforms, add a service in each category: someone filtering the directory by Tents won't see a service listed as Stage. In each service, mark the provinces where you work at no extra charge; a service with no province doesn't show up in searches. If you travel to others, say so in the details. The fields one by one are in [the supplier profile that gets you quote requests](/en/blog/event-supplier-profile-get-quote-requests).

## Sizes and capacities, stated honestly

The temptation is to put the biggest number. It doesn't pay: an inflated size gets found out at the site visit or, worse, on setup day.

- **Sizes in metres, as you set them up.** Width, length and clear height. If they're modular, which combinations you have available.
- **Capacity by use.** The same tent doesn't hold the same number of people seated at tables, in theatre style or standing. If capacity depends on the layout, say it that way instead of giving a single number.
- **What's included.** Side walls, flooring, lighting, anchors or ballast. If any of those are charged separately, make it clear.
- **For stages and platforms:** surface size, height, whether there are steps or a ramp, whether it has a railing and whether it can take sound equipment or screens on top. If you don't know for sure, don't claim it: list it as something to confirm with the technician.

Your work is shown through your Instagram and your website, which go in your details. Photos of structures set up at real sites, with people inside, help organisers picture the size.

## The site visit

The same tent model doesn't go up the same way on grass, in a car park or on a terrace. That's why, for a large structure, the site visit is part of quoting properly:

- The type of ground and whether you can anchor or need to ballast.
- Whether the ground is level.
- Truck access and the distance to the setup point.
- Obstacles: trees, cables, slopes, a pool (if the event is at a country house, also read [event venues with a pool in the summer season](/en/blog/event-venue-with-pool-summer-season)).

Add those questions to your inquiry form (the section in your dashboard where you set how people contact you), so requests arrive with the basics: where it is, what surface, how many people and in what layout. If you can't do the visit, ask for photos and measurements and put in writing that the price is subject to confirming those conditions.

## Setup and teardown

A large tent doesn't go up in an hour, and it's what organisers think about least. In the quote, make clear:

- How far in advance you need to get into the site and how long setup takes.
- When you take it down: that same night, the next day or at another time you agree.
- Whether setup, teardown and transport are included in the price.
- Who on the client's side has to be there to receive you and sign off.

Setup is physical work with risks. If you take on extra hands in high season, share [event setup and teardown: how to work safely](/en/blog/event-setup-and-teardown-work-safely) with them.

## Wind and rain, as a written condition

An outdoor structure depends on the weather, and that isn't negotiated on the day: it goes in the quote.

- **Up to what conditions the structure is used**, according to the manufacturer's instructions or your technical judgement.
- **Who decides to suspend or evacuate** if the weather gets worse. That decision belongs to whoever builds the structure, not whoever organises the party, and it's worth having it written that way.
- **What happens with payment** if the event is called off or moved because of the weather.
- **What the tent covers and what it doesn't.** Whether side walls are optional, whether the flooring keeps water out, whether other suppliers' equipment needs covering.

At an outdoor event, the rain plan belongs to everyone: see how organisers think about it in [outdoor festival staffing](/en/blog/outdoor-festival-staffing).

"I need staff for an event" is a sentence nobody can quote. First you have to answer three things: **which roles**, **how many of each** and **in which time slot**. This guide is the order in which it pays to think about it.

## Start from the guest journey, not the list

The most common mistake is starting from a template of positions. What works is the opposite: **walk through the event in your head** from start to finish and write down every place where a person has to be.

The guest arrives. Who validates their entry? They come in. Who tells them where to go? They leave their coat. Who takes it? They go to the bar. Who serves them, who charges them, who restocks? They go to the restroom. Who keeps it clean? They leave. Who closes up?

Your real list comes out of that journey, and there are always two or three positions the template didn't have.

## The families of roles

These are the categories we work with at LABURO. Not all of them apply to every event: they're a checklist so you don't forget an entire family.

**Access and check-in.** Access control, registration staff, scanner or ticketing operator, door security, cloakroom, wristbands and badges.

**Bar and catering.** Bartender, barback, waiter, runner, cook, kitchen assistant, dishwasher, barista, sommelier, maître d', food truck operator.

**Production and logistics.** Production assistant, field producer, stage manager, show caller, setup and teardown, load-in and load-out, storeroom keeper, props, logistics coordinator.

**Technical.** Sound, lighting, video and LED, switcher operator, stagehand, rigger, electrician, backline.

**Audiovisual.** Photographer, camera operator, drone operator, editor, streaming.

**Guest experience.** Promoter, hostess, receptionist, host, sampling, brand ambassador, entertainer, guest coordinator.

**Sales.** Sales staff, cashier, merchandise sales.

**Cleaning and maintenance.** Cleaning staff, maintenance, restrooms.

**Health and safety.** Doctor, nurse, first aid team, lifeguard.

**Transport.** Driver, shuttle driver, valet parking.

**Other profiles.** Translator or interpreter, volunteer coordinator, live social media, announcer.

## How many: count by points and shifts, not by guests

There is no universal ratio of "one person every X guests". What does exist is a count that doesn't fail:

1. **Count the service points.** Every physical place that must be covered at the same time: each door, bar, till, restroom, access to a section.
2. **Count how many people each point needs** to run without a queue.
3. **Multiply by shifts.** If the event lasts longer than a reasonable human shift, those people multiply. Nobody performs the same in hour nine as in hour two.
4. **Add relief staff** for meals and breaks. If you don't count them, they come out of the same team and positions sit empty right at the peak.
5. **Add a buffer for no-shows.** Someone won't turn up. It always happens.

The detailed calculation, with the entrance math, is in [how many staff you need for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event).

## Think in moments, not as one block

Event staff is not one number: it's different curves that overlap.

- **Setup.** Load-in, build, technical crew, storeroom. Usually the day before or early morning.
- **Pre-opening and doors.** The access peak: check-in, scanning, door security, cloakroom, reception.
- **During.** Bars, catering, guest service, cleaning, health, technical booth.
- **Close and teardown.** Teardown, load-out, final cleaning.

Each moment has its own headcount and its own call times. A well-planned team doesn't have everyone on site from the morning.

## The four that are always missing

- **Teardown.** The setup gets budgeted and the exit is forgotten, and it also happens late at night, when fewer people are available and everything costs more.
- **Relief staff.** Meals and breaks exist. If they're not in the number, the team absorbs them.
- **Someone to coordinate the temporary staff.** With fifteen people who don't know each other, you need a clear point of contact on the day. Without one, every question goes to the producer.
- **Cleaning during the event**, not only afterwards.

## Hiring per event isn't "finding people fast"

Staff demand for an event is a peak: you need a lot of people for a few hours, and then you don't. That's why almost every production company works with a small, stable coordination core and adds temporary staff per event.

At a brand activation, for those hours the promoter is the brand. What they say and how they handle the unexpected depends on something put together beforehand: the brief. This guide is for agencies, brands and production companies that hire promo staff: what the brief needs, a template to copy and the mistakes that come up most often.

## A brief is not the campaign deck

The campaign deck is for the client and the agency. The promoter needs something else: what to do, when, with what, what to say and who to call.

A good brief fits on one or two pages and can be read on a phone. It has two parts:

- **The operational part** (role, date, venue, hours, pay, uniform), best placed in the offer the person accepts.
- **The brand part** (message, product, what to say and what not to say), on a separate sheet sent in advance and reviewed at the briefing before opening.

## What it needs

### Brand and key message

Who the brand is and what people should take away, in one or two sentences: the one the promoter will repeat a hundred times. If the product has a hard name, write it as it's pronounced.

### Goal and metric of the activation

What the activation is for (trial, sign-ups, sales, awareness) and how it's measured: samples handed out, registrations, sales. Whoever knows what's being measured knows where to put the effort.

### Hours and breaks

Call time (before opening), opening, closing and finish time. And the breaks, with times and who covers each spot meanwhile. If breaks aren't on paper, they end up being taken from the same team and a spot is left empty at the worst moment. How to build the rota is in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).

### Uniform and who provides it

What is worn (brand T-shirt, trouser colour, footwear, hair, accessories) and who provides it. If the brand provides it: where and when to pick it up, and whether it's returned at the end. If the promoter provides it, be precise: "black clothes" means something different to each person. More in [event staff dress code](/en/blog/event-staff-dress-code).

### Product and samples

Which product is shown or handed out, how many units there are, how they're restocked and what to do when they run out. If there's an alcohol tasting, the age rule has to be written down: in Argentina, Law 24,788 bans the sale of alcoholic drinks to under-18s nationwide. Also define how age is checked and what to do if someone can't prove it.

### What can and can't be said

The approved phrases, the product facts that can be given and, above all, what is not promised: prices, discounts, gifts, deadlines or benefits that aren't confirmed. This isn't a detail: under article 8 of Argentina's Consumer Protection Law 24,240, the specifics stated in advertising and other media are binding on whoever makes the offer. And add one line for what the promoter can't answer: "I'll check and get back to you", and who they check with.

If the activation collects data (name, email, phone), the brief has to say what text is read or shown to the person before it's entered. In Argentina, Law 25,326 on Personal Data Protection requires free, express and informed consent, and telling people beforehand what their data will be used for and who is responsible for it.

### Who to report to

Name and phone number of the on-site coordinator, and a second contact. Only one person gives instructions: if the promoter gets orders from the agency, the client and the store, they'll follow the last one.

### What to do in an incident

Three or four cases with their response: someone who feels unwell, a complaint, running out of product. It's almost always the same: don't try to solve alone what isn't your job, tell the coordinator and, if anyone is at risk, the venue's security or emergency staff.

### How the day closes

What gets counted (leftover samples, registrations, sales), who it's handed to, what happens with the uniform and materials, and when each person can leave.

## Brief template to copy

| Field | What to fill in |
|---|---|
| Brand | Name and one line on what it is |
| Key message | One or two sentences, as they are said out loud |
| Goal | Trial, sign-ups, sales or awareness |
| Metric | What is counted and how much is expected per hour or per day |
| Venue | Address, meeting point and entrance to use |
| Hours | Call time, opening, breaks (with time and cover), closing and finish |
| Uniform | What is worn, who provides it, where to pick it up and whether it's returned |
| Product and samples | What there is, how many units, how it's restocked, what happens if it runs out |
| Can say | Approved phrases and facts |
| Can't say | Unconfirmed prices, promotions or benefits, comparisons with other brands |
| Personal data | Text shown before registering someone |
| Age | Rule and how it's checked, if there's alcohol or another restriction |
| Reports to | Coordinator's name and phone, and a backup contact |
| Incidents | What to do and who to tell in each case |
| Closing | What is counted, who it's handed to, what is returned and when people leave |

## Common mistakes

- **Not setting a metric.** Without a number, each promoter decides alone what doing the job well means.
- **Forgetting breaks.** They happen anyway, but with no cover.
- **"Black clothes" as the only uniform instruction.**
- **Not writing down what isn't promised.** Saturday's improvised line is Monday's complaint.
- **Changing the promoter on every date.** For multi-date activations, the same person learns the product and the message stays the same.

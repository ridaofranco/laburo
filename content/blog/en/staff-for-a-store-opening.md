Opening a store or a showroom is a short event: two, three, four hours. That seems to make everything simpler, but it does the opposite. **There's no time to fix things as you go**: if the queue on the pavement builds up in the first half hour, that's already the picture of the event. This guide covers the roles, how to think about the peak and what to agree with the brand and the venue.

If what you're launching isn't a store but a product (with specialist press, rehearsal and a presentation), the set-up looks more like [product launch event staff](/en/blog/product-launch-event-staff).

## Before counting people: the space and the peak

An opening has two limits that override everything else.

- **How many people fit in the store at once.** A spacious showroom is not the same as a narrow shop with merchandise on display. Ask whoever manages the premises how many people can be inside at the same time and design the event around that number.
- **When people arrive.** At an invitation-only opening, a good share arrive in the first hour. If there's an announced moment (a ribbon cutting, a few words from the brand, a DJ), everyone arrives together for it.

With those two figures you know whether you'll have people waiting outside. And if you'll have people outside, the pavement is one more post.

## Reception and the guest list

- **A guest list closed** a few days beforehand, with names and, if needed, which group each person belongs to (clients, press, creators, friends of the brand).
- **One person per list point** and another to welcome and direct people. Whoever is looking up names can't also explain where the bar is.
- **What to do with someone who isn't on the list.** Reception doesn't decide: they check with a person from the brand chosen in advance. That goes in writing in the offer for the position.
- **Pace.** To work out how many list points you need, time how long each entry takes and do the maths for the peak, as explained in [how many staff do you need for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event).

If the opening is open to the public, there's no list, but you do need someone counting how many people go in and out to respect capacity.

## Promo staff and hosts inside

Inside, guest service staff get people to walk around the store instead of staying by the door:

- **Welcome and walk** each group to the first point of interest.
- **Know the product or what the store offers**, at least the basics, to answer without making things up.
- **Hand out whatever is being handed out** (a gift, a catalogue, a discount code) with a clear rule on who gets it and how many.

The brief should say what is said and what isn't. How to put it together is in [how to write a brand activation brief for promo staff](/en/blog/brand-activation-brief-for-promo-staff).

## Bar and catering

At an opening, the bar is usually where people bunch up. What to decide:

- **Where the bar goes.** Away from the door, so people move further in, and without blocking the merchandise.
- **What's served and for how long.** A short menu is served faster. If there are canapés, have waiters circulate with them rather than a table that gathers people in a corner.
- **Who provides it.** It can be a catering supplier who brings their own team, or bar staff and waiters you add yourself. Suppliers are listed in LABURO's services directory, and the staff come from the pool.

If the store sells food or drink and there will be tastings, see [how to build a sampling team](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-sampling-team).

## Press and creators

If media or creators are invited:

- **One person in charge** with the press list, who welcomes them separately from the general queue.
- **A time and a place** for them to talk to whoever represents the brand, with good light and without the bar in the background.
- **The rest of the staff don't give statements.** If someone asks, they refer them to the press contact.

If the brand has nobody for this, you can bring in a press and PR supplier from the services directory.

## Order on the pavement

It's the post most often forgotten and the one most visible from the street.

- **One person outside** keeping the queue in order, saying how long the wait is and explaining if people go in in groups.
- **The queue mustn't block** the entrance of neighbouring shops or the flow of passers-by. Talk it over beforehand with whoever manages the premises.
- **Coordination by radio or phone** with the door, to know when to let the next group in.

## Short hours, few reliefs

At a three-hour event there are almost no reliefs: the whole team works the whole time. So:

- **Call staff in early**, with time to receive the brief and walk the store before opening.
- **Put the peak inside everyone's hours.** Nobody starts late or leaves before the busiest hour.
- **Plan the close:** the departure of the last guests and tidying the store, which often has to be ready to open to the public the next day. For that, see [cleaning staff for an event](/en/blog/cleaning-staff-for-an-event).

## Common mistakes

- **Sizing for the total number of guests** rather than the first-hour peak.
- **A bar at the entrance:** people stay there and the queue grows outside.
- **Nobody on the pavement.** The queue forms on its own, in a mess.
- **Staff who don't know the store or the brand** because they arrived at opening time.

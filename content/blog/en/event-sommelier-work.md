At an event, wine isn't chosen table by table: someone chose it beforehand, it arrives in cases and it has to be poured properly into hundreds of glasses at the same moment. That is where the sommelier comes in. This guide is for anyone who knows wine and wants to work at events: which formats you'll come across, what changes compared with a restaurant, what the law says in Argentina and how to set up your profile so dates come your way.

## What a sommelier does at an event

The role changes quite a lot depending on the type of event. The formats you'll see most:

### Dinner with courses and wine pairing

Weddings, gala dinners, company dinners. Each course has its wine and your job is to make sure it arrives with the dish. That involves:

- **Temperature.** Knowing when to take the whites out of the cold and when to open the reds.
- **Glasses.** Which glass goes with each wine and how many are needed per guest.
- **Quantity.** How many bottles to open per course so you don't run short or leave too many open bottles.
- **Waiters.** Often you don't pour every glass yourself: you tell the waiters how to pour, how much and from which side. You work alongside the maître d', who sets the timing on the floor (we cover that in [event maître d' and floor manager](/en/blog/event-maitre-d-and-floor-manager)).

### Tasting or winery booth

At a trade show, a launch or an activation, the sommelier presents the wines to the public. Here talking matters more than pouring: explaining each label in a few words, answering questions and keeping the table tidy even with a queue. How a team like this is put together, from the hiring side, is in [how to build a sampling team](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-sampling-team).

### Wine bar

Some events add a bar for wine or sparkling wine only. It is similar to a cocktail bar: rushes, restocking, order. The difference is that people will ask you what you are pouring.

## What changes compared with a restaurant

- **The selection isn't yours.** A winery, a brand or the organiser decides it. Your job is to know it by heart and serve it well.
- **Everything goes out at once.** You don't recommend table by table: the whole room drinks the same wine with the same course.
- **Logistics weigh as much as your palate.** Ice, chilling, clean glasses, opening on time, clearing empty bottles.
- **The venue is new.** Ask beforehand where the wine is stored, whether there is refrigeration and who restocks glasses.
- **The shift is the event's.** It includes arriving early to prepare and staying for close if that was agreed.

## What's expected

- **Knowing the selection before the event.** If you are sent the list, study it: grape, region, how each label is presented.
- **Speaking simply.** Event guests don't always know about wine. Two clear sentences are worth more than a technical sheet.
- **A well-kept appearance** and the clothing set by whoever hires you. Confirm it beforehand.
- **Coordination with the floor and the bar.** The maître d' sets the timing and the bar may share glasses and refrigeration with you.
- **Good judgement with alcohol.** Pouring tasting measures when the format is a tasting, and stopping when needed.

If your work involves handling food (cheeses, bread, pairing bites), it is worth having an up-to-date food handler certificate. What it is and how to get it in Argentina is in [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work); elsewhere, the name and requirements depend on your country or city.

## What the law says

In Argentina, Law 24,788 against Alcoholism has two articles that affect you directly:

- **Article 1.** Bans, throughout the national territory, the sale of any kind of alcoholic drink to anyone under eighteen. It doesn't change because the tasting is free or because the event is private. If you are unsure about someone's age, you don't serve them and you tell your supervisor.
- **Article 7.** Bans contests, tournaments or events of any kind that require drinking alcohol in a way that distorts tasting. A tasting is about trying, not competing over who drinks the most. If someone suggests something like that, say no and speak up.

The same law bans drinking alcohol inside stadiums during mass events, except in places and at times authorised by the authorities (article 4). If you are called for an event in a stadium, ask what is permitted.

## What to ask before accepting

- **What the format is**: dinner with pairing, tasting, booth or bar.
- **Whether you pour yourself or guide the waiters.**
- **What time you arrive** and whether there is refrigeration and glassware at the venue.
- **What you need to bring**: corkscrew, apron, clothing.
- **The pay** and how it is paid.

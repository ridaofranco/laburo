Yes, 16 and 17 year olds can work at events in Argentina. The catch is the hours: the law doesn't allow anyone under 18 to work between 8 p.m. and 6 a.m., and that is exactly when most events happen. On top of that, the working day can't go over 6 hours, there is no overtime, and they need parental consent, a school certificate and a medical certificate. So a party, a wedding or a concert is out, and what's left is daytime work, like an afternoon brand activation, a trade fair or a morning sports event.

If you are an agency or brand from abroad bringing an event to Argentina, this applies to you too: local labour law covers the people hired for your event, wherever your company is based.

## The minimum age

[Law 26,390](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/140000-144999/141792/norma.htm) raised the minimum age for employment to 16 and bans work by anyone under 16 in any form, so there is no exception for "just one night". From 16 to 17 inclusive, the law calls it adolescent work and allows it with restrictions.

The [Argentine government's FAQ on child and adolescent work](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/trabajo/orientacion/trabajo-infantil) says the same: adolescent work is allowed with special protections, and what is banned outright is dangerous, arduous or unhealthy work.

## Night hours

For events, this is the one that matters most. The law bans night work for under-18s, and the Employment Contract Law counts 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. the next day as night. The government FAQ puts it plainly: working hours must be in the afternoon and no night-time activity is allowed.

Put it against real dates. An end-of-year party starting at 9 p.m. is out completely, and so is a wedding with an 8 p.m. reception. A concert with doors at 7 p.m. leaves an hour at best before the cut-off, which isn't worth the trip. A morning or afternoon event that ends before 8 p.m., breakdown included, does work.

## Up to 6 hours, no overtime

Law 26,390 caps the working day at 6 hours a day and 36 a week for 16 and 17 year olds. The government adds that overtime isn't allowed and that a longer day has to be requested from the labour authority, which decides whether it is appropriate.

At events, it's usually the end that stretches. The client asks for another hour, breakdown runs late, the return shuttle leaves later. With a 17 year old on the team, you can't do that. Give them a short shift with a fixed finish time and have someone make sure they leave at that time, even if everyone else stays.

## The paperwork

According to the government FAQ, an adolescent needs consent from their parents, guardians or carers. It is presumed if they live independently, and the provincial or city labour authority can endorse it. They also need a school attendance certificate, which the employer must request when hiring (work can't interfere with school), a medical certificate confirming they are fit for the task, and formal registration like any other worker.

The employer asks for and keeps these documents, not the young person doing the job. For the rest of the paperwork for temporary staff, see [paperwork for hiring temporary event staff](/en/blog/paperwork-for-hiring-temporary-event-staff).

## Which roles make sense

Even during the day, not every role is suitable. Dangerous tasks are out: rigging at height, heavy lifting, equipment that can hurt someone. And bar work makes no sense if alcohol is being served.

What fits best at a daytime event is registration and reception, handing out material at an activation, helping at a morning fair or conference, or guiding the public at a sports event. Always with an adult in charge and the finish time written into the offer.

## If you are building the team

Ask for date of birth when you call people in, don't assume it. If someone is under 18, check first whether the event has a daytime slot that suits them. If it doesn't, tell them no and explain why. And if there's already an adolescent on the team, write down their finish time so nobody keeps them back at the end. If a local production partner is putting the crew together for you, ask them how they check ages before the list is closed. For students who want to fit events around school, there's [event jobs for students](/en/blog/event-jobs-for-students).

Cleaning is the position nobody looks at when it works and everybody notices when it fails: a toilet with no paper at toast time, a sticky bar, a split bag in the corridor. This guide is for people who hire and people who do this work: what happens before, during and after, what the critical points are and how to calculate the team without inventing a rule.

## Before the event

Cleaning starts at setup, not when guests come in.

- **Walk the venue with the floor plan.** Mark toilets, bars, food area, entrance, dressing rooms, back of house and waste points. That defines the rounds.
- **Ask the venue what it includes.** Some venues hand over the space clean and ask for it back the same way; others have their own staff for some areas. Get it in writing who does what.
- **Agree with the caterer who cleans what.** Tableware, food tables, kitchen and food waste are usually theirs, but not always. Whatever isn't agreed falls through the gap.
- **Set up the supplies store:** paper, soap, bags of every size, gloves, cloths, squeegees, a trolley. Near the toilets and with someone responsible for restocking.
- **A detailed clean after setup.** The build leaves dust, tape, cardboard and cable ties. That pass happens once setup is finished and before doors open.

## During the event

The work is based on **rounds**: each person has an area and a circuit they repeat at set intervals.

- **Toilets.** The most critical point. Restock, dry floors, empty bins and report anything broken. They get dirty at the peaks: at opening, during breaks and at the end of the show.
- **Food and bars.** Clear abandoned glasses and plates, clean spills quickly (they cause slips) and empty the area's bins before they fill up.
- **Corridors and entrance.** The first thing people see when they arrive and the last when they leave.
- **Back of house and dressing rooms.** Fewer guests, but if it's neglected, the working team suffers.

Two rules that keep everything in order:

1. **Bags are changed before they're full,** not when they overflow.
2. **A spill is signposted immediately** and cleaned as soon as possible. It's a safety issue, not a cosmetic one.

## Waste and sorting

Waste is handled with **fixed, signposted points**, not loose bins.

- **Place them where rubbish is generated:** bars, food, entrance and back of house.
- **One container per type,** with a clear sign, if the event sorts waste.
- **A collection area** out of public view, where bags are gathered until pickup.
- **Coordinate pickup** with the venue or whoever is responsible: time, exit route and who receives it.

What is sorted and how it's handed over depends on the municipality and the venue. **Check before setup** with the venue and your city's rules. We don't give waste rules in this guide because they depend on each place.

## After the event

- **Remove waste** from every point to the collection area.
- **Go over toilets, floors and surfaces** until the venue is left as it asked.
- **Take stock of supplies** left over and anything broken.
- **Final walk-through with the venue,** if requested, before the team leaves.

## How to calculate how many people you need

There's no number of staff per so many attendees that works for every event. It depends on the venue, the number of toilets and bars, the distances and what the caterer does. What you can do is **measure rounds** and do the maths.

1. **List the points to maintain** (each toilet block, each bar, each area).
2. **Decide how often someone has to pass** each point at the peaks.
3. **Measure how long it takes one person** to do the full round of an area, walking and dealing with the usual, at a trial run or a previous event.
4. **Divide:** if the round takes longer than the frequency you want, that area needs more than one person.

**Example, with assumed numbers just to see the mechanics:** if at the peak you want someone to pass the toilets every 15 minutes and the round of that area takes 30, you need 2 people there. Those 15 and those 30 are made up: you measure your own.

Add one person to restock supplies and act as the point of contact, and think about shifts: setup, event and teardown aren't always the same people. The general logic is in [how many staff for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event) and relief planning in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).

## If you work in event cleaning

- **Arrive early** and walk your area with the coordinator.
- **Ask where the supplies are** and who to tell if something breaks.
- **Work discreetly:** cleaning without interrupting people is part of the job.
- **Confirm the closing time.** Teardown and the final clean are usually the last thing, and that should be in the offer.

If it's your first event, [first day working at an event](/en/blog/first-day-working-at-an-event) covers the basics.

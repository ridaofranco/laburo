If you work at events in Argentina, sooner or later a production company will tell you: "send me the invoice". This guide explains what the monotributo is, when you'll be asked for it, how to invoice a shift and what to look at when you register. It doesn't replace an accountant: for your specific case (if you have another job, if you're employed, which province you live in), check with one.

## What the monotributo is

ARCA, Argentina's federal tax agency, defines it as a **simplified regime for small taxpayers** that combines tax and social security payments into a single monthly payment. Instead of settling each tax separately, you pay a fixed amount according to your category.

To join, the route ARCA sets out has these steps:

1. **Registration:** CUIT (tax ID), tax password, electronic tax address and registration of your activities.
2. **Monotributo sign-up:** registering for the tax to start your activity.
3. **After sign-up:** enabling a point of sale in the system you choose for invoicing, paying the monthly fee and recategorising.

ARCA also has a **social monotributo** with its own conditions. If you think it applies to you, the information is in the same section of the site.

## When you'll be asked for it

Not every production company hires the same way. Some ask for an invoice for each shift, others per event and others hire you differently. What matters is **knowing before you accept**:

- **Ask whether they want an invoice** and in whose name.
- **Ask when they pay** against that invoice.
- **If you don't invoice yet**, say so from the start. It's better to sort it out beforehand than to have your money held up after the event.

[What event work is like and how you get paid](/en/blog/what-event-work-is-like-and-pay) covers the full route from offer to payment.

## How to invoice a shift

Once you're registered and your point of sale is enabled, invoicing an event is always the same:

1. **Ask the production company for its tax details:** company name, CUIT and VAT status. Without them you can't issue the receipt properly.
2. **Ask how they want it:** one invoice per shift or a single one for the whole event. Some production companies ask for one or the other so they can account for it internally.
3. **Write a clear detail:** role, event name, date and hours worked. For example: "Check-in service, event X, 30/10, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.". That way nobody has to ask you what the invoice is for.
4. **Invoice the amount agreed in the offer.** If there were extra hours or a change in pay, make sure it's confirmed in writing before you invoice it.
5. **Send it as soon as the work is done,** together with anything else they asked for (for example, your clocked hours).

**A detail that saves trouble:** keep the accepted offer and the record of your hours. If something doesn't match, those two documents are your backup.

## Which activity to choose

When you register you have to declare the activity you do. At events, almost all staff work (check-in, promo staff, waiters, setup, runner, stage) is **provision of services**, and in ARCA's table services and sale of goods have different amounts.

What's worth knowing, in general:

- **Choose the activity that describes what you really do,** not the one that sounds cheaper.
- **If you do several things** (for example, you work as staff and also sell something of your own), the choice changes. In that case, check with an accountant.
- **If you have another job as an employee,** the monotributo adds to what you already have and the two need to be looked at together.

We don't give you the exact activity code here because it depends on your case: an accountant solves it in a short consultation, and the activity search tool is inside ARCA's registration process.

## Categories and amounts

The category is set by your annual gross income, with ceilings set by ARCA. According to **ARCA's table with values applying from 1 August 2026** (amounts in Argentine pesos):

- **Category A:** annual gross income of up to 12,009,410.45 pesos.
- **Category A, services:** monthly total of 49,527.18 pesos.
- **Category K:** annual gross income of up to 126,610,838.75 pesos.

These values are updated. **Before registering or recategorising, check the current table** on ARCA's categories page (it's in Sources, at the end).

Two dates set by ARCA:

- **The monthly fee** is paid by the 20th of each month (the first one is due on the last day of the month you registered).
- **Recategorisation is every six months,** in February and August. If a summer with lots of events raised your income, your category may change.

## Common mistakes

- **Finding out after the event** that they wanted an invoice.
- **Invoicing with the wrong production company details.** Always ask for them in writing.
- **Not paying the fee in months without events.** The monotributo is paid every month, whether you work or not.
- **Not recategorising** when your income went up.
- **Taking this guide as advice.** It's a general guide; an accountant looks at your case.

At an outdoor event, the toilets are among the first things people judge and among the last things that get planned. A twenty-minute queue, a unit with no paper at eleven at night or a toilet closed because the truck couldn't get in to empty it can ruin a good production. This guide is for production companies, businesses and organisers who are going to hire portable toilets: how to work out the number, where to place them, how to keep them going during the event and what to ask the supplier before signing.

## First: what your local authority requires

In Argentina there is no national figure of toilets per person. Each municipality sets it for events in its area, and very often proof of the hire is a requirement for the permit. Two official examples:

- [Ordinance 2,140/2021 of Malargüe (Mendoza province)](https://hcd.malargue.gov.ar/hcdpdf/On%202140%202021%20banos%20quimicos%20en%20eventos%20publicos.pdf) requires portable toilets at mass events when the venue's own facilities aren't enough, leaves the number and location to the municipal authority according to the audience, asks for proof of hire to authorise the event and requires units adapted for people with disabilities.
- [Decree 70/2018 of Neuquén province](https://e-legis-ar.msal.gov.ar/htdocs/legisalud/migration/pdf/29904.pdf) asks municipalities to require toilets suited to the event, free drinking water in proportion to attendance and cleaning of the site at the end.

Before asking for a quote, ask the local authority where the event takes place what number it requires and whether anything has to be submitted. If you're outside Argentina, the name and requirements depend on your country or city. That is your baseline: what follows helps you adjust upwards for your event.

## How to work out the number

The supplier will ask you for this information. Have it ready before you call:

1. **People at the peak.** Not the total tickets sold, but how many people are there at the same time at the busiest moment.
2. **Hours of event.** A three-hour afternoon is nothing like a twelve-hour festival. More hours mean more visits per person and more cleaning services in between.
3. **Whether there's alcohol.** With an open bar people go to the toilet more often. It's one of the factors that moves the number most.
4. **The gender split.** Women's queues are usually the longest. If you can, separate units by area and add urinals for men, which take up less space and ease the pressure.
5. **Accessible units.** At least one, even if your local authority doesn't set a number. More if the event is large or the site is spread out.
6. **Staff and performers.** Crew and dressing rooms need their own toilets, separate from the public, so they don't queue with the guests.

With that data the supplier proposes a number. Compare it with what the authority requires and go with the higher one. If in doubt, adding a unit is cheaper than handling a queue that gets out of control.

## Where to place them

- **Spread out, not in a single block**, if the site is large. People don't walk to the far end: they use what's close by, or somewhere they shouldn't.
- **Away from the food area and the bars**, because of the smell, but not so far that people have to cross the crowd.
- **On firm, level ground.** On grass, after rain, the toilet area is the first to turn to mud. If rain is possible, read the [rain plan for outdoor event staff](/en/blog/rain-plan-for-outdoor-event-staff).
- **With lighting.** If the event runs into the night, the toilet area has to be lit.
- **With access for the truck.** The one that delivers, the one that services during the event and the one that collects. If the truck can't get there, the toilet doesn't get emptied.
- **The accessible unit** near a path without steps or long grass, and signposted.

## Hand basins, paper and soap

The portable toilet alone isn't enough. Ask separately for:

- **Hand basins or sinks** with water and soap near the toilets. Some units come with a built-in basin and others don't: ask.
- **Hand sanitiser** as an extra, not a replacement.
- **Paper and soap for restocking** for the whole event, and who restocks them.
- **Bins** by the toilet exits.

## Cleaning and restocking during the event

This is where the difference shows most. There are two levels:

- **The supplier's service.** The truck that empties, cleans and restocks. Agree how many services the price includes and at what times, especially for long events or events with alcohol. Make sure they don't coincide with the busiest moment.
- **Your own restroom staff.** One or more people who patrol the area between services: they restock paper, do quick cleaning, organise the queue, close a unit if it breaks and let the coordinators know. On LABURO this is part of the cleaning and maintenance role.

How many cleaning staff to have and how to split them into shifts is in [cleaning staff for an event](/en/blog/cleaning-staff-for-an-event). For large outdoor events, the general logic is in [outdoor festival staffing](/en/blog/outdoor-festival-staffing).

## What to ask the supplier

| Topic | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Number and type | How many standard units, accessible units and urinals they propose for your audience and your hours |
| Hand basins | Whether the units include them or they're hired separately, and with how much water |
| Services during the event | How many cleanings the price includes, at what time and how much an extra one costs |
| Delivery and collection | Day and time slot, in writing, and how long before the event they arrive |
| Truck access | What size truck they use and where it needs to get in |
| Supplies | Whether paper and soap are included, and how much they leave for restocking |
| Emergencies | A phone number that answers during the event if a unit breaks or fills up |
| Paperwork | Proof of hire to submit to the local authority, if required |

Ask for everything in writing and compare quotes on the same basis: same number of units and same services. How to put that request together is in [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers).

## Common mistakes

- **Counting by tickets sold** instead of people at the peak.
- **Forgetting alcohol** in the calculation.
- **A single block of toilets** on a large site.
- **No service in between** at a long event.
- **No truck access** on collection or service day.
- **No accessible unit**, or one that is accessible on paper but placed on grass or with a step.
- **Nobody in charge of the toilets** between one service and the next.

You have a Monday to Friday job and want to earn extra money, gain experience or simply do something different. Events lend themselves to that: many fall on Friday night, Saturday or Sunday. The challenge isn't getting dates but organising yourself so the event doesn't eat into your week or your rest. This guide is for anyone who wants to work at events at weekends without giving up their other job: which dates to take, how to look after yourself and how to mark your availability on LABURO.

## Why weekends work

Weddings, quinceañera parties, birthdays, children's events, concerts and many night-time parties happen at the weekend. They need people behind the bar, on the floor, on access, in the cloakroom, in entertainment and on setup. If you can do those days, you are exactly what many events are looking for.

What doesn't help is promising more than you can deliver. A conference or a trade show is usually on weekdays and runs all day; if you can't miss work, those aren't your dates.

## Before accepting: do the maths on the hours

An offer states the event's hours, but your day is longer. Add up:

- **The journey there**, with a margin in case the venue is far away or unfamiliar.
- **Arriving early to set up**, if the role requires it.
- **The whole event**, including teardown if it is part of your job.
- **The journey home.** If it ends in the early hours, how you'll get back and what time you'll arrive. [Getting home after a late-night event](/en/blog/getting-home-after-a-late-night-event) has ideas for sorting it out.

With that in front of you, ask yourself: will I get to work on Monday in good shape? If the answer is "sort of", think twice about that date.

## How to organise yourself

**Pick a type of date and stick to it.** For example: Saturday nights yes, Sundays only if they finish early. It keeps you organised and tells the person building the team what they can count on.

**Don't string long events together.** Friday night, a double shift on Saturday and teardown on Sunday is a weekend you pay for on Monday. Every so often, leave a day free.

**Look after Friday.** If you leave work and go straight to the event, bring your clothes, food and whatever you need from the morning. What's worth having to hand is in [what to bring when working an event](/en/blog/what-to-bring-when-working-an-event).

**Check the real length.** Long events have shifts, relief and breaks, or they should. How they are organised is in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).

**Keep the two worlds apart.** Don't use your other job's hours to sort out event matters, except for anything urgent. And if your employer has any rules about other activities, check them first.

**Keep track of what you earn and when.** With two incomes it is easy to lose count. A note on your phone with date, event, hours and amount is enough.

## If you're asked for an invoice

It depends on how you are hired at each event. If you are asked for an invoice, you'll need to be registered to issue one; the name and requirements depend on your country. If you also have a salaried job, it has to be looked at together with what you already have: for your specific case, check with an accountant. If you work in Argentina, what the monotributo is, when you are asked for it and how to invoice a day's work is explained in [monotributo for event workers in Argentina](/en/blog/monotributo-for-event-workers-argentina).

## How to mark your availability on LABURO

Your profile has fields designed for this. Use them all:

1. **"Available at weekends".** It is a box in the experience and availability section. Tick it: it is what says you can do Saturday and Sunday.
2. **"How much notice do you need to take a job?".** Choose between available immediately, with 24 to 48 hours' notice, with a week's notice or to be arranged. If your week is fixed, "with a week's notice" is usually the most honest answer.
3. **The free-text experience field.** Spell out what the boxes don't say: "I work Monday to Friday until 6 pm, I can do Fridays from 8 pm, Saturdays and Sundays." That detail avoids offers you can't take.
4. **"Available to travel" and "own transport".** Tick them only if they are true. Travelling to another city for a weekend may not fit with Monday.
5. **Your roles and your area.** Mark the roles you can do: the more you mark, the more offers can reach you.

Once you are registered, you can change all this from your profile whenever you like. If your hours change (you go on holiday, move to part-time, switch shifts), update it: that is what makes the offers you receive useful. More profile ideas in [what to include in your event staff profile](/en/blog/event-staff-profile-what-to-include).

## When an offer arrives

Each offer comes with the role, dates, hours and pay. You accept or decline it from your phone. Declining a date you can't do in good time is much better than accepting it and pulling out later. If it happens anyway, [how to cancel an event shift](/en/blog/how-to-cancel-an-event-shift) explains how to let people know properly.

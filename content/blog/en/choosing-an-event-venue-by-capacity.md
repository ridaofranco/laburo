"Will we fit?" is the first question when you're looking for a venue, and it's almost always answered badly. People look at a number on a listing, compare it with the guest list, and that's it. The problem is that the number doesn't say which layout it's for, or how much room is left for everything that isn't people: the bar, the stage, the catering table, the sound desk.

This guide goes step by step: how to think about capacity by layout, what to ask the venue before booking, and how to search by number of guests on LABURO.

## First, the layout

The same space holds very different numbers of people depending on how you arrange them. The three most common layouts:

- **Standing (cocktail or party).** The one that holds the most people. It works for launches, after-work drinks or parties, where people move around and nobody has an assigned place.
- **Banquet (round or long tables).** Tables and chairs take up a lot of room, and you also have to leave aisles for service. Quite a lot fewer people fit than standing.
- **Theatre (rows facing a stage).** For talks, conferences or presentations. More people fit than at a banquet, but you have to subtract the stage, the screens and the aisles.

That's why "300 people fit" isn't enough. The right question is: **how many fit in my layout, with my setup?** A good venue answers that for each layout, and often has floor plans or photos of each setup.

We won't give you a square-metres-per-person formula: it depends on the layout, the furniture, what the stage and bars take up, and what that venue is permitted for. The number that counts is the one the venue gives you, and the permit is what rules.

## What takes up space and isn't people

Before you ask, list everything that goes inside apart from the guests:

- Stage or platform, and the sound booth or desk.
- Bars and catering stations.
- Dance floor, if there is one.
- Registration tables at the entrance.
- Space for staff: storage, a dressing room, somewhere to leave their things.

With that list, the venue can tell you whether your event really fits. Without it, they'll give you the number on their listing.

## What to ask the venue before booking

These questions avoid the most common surprises:

1. **What is the permitted capacity?** And for which layout. It's a fact about the place, not an estimate, and your number of guests has to fit within it.
2. **Can I bring outside catering and suppliers?** Some venues only work with their own or charge extra if you bring yours. It changes the whole budget.
3. **What time can I set up and when do I have to clear out?** If the venue has another event before or after, setup time shrinks. And a short setup gets paid for in more people working at the same time.
4. **Is there parking? Where is loading and unloading?** For guests, and above all for suppliers arriving with equipment.
5. **Is it accessible?** Step-free entrance or a ramp, a lift if it's on an upper floor, and adapted toilets.
6. **What does the hire include?** Furniture, crockery, sound, air conditioning, equipped kitchen, generator. Whatever isn't included, you have to hire separately.
7. **How much deposit do I need to leave and until when can I cancel?**

## Bigger isn't always better

The temptation is to book a bigger venue "just in case". But a place that's too big looks empty, costs more and forces you to spend on decoration to fill it. The idea is that your guests fit comfortably in your layout, with room for what goes inside, not that half the venue is left over.

Also think about the people who are coming: how far it is from where most of them live and how they'll get home at the time the event ends.

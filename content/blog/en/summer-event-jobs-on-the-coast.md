Summer moves event work to wherever people are on holiday: brand activations, parties, concerts, beach clubs, weddings. In Argentina that means the Atlantic coast resorts during the December to February season. The dates are there, but teams are put together earlier, and whoever turns up in January asking usually finds the places taken. This guide is so you reach the season with everything sorted: profile, availability, paperwork and where you'll sleep.

You won't find here how many people get hired or how much is paid on the coast: there is no serious source that says so and we are not going to make it up. What you can control is what depends on you.

## First, decide what kind of season you want

Moving to the coast for two months is not the same as taking one-off dates and travelling. Before you fill anything in, be clear on:

- **Whether you'll stay or go back and forth.** It changes which offers work for you and which don't.
- **From when to when.** A specific arrival date and return date are worth more than "all summer".
- **Which roles you can do.** Promo staff, hospitality, setup, access: each asks for different things. If you have no experience, start with entry-level roles, as explained in [event jobs with no experience](/en/blog/event-jobs-with-no-experience).

## Your profile, ready before December

Your profile is what the person building the team looks at before writing to you. On LABURO you can edit it later. For the season, what counts most:

- **Your real province and city**, and under **where you want to work**, the areas that really suit you.
- **The roles you can do**, not all of them. A profile that ticks everything says little.
- **Available at weekends** and **available to travel**, if that's the case. In summer, a good share of events fall at weekends.
- **Own transport**, if you have it: in areas where events are far from the town centre, it is something whoever hires looks at.
- **How much notice you need to take a job.** If you'll be on the coast and can take something at short notice, say so.
- **Your experience described with dates and roles**, and your CV uploaded.

What to write and what not to, with examples, is in [what to include in your event staff profile](/en/blog/event-staff-profile-what-to-include). And to keep getting called after the first date, see [how to get regular event work](/en/blog/how-to-get-regular-event-work).

## Paperwork, before you leave

Don't leave for the coast what you can sort out from home:

- **A valid ID document**, and keep it to hand.
- **Your status for invoicing or for being hired**, depending on how you work. The name and requirements depend on your country; if you don't know which applies to you, find out before the first offer, not after.
- **A bank account in your name** to get paid into.
- **An up-to-date CV** as a PDF, so you can send it quickly.

## If you'll work in hospitality: the food handler certificate

If you'll be working in a kitchen, behind a bar or serving food or drink, you'll be asked for a **food handler certificate**. In Argentina it is set out in **article 21 of the Argentine Food Code**. Three facts from ANMAT (the national food regulator) that matter for the season:

- **It lasts three years** from issue.
- **It is valid nationwide**, so the one you got in your city works on the coast.
- **The course lasts at least 7 hours**, and each jurisdiction can extend it.

If you don't have it, get it before you leave: the course and exam take time and the final assessment is in person. How to get it, step by step, is in [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work). The LABURO profile doesn't have a dedicated box for the certificate: mention it in your experience and on your CV. If you work outside Argentina, the name and requirements depend on your country or city.

## Where you'll sleep

It is the question that decides the most money and the one most often left until the last minute.

- **Don't assume accommodation comes with the job.** Some offers include it and many don't. Ask before accepting.
- **Get it in writing.** If accommodation is included, it should appear in the offer alongside the pay, the hours and the dates.
- **Do the full maths:** what you earn minus accommodation, food and travel. What matters is what you have left.
- **Think about how you get back at night.** Many summer events finish late; knowing how you'll get to where you're sleeping is part of deciding whether to accept a date.

## During the season

- **Reply quickly.** In season, dates get filled within hours.
- **If you can't make it, give notice in good time.** Whoever gives notice stays on the list; whoever doesn't turn up without warning doesn't.
- **Look after yourself in the sun and heat** at outdoor events: water, sunscreen and something on your head when the role allows it.
- **Update your profile** if your dates or your area change.

If you'll be working at brand activations, which are among the most common jobs in summer, see [shopping mall activation staff](/en/blog/shopping-mall-activation-staff) to understand how those teams work.

People looking for a venue don't visit twenty places: they look at a list, rule things out fast and inquire at two or three. What decides whether your venue gets past that first filter is how you described it. This guide is for owners and managers of event venues, country houses and event spaces who want the right people to find them, and want the inquiries they get to be for events that really fit.

## Capacity, stated honestly

It's the first thing you'll be asked and the first thing people search by. On LABURO capacity is entered as two numbers: **maximum guests** (required) and **minimum guests** (optional). If someone searches for a venue for 180, we show them the venues where 180 sits between the minimum and the maximum.

The problem is that a venue's capacity isn't a single number: it depends on the layout. The same number of people don't fit seated for a meal as standing at a cocktail or in rows of chairs facing a stage. To make the search work in your favour:

- **For the maximum, enter what really fits in your largest layout**, the one you offer and can support with your permit, your toilets and your exits. Inflating it brings inquiries you then have to turn down, and a tour where the client discovers their event doesn't fit.
- **If you have a minimum for hiring it out, enter it as the minimum.** That way you don't get inquiries for 20-person parties at a 400-person venue.
- **In the description, give capacity by layout.** For example: how many fit at a banquet with round tables, how many at a standing cocktail, how many in theatre style. It's the figure the client will want to confirm and it saves them an inquiry.
- **If you know the floor area, enter it in m².** There's a field for it, and it helps people working out their own setup.

On the other side, organisers think like this. We explain it in [choosing an event venue by capacity](/en/blog/choosing-an-event-venue-by-capacity): reading it helps you answer before they ask.

## Location and access

The other half of the search is where you are. The **province is required**: without it you don't appear in any search by area. Then there's the **city or town** and the **address**, which are shown on your listing.

Fill in both. A good part of the decision is how far the venue is from where the guests live, and nobody inquires about a place they can't locate. Whatever doesn't have its own field, explain in the description:

- How to get there by public transport and from which avenue or road.
- Whether there's room for supplier trucks to come in and where they unload.
- Whether access has stairs or a ramp.

If your venue is wheelchair accessible or has toilets for people with disabilities, there are options to tick under "what the venue has". Tick them only if it's true from start to finish.

## Photos that help

Photos are uploaded from your dashboard, after you sign up: **up to 8, and the first one is the cover**. A venue without photos appears in the list with text only, and next to one with photos it loses out.

What's worth showing:

- **The whole venue, empty and in daylight.** It's the photo that shows the real size. A good candidate for the cover.
- **The venue set up for an event**, if you have your own photos of a real setup.
- **The entrance and the front of the building**, so people know what they'll find when they arrive.
- **Toilets, kitchen, outdoor area, parking**, whatever you have that sets you apart.

Avoid photos so filtered or so close up that the space can't be understood. And if a photo shows something that's no longer there (a garden that's now a car park, a bar that was removed), take it down.

## What's included and what isn't

The form has three places for this, and it's worth using all of them:

- **"What the venue has".** A checklist: parking, air conditioning, heating, equipped kitchen, crockery, furniture, sound, lighting, screen or projector, WiFi, outdoor space, pool, barbecue area, dressing rooms, security, generator, among others. Tick what's included in the hire.
- **Can outside catering be brought in?** Yes, no or not answered. It's one of the first things that decides whether a client keeps going, so if you know the answer, give it.
- **Is there parking?** Same again.

Anything charged separately or with conditions (closing time, whether the sound system is in-house or hired, whether cleaning is included) goes in the description, stated clearly. An inquiry that arrives knowing what the price includes is an inquiry that moves forward. How to handle it when it arrives is in [how to answer a venue inquiry](/en/blog/how-to-answer-a-venue-inquiry).

## What it's hired for, and how people find you by keyword

Under event types you tick what the venue is hired for: wedding, quinceañera, corporate event, conference or convention, product launch, training, live show, fair or expo, among others. Tick the ones you really do.

This matters because the keyword search in the LABURO venue directory looks at the name, your one-line description, the description, the city, the event types and what you ticked under "what the venue has". Someone who types "wedding" or "barbecue area" will see you if you entered it.

That's why two text fields are worth their weight in gold:

- **"In one line, what it is".** It's the first thing read under the name in the list. Something concrete, like "Venue with garden and barbecue area", works better than a slogan.
- **"Tell us what it's like".** This is where everything without a field goes: capacity by layout, access, what's charged separately, hours. Write it the way you'd tell someone over the phone.

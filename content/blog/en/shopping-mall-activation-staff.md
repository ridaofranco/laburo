An activation in a shopping mall looks easy to produce: the public is already there, there's light, a roof and security. But the shopping centre has its own hours and its own rules, and the team works long days standing in a corridor. This guide covers who you need, how the stand gets built and how shifts are organised so the last day looks the same as the first.

## What team you need

A shopping mall activation usually combines three groups with different hours:

- **Promo staff.** They deal with the public, explain the offer, invite people to take part and, if there's lead capture, handle it. It's the post that defines how the brand looks, and the one that most needs a [clear brief](/en/blog/brand-activation-brief-for-promo-staff).
- **Sampling.** If product is handed out to try, you need people to prepare it, serve it and keep it stocked. When there's food or alcohol, specific requirements apply: they're explained in [how to build a sampling team](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-sampling-team).
- **Setup and teardown.** The crew that builds the stand, connects whatever needs connecting and takes it down at the end. They work different hours from everyone else, almost always early morning or at night.

For activations lasting several days, add a fourth role: **someone to coordinate**, keep the shift schedule and be the only person who talks to the centre's management. If you're not sure whether you need one, see [when you need an event staff coordinator](/en/blog/when-you-need-an-event-staff-coordinator).

The number of people depends on the size of the space, how many people walk down the corridor and how long each interaction lasts. The pace-based maths is in [how many staff do you need for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event).

## The build: loading and unloading out of hours

In most shopping centres, loading and unloading isn't done with the public inside. You come in through a service entrance, at a time set by the mall, often with a goods lift shared by several shops. That has consequences for the setup crew:

- **The mall sets the time, not you.** Before calling people in, get in writing the day and loading window, the right entrance and whether the goods lift has to be booked.
- **A list of who comes in.** Many centres ask in advance for the names and ID numbers of everyone coming in through the service entrance. If someone isn't on the list, they don't get in, even if the stand is half built.
- **Early morning or night hours.** Offer the shift as such, with start and finish times, and think about how each person gets home.
- **Teardown is also out of hours.** If the activation ends on a Sunday at closing time, teardown may be that same night or the following early morning. That should be in the offer from the start.

For the people doing the physical work, [event setup and teardown: how to work safely](/en/blog/event-setup-and-teardown-work-safely) covers what's worth knowing.

## The shopping centre's rules

Each mall has its own regulations for activations and no two are the same. What's usually at stake:

- **The space you've hired.** Where your area ends and whether the team can approach people outside it.
- **Materials and structure.** What can rest on the floor, how tall the stand can be, which materials are allowed.
- **Electricity.** Whether there's a socket available, of what capacity and who connects it.
- **Food and drink.** Whether it can be served, under what conditions and where waste goes.
- **Sound.** Whether music or a microphone is allowed and at what volume.
- **Uniform and conduct.** Some centres have guidelines for staff working in their corridors.

The practical rule is just one: **get the regulations in writing** from the centre's management, keep them and pass them on to the team before the first shift. Arguing over a rule in the corridor, with the brand watching, is the worst thing that can happen to an activation.

## Long shifts with reliefs

A shopping mall is open for many hours in a row, every day. Nobody can be there from opening to closing with the same energy, and it shows in the activation when the team is tired. So:

1. **Split the day into shifts** and cover each position at the stand with a schedule, not with loose names.
2. **Overlap the handovers.** The incoming person arrives a few minutes early and takes over the materials, the product stock and any news from the previous shift.
3. **Write the breaks down** so the stand is never left empty and everyone knows when it's their turn.
4. **Think about the weekend.** Saturday and Sunday usually get more foot traffic: that's when you most need the full team.
5. **The hours go with the offer.** Each person has to know their exact slot before accepting.

The step by step for the schedule is in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events). If the activation is more like an exhibition stand, also see [trade show booth staff](/en/blog/trade-show-booth-staff).

## Common mistakes

- **Calling in the setup crew without the loading time confirmed.** If the mall changes the window, people already have other commitments.
- **Not sending the entry list in time.** Whoever isn't on it doesn't get in.
- **Full-day shifts.** The last stretch of the day looks weak, just when there are most people around.
- **Rules given verbally.** Whatever isn't written down gets argued about at the stand.
- **No single contact person with the centre.** If everyone talks to management, nobody knows what was agreed.

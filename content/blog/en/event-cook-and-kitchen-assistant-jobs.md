At an event, what the guest sees is the dining room. What makes the dining room work is behind it: a kitchen that has to send out hundreds of identical plates at the same time, often in a place that isn't an everyday kitchen. This guide is for anyone who wants to work as a cook, kitchen assistant or dishwasher at events: what changes, what each role does, which paperwork you'll be asked for and how to fill in your profile.

## What changes compared with a restaurant

In a restaurant you cook to order. At an event you cook **in production**:

- **A fixed menu, closed in advance.** There are no tickets: from the start you know what goes out, how many plates and in what order.
- **Everything at once.** When the floor calls a course, the kitchen has to plate it all together. There is no room for one batch to go out ten minutes later.
- **Lots of preparation beforehand.** A good part of the work is done hours before service: cutting, portioning, cooking bases, leaving everything ready to finish at the last moment.
- **Field kitchens.** You often work in a kitchen set up for the day at an outdoor site, in a marquee or in a venue's back kitchen, with ovens and burners that aren't yours. Making do with what's there is part of the trade.
- **Coordination with the floor.** The coordinator calls when each course goes out. The kitchen and the waiters work as a single team; how the other side experiences it is covered in [event waiter jobs](/en/blog/event-waiter-jobs).

## The three roles

In the LABURO catalogue, within bar and catering, the kitchen has three roles.

### Cook

Executes the menu set by the caterer or the chef in charge. Handles the cooking, makes sure every plate comes out the same and manages the timing of their station. Experience with volume is valued: cooking for forty covers is not the same as cooking for four hundred.

### Kitchen assistant

Does the mise en place (peeling, cutting, portioning, laying out trays), helps with plating on the line and keeps the cooks supplied. It is the role where you learn most about how a large production is organised, and a good way in if you come from another kitchen or are just starting. If it is your first time in the industry, see [event jobs with no experience](/en/blog/event-jobs-with-no-experience).

### Dishwasher

Washes crockery, pots, trays and utensils throughout service, and keeps the washing area tidy. It looks like the simplest role and is one of the most decisive: if the sink falls behind, the kitchen runs out of trays and the floor runs out of clean plates for the next course.

## What's expected

What comes up most often when people are called for an event kitchen:

- **Punctuality.** Production starts hours before the first guest arrives. Arriving late in a kitchen delays everything that comes after.
- **Hygiene and appearance.** Hair tied back or under a cap, short nails, no rings or bracelets, clean clothes. The uniform (chef's jacket, trousers, closed non-slip shoes) is set by whoever hires you: confirm it beforehand.
- **A tidy station.** Working clean and organised is what lets you produce volume without mistakes.
- **Following instructions.** At an event the menu and the plating are defined. Doing it the same way a hundred times is valued, not improvising.
- **Attention to allergens.** If there are special dishes (gluten-free, vegetarian), they are prepared separately and labelled. Ask how they are identified before service starts.
- **Stamina.** These are long shifts on your feet and in the heat. How hours are organised over a long day is in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).

## The food handler certificate

In Argentina, article 21 of the Argentine Food Code requires a food handler certificate for anyone who is or could be in contact with food in places where it is prepared, stored or sold. In a kitchen, that is the whole shift.

What the Argentine regulation says, in short:

- The only requirement to get it is **passing a safe food handling course** of at least 7 hours, in person or online, with an in-person assessment.
- The certificate **lasts 3 years** and renewing it requires an exam.
- It is **valid nationwide**.
- The employer is responsible for providing the conditions for the food handler to obtain it.

How to get it in practice and where to apply is in [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work). Having it up to date and on your profile keeps you from missing out on a call. If you work outside Argentina, the name and requirements depend on your country or city.

## What to ask before accepting

- **The full hours**, including prep beforehand and cleaning at the end.
- **Where the kitchen is**: whether it is fixed or a field kitchen, and what equipment there is.
- **What you need to bring**: uniform, your own knives, cap.
- **How many covers** are served and in what format (plated courses, cocktail, buffet).
- **The pay** and how it is paid.

If any of that isn't in the offer, ask before accepting.

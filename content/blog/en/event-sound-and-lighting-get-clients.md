A sound or lighting company lives on being called before the next one. And when an organiser looks for sound, they're not looking for equipment: they're looking for everything to sound right without having to understand equipment. This guide is about making your listing and the way you quote solve that for them.

## What to show: what you can handle, not just what you own

An equipment list is useful to another technician. The person hiring needs to know three things:

- **What kind of events you do.** Corporate events with speakers, weddings, parties, concerts, trade fairs, outdoor events. Doing sound for a conference is not the same as for a dance floor, and organisers know it.
- **How many people your equipment can cover.** Put it in terms of people and type of space, with what you can actually cover. If your rig works for an indoor venue but not an open-air site, say so: you'll save yourself enquiries you can't take.
- **Whether a technician comes with it.** And for how many hours, and whether they stay for the whole event or only for setup.

On LABURO, **Sound** and **Lighting** are separate categories, as are **LED screens**, **Audiovisual**, **Generators and power** and **Rigging**. If you do more than one thing, list one service per category: someone filtering by Lighting won't see a service listed as Sound. In each one, tick the provinces where you really work, because a service with no province doesn't show up in the services directory searches. The detail of each field is in [event supplier profile: what to fill in to get quote requests](/en/blog/event-supplier-profile-get-quote-requests).

In the details field, write what you're always asked: whether the price includes an operator, whether setup is included, whether you need a generator, whether you bring a desk and microphones. The "from" price is optional; if you include it, state the unit (per event, per day or per hour) and make it a figure you can stand by for the most basic service you described.

Your work is shown through your Instagram and website, which you add to your details. An Instagram of real jobs, with the stage set up and the dance floor lit, says more than any description.

## What to ask before quoting

A sound quote with no information is a gamble. There are four questions you shouldn't give a figure without, and you can put them straight into your enquiry form (the "How I want to be contacted" block in your dashboard), so they arrive already answered:

1. **The venue.** Indoor or outdoor, approximate size, whether it has a high ceiling, whether there are neighbours nearby. If you can, ask for photos or a visit. A room with glass walls doesn't sound the same as one with curtains.
2. **How many people.** And what kind of event: whether there are speakers, a live band, a DJ or just background music. That determines how much equipment and how many microphones.
3. **The times.** What time you can come in to set up, when the event starts and ends, and how long you have to take down. A setup that has to be ready by 9 a.m. changes the cost of labour.
4. **The power available.** What sockets there are, how far from the stage, whether the venue has enough capacity or you need a generator. It's the question that prevents the most problems and the one asked least.

Don't add many more: every extra question means more people abandoning the form before sending you the request. Whatever is missing can be asked later, when you reply.

On the other side, organisers tend to follow the logic of [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers): they want to compare like with like. If your quote makes clear what's included, it compares better than one that only has a total.

## Clear terms: what's written isn't argued about

Most problems with a technical supplier aren't about quality: they're about things nobody wrote down. In your quote, make clear:

- **What's included.** Equipment, number of technicians, setup and teardown, transport.
- **The hours.** How many the price covers and what overtime costs. Events run over; don't let that become an argument in the early hours.
- **Power.** Whether the venue provides it or you do, and who pays for the generator if one is needed.
- **Outdoors.** What happens if it rains: whether you need a roof or a tent over the desk and equipment, and whether you can call off the setup if there's no cover.
- **Payment and cancellation.** What percentage is paid to hold the date, when the balance is paid and what happens if the event is cancelled or moved.

When a request comes in, reply quickly and in order, with any missing questions at the top. The step by step is in [how to reply to an event quote request](/en/blog/how-to-reply-to-an-event-quote-request).

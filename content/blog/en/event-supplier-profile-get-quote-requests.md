Nobody reads a supplier profile: they scan it. On the other side is someone with a to-do list for their event, comparing three or four options in the same category in two minutes. This guide is so that, in those two minutes, you have what it takes for them to ask you for a price.

## How clients find you

In the LABURO services directory, people searching have three routes:

- **Category.** A category selector, such as sound, lighting, LED screens, catering and food, security, photography and video, DJ and music, or transport and logistics. The filter looks at the category of each service you added, and it has to match exactly.
- **Province.** It looks at the provinces you selected **in each service**, not your company's province.
- **A keyword.** It searches your name, your short tagline, your description, and the title, detail and category of each service.

That gives you the three rules for your profile: the right category in each service, provinces selected in each service and the words your client uses written somewhere.

## One service for each thing you do

The most expensive mistake is lumping everything together. If you do sound, lighting and screen rental, and you put it all in a single "Sound" service, you don't show up when someone filters by "Lighting" or "LED screens". Three services, three categories, three searches.

Each service has six fields:

| Field | What to put |
|---|---|
| Category | The one from the suggested list that best describes you: if you invent your own, people filtering by the list category won't see you |
| What you offer | What and at what scale: "Sound for events of up to 500 people" |
| Detail | What it includes, with what equipment, whether an operator comes with it |
| Starting price (ARS) | Optional, and only a guide |
| Per unit | Per person, per day, per event or per hour |
| Where you work | The provinces where you provide that service |

**Provinces, for real.** If you don't select any, that service doesn't appear in searches. If you select all of them and then quote with travel costs that double the price, the enquiry won't close. Select where you work without a surcharge, and say in the detail whether you travel to others.

## The short tagline and the description

**The short tagline** is what appears under your name. Category, scale and area, no superlatives: "Catering for corporate events of 50 to 500 people in Greater Buenos Aires" tells the person searching whether to keep reading. "The best catering in Buenos Aires" tells them nothing.

**The description** is for what applies to all your services:

- How long you've worked in events and in what type (corporate, social, concerts, trade fairs).
- How you work: how far in advance you need confirmation, whether you do a technical site visit, how you coordinate on the day.
- What you don't do. If you don't work outdoor events or don't take jobs under a certain number of people, say so.
- The paperwork you have in order and can send if asked. Only what you really have.

## The detail of each service: what you'll be asked anyway

Look at how quotes are requested from the other side in [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers): organisers want to compare like with like. If the detail already covers what's always asked in your category, your profile compares better. Some examples:

- **Sound or lighting:** what equipment is included, whether a technician comes and for how many hours, whether a generator is needed.
- **Catering:** what the per-person price includes, whether service staff come with it, whether tableware is included, whether you cater for coeliac and vegan guests, whether you need a kitchen on site.
- **Photography:** hours of coverage, how many edited photos and within how many days.
- **Structures:** dimensions, materials, whether setup is included and how far in advance.
- **Transport:** vehicles and seats, hours available, whether driver and tolls are included.

You don't need to answer everything with numbers. It's enough for people to understand what's included and what's charged separately.

## Starting price: include it or not

The field is optional and doesn't commit you: you agree the final price with each client. The case for including it is that it filters: people within your range contact you and you save yourself answering requests that won't close. If you include it, make the unit clear (per person, per day, per event, per hour) and make the number one you can stand by for the most basic service you described. If your prices change often, review it from time to time in your dashboard.

## Your questions, in the enquiry form

When someone asks you for a quote from your profile, they fill in a form and **it reaches you by email, already completed**. You also see it in your dashboard, under your enquiries. If you haven't built your own, a basic form is used with six questions: event type, date, venue, number of people, what they need and approximate budget (optional).

From the block for how you want to be contacted you can build your own: text, paragraph, number, date or options, up to a maximum of 12 questions and a short intro. Add the two or three you can't quote without in your category. A sound company asks whether it's outdoors and how much power is available; a caterer, how many covers and whether there are dietary restrictions. And don't overdo it: every extra question means people who give up before sending you the request.

## Photos and past work

The supplier profile has no photo gallery (that belongs to the venue profile). What shows your work is your **Instagram** and your **website**, which you add to your details. If your Instagram mixes personal and work content, consider a separate one for work: whoever hires wants to see how your jobs turned out, not your holidays.

## Review your profile

Check your profile against this list: one service per category, provinces selected in each one, a short tagline with scale and area, details covering what's always asked and your own enquiry form.

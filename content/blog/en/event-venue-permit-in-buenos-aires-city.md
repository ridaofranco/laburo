When a company or a production company is about to hold an event at a venue in Buenos Aires City, sooner or later the question comes up: is the venue authorised for this? This guide explains what a venue permit is according to the City's official sources, what tools exist to check it and what to ask the venue. If you run a venue, it tells you what you'll be asked for so you can have it ready.

One clarification before going on: **you won't find technical requirements or capacity per square metre here.** They depend on the type of business, the zone and the case, and for your venue what counts is what the Government Control Agency (AGC) or a licensed professional says. What follows is what the City's official pages published when we consulted them on 30/10/2026. Outside Buenos Aires City, the name and requirements of a venue permit depend on your country or city.

## What a venue permit is

The Buenos Aires City Government's permits page says that **every commercial activity must have an "Economic Activity Authorisation" before it starts operating**, and that complying helps avoid penalties such as fines or a ban on the activity. The agency in charge is the AGC.

For anyone hiring a venue, that becomes a concrete question: it's not enough for the venue to "have a permit", it has to be authorised **for the activity you're going to hold**. A space can be authorised for one type of business and not another.

## How to know which permit applies

The AGC has an **online permits assistant**. According to the official page, it shows the permit options for a given location and type of business:

1. You enter the address and the floor area to be authorised.
2. You see which activities can be authorised at that location, or search for a specific activity.
3. When you choose an activity, it shows the zones where it's allowed, the type of permit that applies, the steps of the procedure and the paperwork needed.

If you run a venue or are about to open one, that's the first place to look. If you're hiring, it helps you understand whether what the venue tells you makes sense for that address.

## How to check whether a venue has or had a permit

There are two official routes:

- **Request for the permit history of commercial premises.** It's a free procedure, done online on the TAD platform, which requires a level 3 miBA user account (the City's digital ID). You enter the address with its street numbers, floor or unit, and the period when the permit was granted. The page doesn't say how long a reply takes, so don't leave it for the week of the event.
- **The AGC's Digital Book.** The Agency describes it as a tool to see information on the authorised economic activity and on inspections. Access requires an Argentine tax ID password at level 2 or 3, so in practice it's the owner of the premises who checks it.

That's why the simplest thing is almost always **to ask the venue for the paperwork**. A venue that has it in order sends it without any problem.

## If the event exceeds one thousand people

The City has its own procedure for mass events. According to its page, a **mass event** is an occasional act, meeting or happening with artistic or festive purposes, with a gathering of **more than one thousand attendees**. What the procedure says:

- It has to be filed at least **20 calendar days** before the event date.
- The AGC handles it. If the event is in a **private space**, the file goes straight to the AGC; if it's in public space, to the Ministry of Public Space and Urban Hygiene.
- Among the paperwork it asks for, among other things: the registration certificate in the Public Register of Mass Event Producers, the plan with capacity per sector, exits and toilets, civil liability insurance, a noise impact report, Civil Defence approval of the Self-Protection System, proof of the right to occupy the site and **the venue's permit certificate**.

That last point is what links the two: if you're holding a mass event at a venue, the venue's permit is part of your file. What you need to know about security at those events is in [private security at events in Buenos Aires](/en/blog/private-security-at-events-buenos-aires).

## What to ask the venue before booking

If you're organising, add these questions to the ones about capacity and format covered in [choosing an event venue by capacity](/en/blog/choosing-an-event-venue-by-capacity):

1. **What activity is the venue authorised for?** Ask them to tell you the name of the activity, not just "yes, everything's in order".
2. **Does the permit cover your event's format?** If there will be live music, dancing, a show or ticket sales, ask exactly that, in those words.
3. **What capacity appears on the paperwork?** It should match what they promise for your layout.
4. **Can they send you the paperwork in writing?** A copy or the file number. If your client is a company, they'll probably ask you for it.
5. **Who files whatever your event needs?** If you exceed one thousand people, who prepares the mass event request and how far in advance.
6. **What happens if an inspection raises an issue?** Who answers for it and what happens to the booking. Get it written into the conditions.

## If you run a venue

Have your permit paperwork ready to send as a PDF the same day you're asked for it, and say in your listing what types of events your space is for. If a company asks for it and it takes you a week to find it, the enquiry goes to another venue.

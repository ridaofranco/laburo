At an event, the people who drive are the first and last people a guest sees. The valet opens the car door on arrival and hands the car back on departure. The driver or shuttle driver brings the guest from the hotel and takes them back. This guide is for anyone who wants to do that work: what each role does, which licence the law sets in Argentina, what you'll be asked for and how to set up your profile so the offer reaches you.

## Three roles that look alike but aren't the same

At LABURO, the **transport** category has three roles:

- **Valet.** You take the guest's car, park it in the agreed spot and bring it back at the exit. You don't take the car out on the road for long: you move it from the door to the car park and walk back.
- **Driver.** You drive a vehicle with specific passengers: special guests, artists, people from the organisation. Sometimes the car is provided by whoever hires you and sometimes something else is agreed: that has to be in the offer.
- **Shuttle driver.** You run a fixed route with several passengers, for example from the hotel to the venue or from a distant car park to the entrance, and repeat it on a schedule.

If you have never driven for an event, valet work is usually the most common way in. Driver and shuttle roles ask for more: a different licence in many cases, knowing the city and looking after passengers during the trip.

## What a valet shift looks like from the inside

The work isn't even. There are two busy moments and a long stretch in between.

**Arrival.** Almost all guests arrive within the same half hour. You receive, greet, check the car, hand over the ticket, park and get back to the door fast. Every minute you take turns into a queue in the street.

**The middle.** It is quiet. Keys are organised, cars are moved if needed, breaks are taken in turns. It isn't free time: you need to be reachable.

**Departure.** It is the peak that shows most, because guests are tired and want to leave right away. That is when the whole team is needed again.

How whoever hires puts that team together (how many valets, how the peak is calculated, how keys are logged) is in [hiring valet parking for an event](/en/blog/hiring-valet-parking-for-an-event). Reading it helps you understand what's expected of you. What's down to you, in short:

- **The car is only handed over against the ticket.** If someone has lost it, the person at the key desk sorts it out with coordination, not you on the pavement.
- **The key always goes back on the board.** You never keep one in your pocket "to save time".
- **If you see a dent or a scratch when you take the car, it gets noted.** That is what protects you if a complaint turns up later.
- **Drive as if the car were borrowed, because it is.** Slowly, with no unnecessary manoeuvres and without touching anything inside that you don't need to.

## What the law says about licences in Argentina

All of this comes from Argentina's National Traffic Law 24,449, updated text:

- **Class B (article 16):** the licence for cars and vans. It is the baseline for valets and car drivers.
- **Class D (article 16):** the licence for vehicles used for passenger transport. It is the one to look at if you want to drive a shuttle. The law states that the size of the vehicle, among other things, defines subclasses set by the regulations: check with the authority that issues your licence which one applies to the vehicle you'll be driving.
- **Professional licences (article 20):** classes C, D and E are professional driver licences, and to get them you must have held class B for at least a year.
- **Ages (article 11):** 21 for classes C, D and E; 17 for the others, including B.
- **Carrying it (article 40, point a):** to drive, the driver must be licensed for that type of vehicle and carry the licence.
- **Zero alcohol (article 48, point a):** driving any vehicle with a blood alcohol level above zero is banned (text as amended by Law 27,714). At an event, with an open bar nearby, this is not negotiable: zero throughout the shift.

In practice: before marking a role, check which licence you hold and when it expires. If the offer is to drive a vehicle your licence doesn't cover, don't accept it. If you are outside Argentina, the licence rules depend on local law.

## What's expected

Beyond the licence, what gets you called again:

- **Punctuality.** The first guest may arrive early. If the team isn't there, the queue starts on its own.
- **Good appearance and manners.** You are the face of the event on the pavement. Greeting, opening the door, a friendly answer when there is a delay. What's expected in clothing and presentation is in [event staff dress code](/en/blog/event-staff-dress-code).
- **Smooth, calm driving.** All kinds of cars, manual and automatic gearboxes, tight spaces. If you don't know how to drive a car, say so before getting in.
- **Finding your way quickly.** For drivers and shuttles, knowing the area, having the route clear and a plan if a road is closed.
- **Stamina for the end.** Departure is often in the early hours. Think about how you'll get home before accepting: there are ideas in [getting home after a late-night event](/en/blog/getting-home-after-a-late-night-event).

Before you confirm, make sure the offer is clear on: full hours (arrival and the departure of the last cars), which vehicle you'll drive, where cars are parked and the pay. If anything is missing, ask before accepting.

1. **Upload your CV.** A good part of the form fills itself in; then you check and correct it.
2. **Mark the roles you really do:** valet, driver or shuttle driver. You can mark several. If you also do something else at events (access control, for example), add it.

4. **Mark "own transport"** if you have it, and your real area with province and city.
5. **Say how much notice you need** to take a job and whether you are available at weekends or to travel.

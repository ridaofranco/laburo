The tour is the moment an inquiry turns into a booking or gets lost. The person has already seen your photos, already written to you and you've already replied: now they're coming to confirm that what they imagined is real. This guide is for owners and managers of event venues, country houses and event spaces, and it goes in order: when to offer the tour, what to show first, what to have ready, what you'll be asked, what to ask yourself and how to follow up.

## When to offer it

The tour is offered **after** checking two things: that the date is free and that the venue works for that number of people in that layout. Otherwise, you both lose an afternoon. How to answer that first inquiry is in [how to answer a venue inquiry](/en/blog/how-to-answer-a-venue-inquiry).

Suggest **two or three specific times**, not "drop by whenever you like". If you can, make one similar to the time of the event: a venue in the morning with daylight doesn't look the same as at night with the lights on. And if the person is going to decide with someone else (their partner, their boss, the quinceañera's family), ask them to bring that person. A tour with the decision maker is worth two.

## Before they arrive

Half an hour beforehand, walk through the venue as if you were a guest:

- **Clean and lit.** Switch on everything that will be used at the event, including outdoor lights if there are any.
- **Spotless toilets.** It's one of the things people remember most after a tour.
- **A comfortable temperature.** If you have air conditioning or heating, have it running, so it shows.
- **Nothing left over from another event** in sight, unless it's set up on purpose to show a layout.

## What to show and in what order

The best order is the route a guest takes on the day of the event. That way the person pictures their party instead of seeing a list of rooms:

1. **Arrival.** Where people park, where they come in, whether there's a ramp or steps.
2. **The entrance and reception.** Where guests are welcomed, where a cloakroom or registration desk can go.
3. **The main space.** Explain where the tables, the dance floor, the bar and the stage or sound booth usually go.
4. **The toilets.** How many there are and where they are in relation to the main room.
5. **The outdoor area**, if you have a garden, terrace or pool. If you have outdoor space, the rain plan goes here too (we cover it in [event venues with a pool in the summer season](/en/blog/event-venue-with-pool-summer-season)).
6. **The service areas.** Kitchen, storage, supplier access, power points and emergency exits.

Leave the service areas for the end: whoever hires the caterer or the sound company cares a lot about them, but they're not what makes people fall for the place.

## What to have ready

Whatever they ask on the tour, they'll compare with other venues. If you have it in writing, they take it with them:

- **Floor plan or measurements** of the main space and the outdoor area.
- **Capacity by layout.** Seated at tables, standing cocktail, theatre. It's the figure that causes most confusion, and the logic from the other side is in [choosing an event venue by capacity](/en/blog/choosing-an-event-venue-by-capacity).
- **A list of what the hire includes and what it doesn't.** Furniture, crockery, sound, cleaning, security, setup and teardown hours.
- **Booking terms.** Deposit, until when you hold the date, what happens if they cancel, when the balance is paid.
- **Suppliers who know the venue**, if you have them, and whether using them is compulsory or not.

If your venue is in Buenos Aires City, they may also ask about your permit: what's worth knowing is in [event venue permits in Buenos Aires City](/en/blog/event-venue-permit-in-buenos-aires-city).

## The questions you'll be asked

The same ones almost always come up. It's worth having a short answer ready:

- Until what time can we stay, and until what time with music?
- Can I bring my own caterer, bar or sound?
- How many hours beforehand can we set up?
- Where do guests and suppliers park?
- What happens if there's a power cut?
- What happens if it rains? (if there's an outdoor area)
- What exactly does the price include?

If you don't know something, say you'll find out and send it in writing. An answer made up on the tour is an argument on the day of the event.

## What you ask

The tour also helps you quote properly. Ask:

- **Date, start and finish times.**
- **Number of people and layout**: seated, standing, mixed.
- **Type of event.** A wedding, a corporate event and a quinceañera call for different things.
- **Which suppliers they're bringing and which they're looking for.**
- **Whether there will be minors** and how guests will arrive.
- **Who else is deciding**, so you know whether another visit is needed.

Write it down on the spot, not that night from memory.

## Follow-up after the tour

That same day or the next, send **a single email** with everything: a summary of what you discussed, the price, what's included, the terms and the exact day until which you'll hold the date with no commitment. If they asked something on the tour you didn't know, the answer goes in that email.

If they don't reply, one reminder before the hold date expires is enough. If they say no, thank them and ask what made them choose another place: that answer helps you with the next tour.

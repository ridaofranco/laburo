Almost every argument with staff after an event is about money, and almost none is down to bad faith. They're about things that weren't said: whether the meal was included, whether teardown was paid separately, from what time overtime started. This guide is for people who hire and want to close the event with a clear calculation, not an angry WhatsApp group.

We won't give you amounts: they depend on the role, the area and the event. For a reference there's [how much event staff cost](/en/blog/how-much-does-event-staff-cost). Here's the method.

## 1. Put everything in writing before you book anyone

What isn't written down gets remembered differently on each side. Before sending a single offer, define:

- **Amount.** Per day, per shift or per hour. One, and clear.
- **What it includes.** How many hours that amount covers, whether it includes setup or teardown, whether there's a meal, whether there are travel expenses or transport.
- **Call time and estimated finish time.** With setup and teardown included, not just the event's hours.
- **Overtime.** How it's paid and who authorises it.
- **Dropouts.** Until when people can give notice and what happens if someone doesn't show up.
- **When and how payment is made.** A specific date and payment method. "After the event" isn't a date.
- **What paperwork you need.** If you're going to ask for an invoice, say so from the start. The name and requirements depend on your country; in Argentina, workers usually invoice under the simplified tax scheme explained in [monotributo for event workers in Argentina](/en/blog/monotributo-for-event-workers-argentina), and for the hiring side there's [paperwork for hiring temporary event staff](/en/blog/paperwork-for-hiring-temporary-event-staff).

On LABURO, each offer carries the role, date and time, venue, **amount** and a **conditions** field for hours, tasks and anything else you want to make clear. The person reads it in full and accepts from their phone. Use that field to write everything above: everyone gets the same information, in writing.

## 2. Measure the working day with a record, not with memory

If pay depends on hours, you need to know what time each person arrived and left. A paper sheet signed by the coordinator works until it gets wet, gets lost or someone signs for someone else.

LABURO has **clock-in**: each person confirmed for the event clocks in and out from their phone, with their GPS location. On the event **board** you see who clocked in and at what time. If someone forgot to clock out, you correct it by hand and write the reason; corrected entries stay marked, so it's clear the person didn't clock it themselves.

Two rules that help:

1. **Say at the briefing that people clock in**, and who to tell if their phone isn't working.
2. **Check the clock-ins before the team leaves**, not the next day. Correcting with the person in front of you avoids the argument.

If the event has long shifts or relief staff, it's worth planning them beforehand with [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events): each shift with its own hours makes measuring and paying simpler.

## 3. Overtime: authorised and noted on the spot

The event runs over: the client asks to stay longer, teardown takes more time, the truck arrives late. Overtime itself isn't the problem. The problem is overtime nobody authorised and everyone counts differently.

- **One person authorises** overtime (you or whoever is coordinating) and says it out loud to the team: "we're staying an extra hour, it's paid as agreed".
- **The rule for how it's paid is written in the offer** from the start.
- **It's noted at closing**, with the clock-in record as backup.

## 4. Dropouts and absences: the same rule for everyone

Someone will drop out. What matters is that the rule is written beforehand and applied the same way to everyone. Some points to define:

- Until when a dropout can be notified.
- What happens with someone who doesn't show up without notice.
- What happens with someone who had to leave early for a valid reason: they're paid for the time worked according to the clock-in.

The day of the event isn't the time to do sums: the urgent thing is covering the position. That's what [what to do when event staff don't show up](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do) is for. The calculation goes on the closing sheet.

## 5. The closing sheet

When the event ends, put together a sheet with one row per person. The minimum columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Name | Who worked |
| Role | Which position |
| Agreed amount | The one in the offer |
| Clock-in and clock-out | From the record (mark if corrected) |
| Extras | Authorised hours and amount |
| Travel expenses | If applicable |
| Deductions | Only if they're in the written rule |
| Total | The sum |
| Paid | Date and method |

On LABURO, the Payments screen shows what you've committed to the staff who confirmed: the amount per person and per event, and the total. That's your base: you transfer it to your sheet, add the extras and travel expenses columns and your closing is ready.

Send each person their row before paying. If someone spots a mistake, it gets corrected before, not after.

## 6. Pay when you said you would

What weighs most in getting the same team to want to work with you again isn't the amount: it's paying on the date you promised. If something is delayed, tell them before the date, not after. A team that trusts it will be paid is a team that doesn't drop out.

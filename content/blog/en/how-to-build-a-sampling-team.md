A sampling activation looks simple: someone with a tray and a smile. In practice it's an operation with stock, pace, message and reporting. If the team is well built, at the end of the day the brand knows how many units went out, where, and what people said. If it isn't, there are boxes left over at one point, shortages at another and nobody knows what happened. This guide is for agencies and brands that need to put that team together.

## What the team does

Three tasks, in this order of importance:

- **Offer the product.** Approach, invite people to try, hand out the sample in the size the brand defined. Don't chase anyone and don't give away too much.
- **Deliver the message.** One or two sentences the brand chose (what it is, what makes it different, where to buy it). The team doesn't improvise benefits or promise things that aren't in the brief.
- **Record what's handed out.** Units at the start, restocks, units at close and waste. It's the figure the brand will use to measure the activation, and if it isn't written down on the spot, it gets made up later.

Around that: keep the stand clean, protect the cold chain if the product needs it, restock before running out and tell coordination when something goes off plan.

## How many people to staff

The calculation comes from pace, not from an imagined peak. You need three figures per point:

1. **How many people pass** in the busiest hour (the venue can tell you, or a site visit, or experience from previous activations at that point).
2. **How many samples one person hands out per hour** with your product. Handing over a sachet isn't the same as pouring a measure, explaining and cleaning up.
3. **How many hours the activation lasts**, to plan relief staff and breaks.

**Example with assumed numbers** (just to show the calculation, not real data): a point in a supermarket where, in the best hour, you want to hand out 120 samples, and one person pouring and explaining manages about 60 per hour. That's 2 people at the point in that hour. If the activation lasts 8 hours, you add at least one relief person to cover breaks without closing the stand. With 3 points like that, plus one person coordinating and restocking between points, the team comes to 10. By measuring the real pace on day one, you adjust the remaining dates.

The general sizing logic is in the guide on [how many staff you need for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event), and if the activation is inside a trade fair, in [trade show booth staff](/en/blog/trade-show-booth-staff).

## What profile to ask for

- **A natural way with strangers.** Knowing how to approach without intruding and accepting a no without insisting.
- **Remembering the message.** Being able to repeat what the brand says without reading it and without adding to it.
- **Order with stock.** Counting, noting down and restocking without being asked.
- **Hygiene and tidiness** if there's food or drink: nails, hair tied back, gloves or tongs depending on the product.
- **Punctuality and stamina.** Many activations mean standing for long hours, sometimes on the street.

In LABURO the role is called **Sampling / tasting**. Depending on the activation, **Promoter** and **Brand ambassador** (when the brand wants someone who knows the product in depth) also work. For a fuller view of promo roles, read [shopping mall activation staff](/en/blog/shopping-mall-activation-staff).

## Food handling

If the team hands out food or drink, check the food handler certificate. In Argentina, article 21 of the Argentine Food Code covers anyone who is or may be in contact with food in places where it's prepared, stored or sold, and a tasting usually falls under that. What the rule says, how to get the certificate and how long it lasts is in the guide on the [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work). Outside Argentina, the name and requirements depend on your country or city. Ask for it when you call people in, not on the day of the activation.

## If there's alcohol

In Argentina, **Law 24,788** (the national law against alcoholism) is the reference. Three points directly affect a tasting:

- **Article 1:** bans the sale of alcoholic drinks to under-18s nationwide.
- **Article 6, paragraph a:** bans advertising aimed at under-18s.
- **Article 7:** bans contests or events that require drinking alcohol in a way that distorts the principles of tasting.

For the team, that means: the age rule has to be written in the brief (how to ask, which documents are valid, what to do if someone doesn't show one), the tasting measure has to be a single one everyone knows, and nobody on the team suggests drinking games or pours too much. The team should also know how to politely cut off someone who has had enough, and who to call if things get tense.

## What to report at the end of the day

Ask for a short report, the same at every point, so you can add them up:

| Item | What it includes |
|---|---|
| Point and hours | Where the team was and from what time to what time |
| Stock | Units at the start, restocks, units at close |
| Handed out | Units that went out, by time slot if possible |
| Waste | Broken, expired or discarded product, with the reason |
| Comments | What people asked or said most, without making anything up |
| Incidents | Shortages, problems with the venue, age cases |
| Photos | Of the stand set up and running, if the brand asks for them |

How to write the brief that feeds all of this is in the guide on [the brief for promo staff at an activation](/en/blog/brand-activation-brief-for-promo-staff).

## Common mistakes

- **Sizing the team by the peak without looking at pace.** You end up with people standing around half the day or queues at the busy hour.
- **Not planning relief.** The stand closes at lunchtime, exactly when most people pass.
- **A long or open message.** Each person says something different and the brand loses control of what's said.
- **Recording at the end, from memory.** The numbers don't add up and the report is useless for measuring.
- **An age rule that's spoken, not written**, at activations with alcohol.
- **Asking for the certificate on the day.** If someone doesn't have it, it's too late to replace them.
- **Nobody to call.** Every point needs to know who's coordinating and how to reach them.

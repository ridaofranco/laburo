"How much will the staff cost me?" is always the first question, and the only honest answer is that it depends on five things you haven't told me yet.

You won't find a rate table here. You'll find the structure of the cost: what makes it up, what always gets forgotten and how to tell whether the number you were given is expensive or cheap for what you're asking.

## The five variables of the cost

Every staff quote, wherever it comes from, is built from these:

1. **How many people per role.** The headcount, calculated by service points and not by number of guests ([here is the math](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event)).
2. **How many real hours each one covers.** From call time to teardown.
3. **What that role is worth.** A loader is not the same as a rigger or a sound technician.
4. **What the time slot adds.** Late night, public holiday, an event out of town.
5. **How much buffer you keep for no-shows.** The extra people you add so a missing person doesn't leave a point empty.

If a quote doesn't let you see these five, you can't compare it with another one.

## The most underestimated variable: real hours

The most expensive mistake in the trade is budgeting for the length of the event instead of the length of the shift.

A four-hour event is almost never a four-hour shift. It's a call time one or two hours earlier, a briefing, the event itself, and then a close that drags on. The person is on duty long before the first guest arrives and leaves well after the last one.

Multiply that gap by twenty people and there's the difference between what you budgeted and what you paid.

**Always quote by hours on duty, not hours of show.**

## What makes one role worth more than another

When you compare two numbers for "event staff", you are really comparing different things. What pushes the value of a position up is always one of these:

- **Responsibility for money or safety.** Till, access control, first aid, rigging.
- **A certification, licence or their own tools.** An electrician, a driver, a drone operator.
- **Scarcity of the profile in your area.** A video technician with LED experience is hard to find on a Saturday night.
- **Autonomy.** Being able to send the person alone without having to explain anything.
- **The time slot.** A teardown that starts at 3 a.m. isn't paid like an afternoon shift.

That's why a quote that says "20 event staff" and gives one single number is useless: it can't be audited.

## The five costs nobody budgets for

And that get paid anyway, almost always out of your margin:

1. **Teardown.** Setup gets budgeted and the exit gets forgotten, even though it happens late at night with fewer people available.
2. **Relief staff.** Meals and breaks. If you don't count them, they come out of the same team and you get empty positions at the peak.
3. **Travel costs.** An event that ends after public transport has stopped has a cost for getting home, whether you put it in the spreadsheet or not.
4. **Staff meals.** They exist. Always.
5. **Coordination.** Someone has to manage the temporary staff on the day. If that someone is you, it isn't free: it's the hour you're not producing.

## What you are really paying for

When you compare two staff quotes, you're not comparing people. You're comparing **how much uncertainty you take off your plate**:

- Profiles you can check before confirming, not a name on a spreadsheet.
- A confirmation that is explicit and in writing, with amount, dates and times inside it.
- Being able to see, before the day of the event, how many positions are truly covered and how many are still open.
- A record of who was there and during which hours, so payroll isn't put together from memory.

A cheaper quote that leaves you without all four isn't cheaper: it's the same price, with the risk on your side.

## Margin: the calculation almost nobody gets right

Margin is simple: **what you charge the client minus what your confirmed staff costs you**.

The catch is in "confirmed". If you calculate cost on the offers you sent, you're measuring an intention. Only what's accepted is committed money, and only what's accepted is a covered spot.

## How to request a quote you can compare

Always send this, and you'll get numbers you can audit:

- Date, venue and address.
- Call time and estimated finish time, **including setup and teardown**.
- Role and quantity for each position.
- Whether meals and transport are included or not.
- What happens if someone doesn't show up.

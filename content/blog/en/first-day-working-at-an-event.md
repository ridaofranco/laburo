You've accepted your first offer. Now comes the part nobody tells you about: what the day is like, what time you really arrive, what to bring and how your hours get recorded.

This guide covers exactly that, in chronological order.

## Before: reread the offer

The offer you accepted already has almost everything you need to know: **the role, the event, when, where, the pay and the conditions**.

That last field, conditions, is the one most often skipped and the one that prevents most problems. It's where the producer spells out what's particular to that event: clothing, whether you need to bring something, any detail that doesn't fit in the other fields. Read it the night before, not on the bus.

If something isn't clear, ask beforehand. Asking the day before is normal; asking at the door, with the event starting, is too late for everyone.

## The reminder: your safety net

The day before or on the day of the event you get a **reminder of your shift** by email, with the role, the event and the hours.

That email says something important: if something has happened and you won't make it, let us know right away on WhatsApp.

Take it seriously, because it's the difference between a problem and a disaster. **A warning hours ahead can be covered.** The same absence discovered with the doors open leaves an empty position and ruins someone else's event. Nobody will punish you for letting them know; what does keep you out of future calls is disappearing.

## Hours: call time is not opening time

The time in your offer is the **call time**, and it almost never matches the time the event opens. People are called earlier on purpose: for the briefing, for setup, so everything is running when the audience arrives.

Three things worth being clear about on your first event:

- **Arrive on time, not a few minutes later.** At events there's no margin: if the doors open at 8 p.m., someone has to be there at 8 p.m.
- **Shifts can be long** and in most roles you're on your feet the whole time.
- **Setup and teardown exist.** If your role is setup or teardown, your work happens before the audience arrives or after it leaves, often in the early hours.

And a practical tip that isn't LABURO's, it's the trade's: **sort out how you'll get home before you go.** A teardown that ends at 5 a.m. in an area with no transport is a problem if you only think about it at 5 a.m. More on that in [getting home after a late-night event](/en/blog/getting-home-after-a-late-night-event).

## What to bring

- **Your ID.** You may be asked for it at the door or by production.
- **A charged phone.** Clocking in is done from your phone. A charger or power bank is never too much on a long shift.
- **The offer at hand**, with the hours and the address.
- **Whatever the conditions** of your offer say: clothing, footwear, tools.
- **Comfortable shoes**, if nobody specified otherwise. You'll be standing more than you think.

The full list is in [what to bring when working an event](/en/blog/what-to-bring-when-working-an-event).

## How to get there

In your staff panel you'll see your **next confirmed event**, your schedule of upcoming events and a **directions** link that opens the location on the map. You log in to the panel with your email and password, or with Google; if it's your first time or you don't remember it, you request a link by email to create it.

If it's a place you don't know, look it up the night before. Event venues often have several entrances, and the staff entrance isn't the public one.

## Clocking in and out

At events where it's enabled, your hours don't depend on anyone's memory: you record them yourself.

It works like this: you open your staff panel on your phone, and **when you arrive you tap clock in**. At that moment your location is taken to show you were there, so **you need to give the browser location permission: without it the clock-in can't be completed**. If you're far from the venue you can still clock in, but the distance is recorded.

When you finish, **you tap clock out**. That closes your shift and records your real hours.

Two things worth knowing:

- **Clock out.** It's as important as clocking in: it's what defines how many hours you worked.
- **Your history stays visible.** Your panel builds up the jobs you've done, with clocked hours and total earnings.

That record is what backs up your pay. If there's ever a difference, it's raised with the data in front of everyone instead of arguing from memory.

## During: the three first-day questions

As soon as you arrive, get these three answered and the day becomes much easier:

1. **Who is my point of contact?** Who you ask and who you tell if something happens.
2. **What exactly is my position?** Where you stand and what you do. "Support staff" isn't a role: ask them to define it.
3. **How do breaks and meals work?** When and with whom you swap.

And a general rule for a first event: if you don't know something, ask early. At an event, a mistake corrected at 7 p.m. doesn't exist; the same mistake at 11 p.m., with the bar full, does.

## What gets you a second event

Accepting an offer means **you're confirmed for that event**. It isn't a permanent job and there's no guaranteed number of events.

But what you do control is everything that gets you called again, and it's simpler than it seems: **arrive on time, do your job well, clock in and out, and let people know as soon as you know you can't make it.**

In a trade where most problems come from people who don't turn up, being reliable is the best card you have.

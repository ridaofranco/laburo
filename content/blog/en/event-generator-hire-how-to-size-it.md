A generator that cuts out mid-show leaves the stage dark, kills the sound and stops the bars all at once. It's almost never the equipment's fault: it's a power calculation done by eye, a tank that wasn't big enough or one power strip too many on the board. This guide is for production companies, businesses and organisers about to hire a generator: when you need one, how to give the supplier what they need, where to put it and who looks after it during the event.

## When you need one

Three situations:

- **There's no mains power.** Outdoor sites, fields, beaches, squares, car parks. The generator is the only source.
- **The mains supply isn't enough.** The venue has power, but its board can't handle sound, lighting, screens and catering together. Ask the venue in writing how much power is available and on which board.
- **You can't afford to lose power.** Even with mains, a cut would stop the event. Then the generator is backup for what's critical: walkway and exit lighting, stage sound, tills and access control.

If you're torn between mains and generator, ask the venue for the figure before asking for quotes. It saves a lot of back and forth.

## How to give the supplier the power you need

The most common mistake is asking for "a big generator". The supplier needs a list. Build it like this:

1. **Everything that plugs in.** Sound, lighting, screens or LED, video, catering (fridges, coffee machines, griddles, electric ovens), bars, marquees with lighting or climate control, walkway and toilet lighting, equipment charging, production office.
2. **The power of each item.** The figure on the equipment's rating plate or in its manual, or the one each technical supplier gives you. Ask the sound, lighting and screen suppliers for their total consumption in writing: having it is part of their job.
3. **When each one runs.** Not everything is on at the same time. Catering may run hard before the show and ease off afterwards; stage lighting, the opposite.
4. **What starts with a surge.** Fridges, cold rooms, pumps and motorised equipment draw more on start-up than when running. Mark them on the list.
5. **What's critical.** What can never go off, so it can be separated onto its own circuit or a backup unit.

With that list the supplier works out the unit and the right margin. Don't pick a number yourself: give them the loads and let them come back with a proposal and the calculation. If sound and lighting come from a separate supplier, coordinate with them; how that trade works is in [event sound and lighting: getting clients](/en/blog/event-sound-and-lighting-get-clients).

## Backup

Ask yourself what happens if the generator stops. The options, from least to most:

- **Separate circuits**, so a fault in catering doesn't kill the stage.
- **A second unit** for what's critical.
- **Two units in parallel** or a spare ready to connect, at large or long events.

Whatever you choose, make sure it's written in the quote.

## Fuel

- **For how many hours.** Count from the sound check to pack-down, not just the show times. Setup and tests use fuel too.
- **Who supplies it and who refuels.** If the tank won't last the whole event, who brings fuel, at what time and through which access.
- **Where it's stored.** Away from the public and any heat source, in suitable containers and with an extinguisher nearby.

## Location: noise and fumes

- **Always outdoors.** Petrol or diesel generators can produce carbon monoxide, as the [Ministry of Health of Río Negro](https://rionegro.gov.ar/articulo/12194/salud-advierte-sobre-riesgo-de-intoxicacion-con-monoxido-de-carbono-ante-frios-intensos), an Argentine province, warns. Never inside a marquee, a shed or an enclosed space.
- **Exhaust pointing where there are no people**, away from marquees, dressing rooms, kitchens and air intakes.
- **Away from the stage and the audience** because of the noise. If possible, behind a structure or with a soundproofed unit.
- **Barriers around it**, so nobody leans on it or touches cables.
- **Firm, level ground**, with access for the truck that delivers and collects it.

## Distribution board and cabling

The generator isn't plugged straight into the equipment. You need a **distribution board** with protection on each circuit and earthing, and cables of the right length and gauge. In Argentina, [Decree 351/79, which regulates Law 19,587 on Health and Safety at Work](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/30000-34999/32030/dto351-1979-anexo1.htm), requires in articles 95 and 96 that electrical installations and equipment avoid risks to people or property and that materials meet the relevant technical standards; the technical detail is in its [Annex VI](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/30000-34999/32030/dto351-1979-anexo6.htm).

In practice:

- **Cables away from where people walk**, or covered with cable protectors at crossings.
- **No daisy-chained power strips** or joints made with tape.
- **A closed, signposted board with a roof**, if rain is possible. For that, read the [rain plan for outdoor event staff](/en/blog/rain-plan-for-outdoor-event-staff).
- **A simple plan** of where each circuit goes, held by production and the electrician.

## Who operates it during the event

Someone has to stay with the generator from start to finish: watch the fuel, check the load, deal with a tripped breaker and talk to sound and lighting before adding equipment. [Article 98 of Decree 351/79](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/30000-34999/32030/dto351-1979-anexo1.htm) states that maintenance work is done exclusively by trained and authorised staff. Ask whether the supplier includes an operator or whether you provide one. In the second case, add an **electrician** to the staff. For large outdoor events, the general logic is in [outdoor festival staffing](/en/blog/outdoor-festival-staffing).

## What to ask when quoting

| Topic | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Unit | What power they propose for your list of loads and with what margin |
| Backup | Whether a spare unit or separate circuits are included |
| Fuel | For how many hours, who refuels and how much extra costs |
| Noise | Whether the unit is soundproofed |
| Board and cables | Whether they're included, with what protection and how many metres |
| Operator | Whether someone stays for the whole event or they only deliver and collect |
| Times | Delivery, load test, collection, in writing |
| Access | What truck they use and where it gets in |
| Emergencies | A phone number that answers during the event |

Ask for everything in writing and compare on the same list of loads. How to put that request together is in [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers).

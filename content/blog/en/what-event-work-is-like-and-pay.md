If you're thinking about working at events, this is what's worth knowing first: what the work is really like, how you get in, how offers arrive and (the question everyone asks and few answer clearly) **when you get paid**.

## What the work is like, without the gloss

Event work is **temporary**. It isn't a permanent job with office hours: you're hired per event, with set dates and hours.

Some things worth being clear about from the start:

- **The hours are the event's hours**, not yours. Very often that means weekends, public holidays and nights.
- **Shifts can be long**, and in most roles you're on your feet.
- **Setup and teardown are real.** A good part of the work happens before the public arrives and after they leave, sometimes in the early hours.
- **Demand isn't steady.** There are weeks with several events and weeks with none.
- **Punctuality is almost everything.** At an event there's no margin: if the doors open at 8 pm, someone has to be there at 8 pm.

None of this is meant to put you off. It's so you can decide with the real picture: plenty of people make this their main income and it works for them, but it works because they know what it's like.

## How you join the pool

On LABURO you join by filling in the **public form**. It takes a few minutes:

1. **Your details and your CV.** You can upload your CV to go faster: if it reads well, it fills in a good part of the form and you check and correct it. If something doesn't come through, you enter it by hand and that's it.
2. **The trades you can do.** You choose from a specific list: bartender, barback, waiter, runner, access control, registration staff, scanner operator, setup and teardown, sound technician, lighting, promoter, hostess, cashier, cleaning, driver and many more.
3. **Your experience.** At which events, in which roles, with which brands you've worked. Be specific here: it's what a producer looks at.
4. **Your availability and your area.** Weekends, nights, how far you can travel.

**Adding your profile is free.** Being in the pool costs nothing.

A practical tip: vague profiles don't get chosen. "I have hospitality experience" loses to "two seasons on the bar at events of 500 to 2,000 people". You don't need to make anything up, just write what you've done precisely. More on that in [what to include in your event staff profile](/en/blog/event-staff-profile-what-to-include).

## How work arrives

When an event needs your role, in your area and in your availability slot, **you get an offer by email**.

That offer includes, before you accept: **the role, the dates, the hours and how much it pays**. There's nothing to find out afterwards or negotiate on WhatsApp: it's all written there.

To respond **you don't need to create an account or a password**. The email has its own link: you open it, check the conditions and accept or decline right there. If you accept, you're confirmed for that event and it's recorded.

**The link expires.** It's best to answer quickly: an expired offer can no longer be accepted, and the place is offered to someone else. Declining doesn't count against you; not answering is what makes offers stop coming.

## On the day of the event

You arrive at the call time. At events where it's enabled, **you clock in and out from the staff dashboard**: that way your real hours are recorded, without depending on anyone's memory. That's what later backs up your payment. For the rest of the day, read [your first day working at an event](/en/blog/first-day-working-at-an-event).

That's what the policy says, and it's the same thing you'll see written in the offer before accepting. The amount is in the offer too: it isn't decided afterwards or adjusted on the fly.

If something doesn't match (the hours you clocked, the amount, the dates), it gets raised with the information in plain view, which is exactly what having everything recorded is for.

## What we won't promise you

We'd rather say it up front:

- **Being in the pool doesn't guarantee you'll be called.** It depends on there being events that need your role, in your area, at your availability.
- **It isn't a permanent job.** When you accept an offer, you're confirmed **for that event**.
- **There's no guaranteed number of events** per month.

What is true, and it's the only thing you really control: **the people who arrive on time, do their job well and give notice when they can't make it are the ones who get called again.** In a trade where most problems come from people who don't show up, being reliable is the best card you have.

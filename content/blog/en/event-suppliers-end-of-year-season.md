November and December are the months when the difference between an organised supplier and one running around in a rush shows most. Company end-of-year parties, end-of-term events, social events and all the commitments everyone wants to fit in "before the holidays" pile up at once. This guide is for anyone who provides sound, catering, structures, photo and video, or any other event service, and wants to reach the season with the diary, the team and the paperwork ready. There are no figures on how much demand grows because we don't have a serious source to give them: what follows is method.

## The diary: one only, with clear statuses

The typical year-end problem isn't a lack of work: it's promising the same date twice. To avoid it:

- **A single diary,** shared with anyone else who takes bookings (partner, manager, whoever answers the phone). Two diaries are two versions of the truth.
- **Three statuses:** *enquired* (they asked for a price), *reserved* (what you require to hold the date has been met, for example a deposit) and *confirmed* (everything closed). Only the last two block the date.
- **Expiry dates.** Every quote has to say how many days it's valid. If it expires, the date is free again and you know it, you don't guess it.
- **Real capacity per day.** If you have one complete sound rig, you can do as many events per day as you have rigs and people, not as many as you're asked for. Write down how many you can do at once and don't go over it.
- **Travel and pack-down time.** Two events on the same day only fit if packing down the first and travelling to the second fit within the schedule.

## The backup crew, before you need it

In peak season, what fails isn't the equipment: it's people. Someone gets ill, someone took another date, someone doesn't turn up.

- **Build your backup list in October,** with the name, role, area and availability of each person for the busy dates.
- **Confirm each shift in writing** with arrival time, finish time and agreed pay.
- **Have a replacement in mind for each key position,** not for all of them: the lead technician, the head chef, the person who handles the structure.
- **Check the physical equipment** before the first date: cables, lights, tableware, marquees, batteries and memory cards. What breaks in December gets repaired in January.

## Conditions, written before you quote

In peak season every date is worth more, and a late cancellation leaves you with an empty date. That's why it's worth having a base text of conditions, which you adapt but don't reinvent each time:

- **What the price includes and doesn't include:** travel, setup, hours, staff, consumables.
- **How long the quote is valid,** in days.
- **Payment method and timing:** how much to reserve, how much before the event, how much after.
- **Cancellation and date change:** what happens in each case and with how much notice.
- **Setup and pack-down times,** and what happens if the event runs over.
- **What you need from the venue:** power, loading access, storage space, kitchen.

## Your directory profile, up to date

If you're listed on LABURO, before November review your profile as if a client in a hurry were looking at it:

- **One service for each thing you do,** with the right category. The services directory search filters service by service.
- **Provinces selected in each service.** Without a province, that service doesn't appear in searches.
- **The starting price, if you show one, up to date.** It's optional and only a guide, but if it's out of date it brings enquiries that don't close.
- **Your enquiry form.** From the block for how you want to be contacted, you can add the questions you can't quote without (for example, whether it's outdoors or how many covers). That way the request arrives complete and you reply faster.
- **Your Instagram or website** with recent work.

Enquiries from your profile reach you by email and you also see them in your dashboard, under your enquiries. And if a production company invites you to a quote request, you get your own link to enter your price line by line, with currency, what's included, what isn't and how many days it's valid, without creating an account. What to fill in on each part of the profile is in [event supplier profile: get quote requests](/en/blog/event-supplier-profile-get-quote-requests).

## By category, what's most worth checking

- **Sound and lighting:** what power you need from the venue, what happens if it rains at an outdoor event and how long your real setup takes.
- **Catering:** maximum covers per day with your kitchen and staff, the dietary restrictions you can cater for and your team's food handler certificates being current. On that, see [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work).
- **Structures and marquees:** how many you can put up at once, what surface you need and what happens with wind.
- **Photo and video:** how many jobs you can cover per day with your equipment, how long you take to deliver and what backup you have for the files.

## After December

Write down which dates you turned down for lack of capacity, which backups worked and which conditions you were missing. That's the material for preparing next season and for deciding whether to add equipment or people.

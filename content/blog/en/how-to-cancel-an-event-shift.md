It happens: you accepted an event two weeks ago and now you're ill, an exam got moved or a family problem came up. Pulling out of an event isn't ideal, but there is a way to do it that looks after whoever hired you and looks after you for next time. This guide is that way, and at the end it explains how to decline a LABURO offer in time if you haven't accepted it yet, which is the best way to avoid having to pull out.

## When it happens

The most common reasons are three:

- **Something you couldn't foresee**: an illness, a family problem, a transport issue.
- **A clash**: you accepted two things that ended up overlapping, or your study timetable or another job changed.
- **Something that wasn't clear when you accepted**: the real hours, the venue or the journey home turned out different from what you thought.

The third is almost always avoided by asking beforehand. If the event finishes late, for example, sort out how you're getting home before you accept, as the guide on [getting home after a late-night event](/en/blog/getting-home-after-a-late-night-event) explains.

## Tell them as soon as possible

Every hour between knowing you can't go and telling them is one hour less for whoever hired you to cover your place. Notice given days ahead gets solved with another call-out; notice two hours before the event can leave a position empty. From the other side, [what to do when event staff don't show up](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do) shows how much it costs to cover a last-minute gap.

Don't wait until you're completely sure if you already know you probably can't make it: tell them there's a risk and confirm as soon as you know.

## Through the agreed channel

Use the channel they coordinated with you on: if you spoke on WhatsApp, use WhatsApp; if they emailed you, email. If it's very close to the event, call as well as sending the message. A message nobody reads in time is almost the same as not telling them.

On LABURO, an accepted offer is confirmed and **there is no button to pull out from the app**. You tell whoever hired you directly. When you accept, the confirmation screen offers you a WhatsApp button to coordinate the details: it's a good channel to use if you later can't make it, too.

## What to say

Short and clear. Three things are enough:

1. **That you won't be able to go**, with the name of the event and the date, so there's no doubt which one it is.
2. **The reason, in one line.** You don't need to share details of your life; just make it clear it isn't a whim.
3. **What you can offer**: if they ask, someone to cover you, or your availability for another date.

Something like: "Hi, I'm writing about Saturday's event. A family problem has come up and I won't be able to go. I'm telling you now so you have time to cover it. If it helps, I know someone who has done the same role and I can pass on their contact."

## Suggesting a replacement, if asked

Some producers appreciate you suggesting someone and others prefer to choose themselves. Offer it, don't impose it: don't send another person to the event without whoever is hiring agreeing, because that person isn't on their list, doesn't have the offer and doesn't know the conditions. If they say yes, pass on the contact and let them coordinate directly.

## What not to do

- **Disappear.** Not telling them and not showing up is the worst thing you can do: it leaves a position empty without warning and breaks trust in one go.
- **Tell them through a channel nobody checks**, or leave the message with a colleague to pass on.
- **Pull out to go to another event that pays more** without saying so. It happens, and in a trade where people know each other, it gets around.
- **Promise you'll go and not show up.** If you're not sure, say so.

## How it affects the next call

Whoever hires remembers two things: whether you told them and how much notice you gave. Notice in time, with a solution offered, is usually understood; what counts against you is doing it repeatedly or doing it badly. The guide on [how to get regular event work](/en/blog/how-to-get-regular-event-work) explains why answering fast and delivering weigh so much when a producer chooses who to call again.

## The best way not to pull out: decline in time

Many cancellations are avoided by saying no earlier. On LABURO, each offer comes with the role, the event, the date, the venue, the pay and the conditions. If something doesn't work for you, **decline it**:

- **From the link** of the offer you received by email: it has Accept and Decline buttons.
- **From your staff dashboard**, if you already have an account: pending offers show up there with the same two buttons.

Declining frees the place so whoever is hiring can call someone else. If you declined by mistake, the offer can't be accepted again from the same link: the screen offers you a WhatsApp button to let them know, and it's best to do it straight away.

Offers also **expire**: they have a deadline and, after it, the link stops working. If you can't make it, don't let it expire in silence: decline it, which is a clear answer for whoever is building the team.

Before accepting, check that the hours, the venue and the journey home work for you.

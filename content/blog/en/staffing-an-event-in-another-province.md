Producing an event far from your base raises a question that never comes up in your own city: do you bring your people or build the team there? This guide helps you decide post by post, explains how to search for staff by province and city, what to make clear about expenses and accommodation, and how to coordinate a team you haven't met in person.

Argentina is a big country divided into provinces, and LABURO, which operates there, is often used to staff events far from the producer's home city.

## Local team or people who travel

It's not an all-or-nothing decision. What usually works is **splitting the team in two**:

- **What travels.** Overall coordination, area leads and the positions that need to know your way of working or the brand inside out. They're few people and they carry the method.
- **What's hired there.** Guest service, promo staff, bar, waiters, access, setup and teardown. These are positions that can be learned with a good brief and where knowing the city helps: how to get there, where to park, how to get home in the early hours.

Bringing the whole team has advantages (they know each other, they already know how you work), but it multiplies travel, accommodation and meals, and adds hours on the road before the first task. Hiring everyone there saves that, but leaves you with nobody who knows the plan. The middle ground is to send the people who lead and build the team that executes with local people.

If it's your first time in the city, at least one person from your team should arrive early. To know whether that role is needed, see [when you need an event staff coordinator](/en/blog/when-you-need-an-event-staff-coordinator).

## How to search for staff by province and city

On LABURO the team builds the staff by filtering the pool by role, province and city, and each profile shows where the person lives and their experience. A few recommendations:

1. **Start earlier than you would in your own city.** A provincial capital has more people than a small town, and you don't know in advance how many profiles you'll find for each role.
2. **Look at nearby towns.** If the event is in a small town, there may be people a short distance away. Ask in the offer how they'd get there.
3. **Read the profile before making an offer.** What to look at is in [what to check before hiring event staff](/en/blog/what-to-check-before-hiring-event-staff).
4. **Line up stand-ins from the start.** From a distance you can't go out and find someone at the last minute. What to do if it happens is in [event staff no-show: what to do](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do).

## Expenses and accommodation, spelled out

For people who travel, the offer has to answer everything before they accept. No made-up amounts, but every point defined:

- **Travel.** Who pays, by what means and at what time. If it's reimbursed, against what receipt.
- **Accommodation.** Who books it, where, whether the room is shared or not, and how many nights.
- **Meals.** Whether they're covered at the event, whether there's a daily allowance or whether each person pays their own.
- **Travel hours.** Whether the journey is paid as a full day, as part of the working day or not at all. All three are possible; what you can't do is not say.
- **When people get paid.** Payment for the work and for expenses, separately if they go through different channels.

Local people also need something spelled out: if the venue is far from the centre, how they get there and back. What to plan for the trip home is in [getting home after a late-night event](/en/blog/getting-home-after-a-late-night-event). And to organise everyone's payments, see [how to organise event staff payments](/en/blog/how-to-organise-event-staff-payments).

## Coordinating from a distance

With a team you haven't met in person, whatever isn't written down doesn't exist.

- **A brief that makes sense without you.** Times, exact address, staff entrance, dress code, what each post does and who it reports to. The model in [how to write a brand activation brief for promo staff](/en/blog/brand-activation-brief-for-promo-staff) works for any role.
- **A single lead on site.** The person everyone calls. If you travel, it's you; if not, someone with the authority to decide.
- **One message group per event.** On LABURO you have each person's WhatsApp from their profile and from the offer, so the contact is at hand.
- **Venue rules in writing.** If it's a hotel or an events site, ask for them beforehand. In a hotel, for example, the events area has its own guidelines: they're in [staffing a hotel event](/en/blog/staffing-a-hotel-event).

## The day before

It's the day that matters most when the event is far away:

1. **Confirm one by one.** A message to each person with the time and address, and wait for a reply. If someone doesn't answer, bring in the stand-in.
2. **Walk the venue** with the local lead, if you're already in the city. Access, staff toilets, where things are stored.
3. **Check that the materials have arrived.** Uniforms, badges, product, signage.
4. **Go over the close.** Finishing time, teardown and how everyone gets back.

## Common mistakes

- **Bringing the whole team out of habit** when half the posts could have been filled there.
- **Expenses agreed by word of mouth.** The complaint comes up on the trip home.
- **Looking for local staff with the same lead time as in your own city.**
- **No stand-ins.** From a distance, a dropout with no replacement is an empty post.

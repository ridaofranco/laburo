From the organiser's side we've already written [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers): complete details, the same request to everyone, a price broken down and a deadline. This guide is the other half, for whoever receives that request: how to reply so it can be compared and so that, if your price and your service work, you're the one chosen.

## First, read the whole request

It sounds obvious and it's the most common mistake: replying to the email with a price without reading the details, so the other side has to ask again what was already written. Before quoting, check:

- What type of event it is, the date, the venue and how many people.
- What exactly they're asking for, and whether there are separate lines.
- What questions they're asking besides the price.
- **The deadline.** If the request has one, no more quotes are accepted after that date.

If something is missing and it changes your number, ask. But ask everything together, in a single message.

## When to reply

There's no universal deadline, but there are two rules:

1. **Before the deadline, always.** A quote that arrives late doesn't make it into the comparison.
2. **As soon as possible.** Organisers are usually asking several suppliers in the same trade for a price, and they often build the proposal for their client with the first complete one they have.

If you can't quote straight away (because you need to get a price for some equipment or confirm staff availability), reply anyway: "Received, I'll send you the quote on Thursday, I just need to confirm X". That's worth more than silence.

## What to ask before quoting

The useful questions are the ones that change your price or your ability to do the job. Some come up in almost every trade:

- **Setup and teardown times.** From what time you can get in and by what time you have to be out. A setup in the early hours doesn't cost the same.
- **Loading access.** Whether there's a ramp or a lift, where the vehicle parks and how far you have to carry the equipment.
- **Power.** How much power is available at the venue or whether a generator is needed.
- **Kitchen.** If you're a caterer: whether the venue has a kitchen or you have to work with off-site production and transport.
- **Layout.** 300 people standing is not the same as 300 at a banquet, for catering, sound or security.
- **Outdoors or indoors**, and what happens if it rains.

Ask, don't exclude. If you can't deliver part of the request, say so in the quote and price the rest.

## What to include in the quote

An event quote that can be compared has, at a minimum:

- **The price, with its currency.** Say whether it's local currency or dollars. If you mix currencies, make clear which part is in each.
- **What's included.** Equipment, staff, hours, transport, setup. What's inside the number.
- **What isn't included.** Freight, travel expenses, overtime, waiting time if there are delays. What's charged separately is what causes most arguments later.
- **Until when that price is valid**, and whether you update it if the event moves date. When prices change often, a number without a validity date is no use for deciding.
- **Payment method and deposit** to reserve the date.
- **A broken-down price if they ask for it in parts.** A closed package doesn't let anyone cut things: if the client drops a part, everything has to be re-quoted.

## What to avoid

- **"It depends" with no number.** If the price depends on something, give a number with the assumption written down ("with setup from 2 pm") and say how much it changes if the assumption doesn't hold.
- **Answering what they didn't ask.** If they ask for sound for a conference, don't send your whole lighting catalogue.
- **A price on WhatsApp with no details.** If the request came through a form or a quote link, reply there: that's what the organiser is going to compare.
- **Promising what you don't have.** Capacity, equipment or paperwork you can't show if asked. It falls apart at the site visit or on the day of the event.

## How requests reach you on LABURO

There are two ways, and each one is answered differently.

**The inquiry from your listing.** Someone visits your listing in the LABURO supplier or venue directory and fills in your inquiry form. It reaches you by email, already complete, and you also see it in your dashboard, under your inquiries. You reply directly to that person, with your quote and your terms. If inquiries always arrive missing the same detail, add that question to your form from your inquiry settings.

**The quote request.** A production company builds a request and invites you by email: you get your own link to enter your quote, without creating an account. On that screen you see when the event is, until when you can submit, and:

- **What they're asking for, line by line**, if the request has parts. You enter a price per line or mark it **"I don't do this"**, which is different from leaving it blank. Your total adds up automatically.
- **Currency**, pesos or dollars.
- **What's included** and **what isn't**.
- **How many days this price is valid.**
- **What they're asking you:** the trade questions the production company chose, plus the standard ones (price broken down, validity and payment method).

Until the deadline you can correct and save changes. After that you can no longer submit or correct anything, and when they decide you're notified by email, whether yours was chosen or not.

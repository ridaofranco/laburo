A large kids' event (a birthday party with lots of guests, a family day for a brand, a school or club end-of-year party) has one rule that organises everything else: **every child who comes in has to leave with the right person.** Entertainment, food and decoration matter, but the staff are built around that rule. This guide covers the roles, what to decide beforehand and how to choose the people.

If the event is a quinceañera party, the guests are teenagers and the set-up is different: it's in [staffing a quinceañera party](/en/blog/staffing-a-quinceanera-party).

## First: do the adults stay or leave?

Before counting people, settle one of two things with the organiser:

1. **Adults stay.** Staff entertain, serve and keep order, and each family keeps looking after its own children. Reception controls entry, but pickup is simpler.
2. **Adults drop the children off and come back for them.** Staff are in charge for the whole event. Reception, pickup and watching the space become the core of the job.

Many brand events mix the two: the adult stays in one area and the child goes to a workshop or a game. In that case, every area where the child is without their adult works like the second model.

## Reception with pickup control

It's the most important post and it's best not to add other tasks to it.

- **A list prepared beforehand.** The child's name, the adult bringing them, the adult collecting them and a phone number. If it's an open event, the list is made at the entrance, on the spot.
- **An ID for each child.** A wristband or sticker with a number that matches the list. The adult keeps the same number, as in a cloakroom: the logic is the same as explained in [how to run an event cloakroom](/en/blog/how-to-run-an-event-cloakroom).
- **Pickup only against the list.** At the end, the child is handed to the listed person and the time is noted. If someone else comes, reception doesn't decide: they call the family's or the organiser's contact. That rule goes in writing in the offer for the post, so nobody argues about it at the door.
- **A single exit.** If there are several, all but one are closed or staffed by someone from the team.

## Entertainment

Entertainment keeps the event's rhythm going. It can come from two places:

- **An entertainment supplier** who brings their show, team and sound. On LABURO you'll find them in the services directory, in the entertainment category, and you ask them for a quote through a form.
- **Individual entertainers** you add to the staff for games, workshops or to support a supplier's show. In LABURO's catalogue the role is Entertainer.

What the entertainment team needs to know: ages, number of children, duration, available space and the full schedule (when food is served, when the cake is cut, when pickup starts).

## A safe space

This isn't about private security but about looking at the venue through a child's eyes:

- **A walk-through beforehand** with the venue: stairs, pools, doors onto the street, cables, glass, tables with sharp edges.
- **Areas closed off** or staffed by someone from the team: kitchen, storeroom, supplier exit, pool.
- **One person watching the space** who isn't entertaining or serving. Their job is to count heads, see who went where and raise the alarm.
- **What to do if a child goes missing.** Decided beforehand: who raises the alarm, who closes the exit and who searches each area.

If the event is outdoors or at a country house, there's more to check in [event venue with a pool in summer](/en/blog/event-venue-with-pool-summer-season).

## Toilets

At a kids' event the toilets get a lot of use, often with help. Decide:

- **Who goes with** the youngest children, if the adults aren't there. The neatest approach is for two staff members to go together, or for the child to go with another child while a team member waits at the door.
- **Cleaning and restocking** throughout the event, not only at the end. The detail is in [cleaning staff for an event](/en/blog/cleaning-staff-for-an-event).

## Food and drink

- **Ask about allergies** and dietary restrictions beforehand, and have them marked on the reception list.
- **Alcohol-free drinks in the children's area.** If there's a bar for adults, it goes separately.

## How to choose staff who work with children

This point needs more care than any other. What to do:

- **Look at previous experience with children** in the profile and ask about it before sending the offer.
- **Talk to the person beforehand.** Explain the post in detail, the pickup rules and what to do if something goes wrong. If they hesitate over a rule, better to know beforehand.
- **Nobody alone with a child** out of sight of the rest of the team. That rule protects the children and the staff too.
- **Phones put away** while in charge, and no photos of the children unless the organiser has authorised it and there's a photographer for that.
- **Call back the people who did well.** On LABURO you can save that person to your favourites with a note, for next time. How to evaluate is in [how to evaluate event staff](/en/blog/how-to-evaluate-event-staff).

If the organiser has its own requirements (a clearance, a certificate, a training course), they go in writing in the offer so the person knows before accepting.

## Common mistakes

- **Not deciding whether adults stay** until the day of the event.
- **Reception that also hands out food or entertains.** When pickup comes, nobody is watching the list.
- **Handing over a child "because they knew them".** The rule is the list, always.
- **Closing the staff shift with the last game.** Pickup is the end of the job, and reception is the last post to leave.

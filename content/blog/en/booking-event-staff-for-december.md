If you have a single event in December, the challenge is getting there in time. If you have five, eight or twelve, the challenge is different: stopping them from clashing, making sure key people aren't handed out at random and having a planned replacement for every dropout. This guide is for production companies and agencies heading into the end-of-year season with several dates. If yours is a single company party, the step by step is in [staffing a company end-of-year party](/en/blog/staffing-a-company-end-of-year-party).

In Argentina, where LABURO operates, the end-of-year season runs through November and December and rolls straight into summer.

## Why you plan the whole season, not event by event

The rest of the year, each event more or less comes together on its own. In November and December that stops working, for a calendar reason: many events fall on a few nights, and the pool of experienced people doesn't multiply. A good bartender works one bar a night. If they get three offers for the same Friday, they pick one.

When each event is staffed separately, the usual problems appear: the same person called for two places at the same time, the best coordinator assigned to the smallest event, and nobody in reserve for the busiest night.

## Step 1: the season map

Before you send a single offer, put all your events in one place, with date, hours, venue and headcount by role. With that in front of you, you'll see three things:

- **Overlapping nights.** That's where your people will clash.
- **Back-to-back nights.** Someone who works a close in the early hours won't arrive fresh for an early setup the next day. Count it as two shifts that can't be doubled. The logic of rest breaks is in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).
- **The peak of the season.** The night or week with the most positions to fill. That's what determines how many people you need confirmed, not the average.

On LABURO, the **Calendar** shows your events for the month with how many confirmed staff each has, and the **Events** board shows, per event, how many spots are covered and how many are still open according to the headcount you entered.

## Step 2: the public holidays that shape the season

According to Argentina's official public holiday calendar on argentina.gob.ar:

| Date | What it is | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday 9 November | Visit of Pope Leo XIV | Fixed public holiday |
| Tuesday 10 November | Visit of Pope Leo XIV | Holiday in Córdoba and Buenos Aires City |
| Wednesday 11 November | Visit of Pope Leo XIV | Holiday in Buenos Aires Province |
| Monday 23 November | National Sovereignty Day (20/11) | Movable public holiday |
| Monday 7 December | Non-working day for tourism purposes | Tourism day |
| Tuesday 8 December | Immaculate Conception | Fixed public holiday |
| Friday 25 December | Christmas | Fixed public holiday |
| Friday 1 January 2027 | New Year's Day | Fixed public holiday |

What to watch for staffing: **from Saturday 7 to Wednesday 11 November** there's a long weekend, with extra days depending on the province, and **the weekend of 5 to 8 December** becomes a long one, when many people who work at events also go away or prefer not to work. **Christmas and New Year fall on Fridays**, so Thursday 24 and Thursday 31 are nights with less availability. Check the official source before locking anything in, because the calendar can be changed by regulation.

## Step 3: key people first

Not every position is equally hard to fill. Order your offers by what's hardest to replace:

1. **Coordination.** If you have several events on the same night, each needs someone in charge of the team. That's the first thing to secure. When you need one and what they do is in [when you need an event staff coordinator](/en/blog/when-you-need-an-event-staff-coordinator).
2. **Specific profiles.** Experienced bar staff, technical crew, interpreters, registration staff who can run the event's system.
3. **Positions with more people available.** Service, support, setup. They're closed later, but with the headcount already written down.

Start with your **Favourites**: people who have already worked well with you are the ones to secure first, and the ones most likely to be getting offers from others. If you rate and take notes after every event, that list builds itself (how to do it is in [how to evaluate event staff](/en/blog/how-to-evaluate-event-staff)).

## Step 4: calling and confirming

**When to call people:** as soon as an event has a date, venue and headcount. There's no number of weeks that works for everyone; what always holds is that in peak season, getting there first with a clear offer counts for more than at any other time of year.

**What counts as confirmed:** only an accepted offer. On LABURO each offer carries the role, the event, the date, the pay and the conditions, and the person accepts or declines it from the link. You see the status of each one: sent, seen, accepted, declined or expired. Offers expire, and anyone with one about to expire gets a reminder. An expired offer isn't a pending yes: it's an open spot.

**So you don't double-book anyone:** when searching, turn on the option to hide people already assigned to one of your events. It saves you sending a second offer to someone who is already working with you that night.

## Step 5: replacements, before you need them

In December a dropout hurts more, because replacing someone at the last minute is harder than in March. Three things to set up in advance:

- **A short list of stand-ins per role**, people who have already worked with you and know they may be called.
- **A buffer in the critical positions** on the busiest night: door, bar and service.
- **Filters ready for a fast search:** by role, province and city, weekend availability, own transport and willingness to travel.

If the dropout has already happened, the step by step is in [event staff no-show: what to do](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do).

## After December comes January

In many places the season doesn't end on the 31st: it carries on into summer. If your events continue in January, the same map works, and the people you rated well in December are your starting point.

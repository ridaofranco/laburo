Event work is one of the few jobs that fits around a course timetable: you are paid by the date, it is often at night or at the weekend, and it doesn't tie you to fixed Monday to Friday hours. But it also has an obvious risk: taking on too much and turning up to a mid-term without sleep. This guide is so you combine the two well.

## Why events fit with university

- **You work by the event, not by the month.** You can take one date and not the next. There is no weekly commitment to clash with your classes.
- **The hours usually fall outside class time.** Parties, weddings and concerts are at night or at weekends. Conferences and trade shows, on the other hand, are daytime and midweek: they work if you study in the evening or during the holidays.
- **You gain experience in customer service, organisation and teamwork,** which is useful on any CV later.

If it is your first time, start with [event jobs with no experience](/en/blog/event-jobs-with-no-experience) and then come back here to plan your calendar.

## Plan your calendar before accepting offers

The most common mistake is saying yes to everything in the first few weeks and having no margin left when exams arrive. Before filling in your profile, look at the whole term:

1. **Mark the blocked dates:** mid-terms, finals, deadlines and the week before each one.
2. **Mark the free windows:** weekends with no exams, the winter break, the summer holidays.
3. **Set your own limit.** For example, how many events a month you can take without your studies slipping. You decide that number based on your degree and your pace; there isn't one that works for everyone.

With that clear, every offer gets a quick answer: if it falls on a blocked date, no.

## Seasons: where the work is concentrated

Some times of year have more events and others are quieter. In Argentina, December is usually high season because of company end-of-year parties (we cover it in [booking event staff for December](/en/blog/booking-event-staff-for-december)), and the summer, from December to February, brings work in tourist areas, as explained in [summer event jobs on the coast](/en/blog/summer-event-jobs-on-the-coast).

What helps: if you have finals in December, decide beforehand what you'll prioritise. And if you are free in summer, it is a good time to add dates and experience.

## How to mark your real availability on your profile

On your LABURO profile, in the experience and availability section, there are specific options:

- **Available at weekends.**
- **Available to travel.**
- **Own transport.**
- **How much notice you need to take a job:** available immediately, with 24 to 48 hours' notice, with a week's notice or to be arranged.

You also choose **where you want to work** and **the roles you are applying for.** There is just one rule: **mark what you can deliver, not what sounds better.** If you have classes on Saturday mornings, don't tick weekends without explaining it in your experience. If you live far away and rely on public transport, don't mark own transport. The team is built by role and area from a pool of more than 1,200 applicants, based on what your profile says: if you then can't make it, it is a problem for both sides.

For the rest of the profile (photo, experience, CV), see [what to include in your event staff profile](/en/blog/event-staff-profile-what-to-include).

## Which roles tend to suit you

There are no roles "for students", but some suit people who are starting out and have changing hours better:

- **Registration and reception.** Quick to learn and very common at conferences and corporate events. If you want to see what's involved, read [what conference registration staff do](/en/blog/conference-registration-staff-role).
- **Promo work and sampling.** Short days or work in blocks, at brand activations. More detail in [how to build a sampling team](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-sampling-team).
- **Runner.** Production support, lots of moving around and a good school for understanding how an event is put together. See [what an event runner does](/en/blog/what-does-an-event-runner-do).
- **Bar and floor.** If you already have some hospitality experience, see [event waiter jobs](/en/blog/event-waiter-jobs).

A good rule of thumb: if you study languages, tourism, communications, design or hospitality, look for roles where that gets used. The experience works for you twice.

## Exams: how to say no without burning bridges

On LABURO, offers arrive with the pay and hours, and you accept or decline them from your dashboard. Declining an offer you can't deliver is fine. What damages your reputation is accepting and not turning up, or giving notice at the last minute.

- **Reply quickly,** even if it is a no.
- **If you have accepted and an exam comes up that you didn't expect,** let them know as soon as you find out, not the day before.
- **Before finals season, update your availability** on your profile, for example by switching to "to be arranged".

Delivering what you accept is what gets you called again. We go into it in [how to get regular event work](/en/blog/how-to-get-regular-event-work).

## Protect your rest

A night event can finish late and the teardown even later. If you have classes the next day:

- **Check the finishing time before accepting,** not just the start time. Ask whether it includes teardown.
- **Work out the journey home:** how you'll get back and what time you'll arrive.
- **Don't string** a long event together with an exam first thing in the morning.
- **Prepare the day as if it were your first:** hours, what to bring and how to get there are in [first day working at an event](/en/blog/first-day-working-at-an-event).

If you start taking dates regularly, it is also worth looking at how to invoice; the name and requirements depend on your country, and in Argentina it is covered in [monotributo for event workers in Argentina](/en/blog/monotributo-for-event-workers-argentina).

## If you are under 18 (Argentina)

In Argentina, Law 26,390 bans work by people under 16. Between 16 and 18 you can work with permission from your parents or guardians, for a maximum of 6 hours a day or 36 a week, and **night work, between 8 pm and 6 am, is not allowed.** Since many events are precisely at night, most calls ask for people who are of age. If you have finished secondary school and are already 18, that limit doesn't apply to you.

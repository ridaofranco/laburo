Entertaining children at a neighbourhood birthday party and entertaining at a large children's event have things in common, but they are not the same. At an event with a big audience, a brand family day or a school or club end-of-year party, you are part of a team with rules, a schedule and people in charge. This guide is for anyone who wants to work as a kids' party entertainer at events: what you'll be asked for, what the day looks like and what to put in your profile so you get called.

## What the entertainer does at a children's event

The entertainer keeps the pace going. That includes:

- **Games, workshops or activities** designed for the age of the children attending. A group of four-year-olds is not the same as a group of ten-year-olds.
- **Supporting a supplier's show**, if the event has one: going in first to gather the group, holding their attention during changeovers and organising the exit.
- **Marking the moments on the schedule**: food, the cake, the group photo, the close. Whether the children reach each moment without chaos depends a lot on you.
- **Watching the group the whole time.** While you entertain, you count heads. If a child wanders off, cries or heads towards a closed area, you report it.

What isn't yours: reception and pick-up control. They are separate roles, and it is better that they stay that way. If you are asked to do both at once, ask who is watching the list while you are with the group.

## What's expected

**Experience with children.** It is the first thing the person building the team looks at. You don't need to have worked big events, but you do need to have been in charge of groups: birthdays, holiday camps, workshops, Sunday school, clubs, schools.

**Energy that lasts.** A children's event can have three or four hours of entertainment. Someone who starts at full speed and is spent after an hour stands out. Pace yourself: alternate lively games with calmer ones.

**Voice and presence.** People should hear you without you shouting all the time. If there is a microphone, ask beforehand who handles it and whether there is a sound person.

**A plan B.** If a game doesn't take off, you need to be able to change it on the spot. Bring more activities than you think you'll use.

**Following rules even under pressure.** At an event with children there are rules that are not up for debate, and whoever hires you wants to know you'll stick to them. The most common:

- **Never alone with a child** out of sight of the rest of the team. That rule protects the children and protects you too.
- **Toilet trips in pairs**, or with someone from the team at the door, depending on what has been agreed.
- **Phone put away** while you are in charge, and **no photos** of the children unless the organiser has authorised it.
- **Pick-up belongs to reception.** If an adult asks to take a child with them, you send them to reception, even if you know them.
- **Allergies.** If you are running a moment with food, ask beforehand whether any children have restrictions.

## What the day looks like

1. **Arrive early.** Before the children. Walk the space with your supervisor: where you'll be entertaining, where the toilets are, which areas are closed off (kitchen, pool, storeroom, street exit).
2. **Setup.** Materials, music if you use it, the play area. If you bring your own things, they should be in the offer.
3. **Go over the schedule.** When food is served, when the cake is cut, when there is a show, when pick-up starts. Also ask what to do if a child goes missing: who raises the alarm and with whom.
4. **Welcoming the children.** As they arrive, a welcome game that doesn't need everyone to be there.
5. **Entertainment in blocks.** Following the schedule, with breaks for food and the show.
6. **Close and pick-up.** The last game has to be one you can stop at any moment, because children leave one by one. You stay with the group until the last one is collected or until your supervisor releases you.
7. **Tidy up.** Pack your things and leave the space as you were asked.

How the rest of a children's event team is put together (reception, pick-up control, toilets, food) is in [staffing a kids' event](/en/blog/staffing-a-kids-event). Reading it helps you understand what the person hiring you expects.

## Before you accept an offer

Make sure these are clear:

- **The children's ages and how many there are.**
- **Duration and full hours**, including arrival for setup and the end of pick-up.
- **Whether the parents stay or leave.** It changes how much responsibility you carry.
- **What you need to bring** (materials, clothing, face paint, sound) and what the event provides.
- **The pay** and how it is paid.

If any of that is missing, ask before accepting. If you have already accepted and can't go, let them know as soon as possible: [how to cancel an event shift](/en/blog/how-to-cancel-an-event-shift) explains how to do it without burning bridges.

## What to prepare in your LABURO profile

1. **Upload your CV.** A good part of the form fills itself in; then you check and correct it.
2. **Mark entertainer.** If you also do these, add host or promoter: you can mark several roles.
3. **Describe your experience in the free-text field.** Which ages you have worked with, at what kind of events, with groups of how many children and what you can do (games, face painting, balloon modelling, magic, working a microphone). It is the field that carries the most weight when someone opens your profile.
4. **Mark yourself as available at weekends** if you can: most children's events fall on a Saturday or Sunday.
5. **Choose how much notice you need** to take a job, and your area.
6. **Add a portfolio link** if you have videos or photos of your work in which no child's face can be seen.

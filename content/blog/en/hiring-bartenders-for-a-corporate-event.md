At a company event, the bar is the place where guests queue without anyone asking them to. If it gets stuck, it's the first thing people talk about. This guide is for whoever is hiring (production companies, agencies, marketing or HR departments): what profile to ask for, how to work out how many bars and bartenders, what to ask beforehand and what each side has to provide.

## First: individual bartenders or an outsourced bar?

There are two routes, and it's worth choosing before you look for people:

- **Outsourced bar:** a supplier arrives with the bar, drinks, glassware and their own team. You set the menu and the hours.
- **Bartenders you hire yourself:** you (or the venue or caterer) sort out drinks, ice and equipment, and you hire the people who serve.

Both work as long as it's written down who provides what. If you go with a supplier, [how to request quotes from event suppliers](/en/blog/how-to-request-quotes-from-event-suppliers) explains how to compare. The rest of this guide is for when you build the team yourself.

## What profile to ask for

Don't ask for "bar people". Ask for separate roles:

- **Bartender:** prepares and serves. Check on their profile what kind of bar they've worked: volume, cocktails, large events. A bartender from a craft cocktail bar and one from an 800-person party do different jobs.
- **Barback:** restocks, cuts, brings ice, washes and tidies so the bartender doesn't leave their station. It's the most forgotten role and the one that keeps the pace going.

For a corporate event, also look for:

- **People skills.** The bartender talks to almost everyone. They should know how to wrap up a long chat without being rude.
- **Judgement with alcohol.** At a company event some guests drink too much and the bar is the first to notice. Decide beforehand who they tell.
- **Experience setting up in someone else's venue:** mobile bars and restocking from far away.

More general criteria in [what to check before hiring event staff](/en/blog/what-to-check-before-hiring-event-staff).

## How many bars and bartenders: the calculation by pace

There's no number of bartenders per hundred guests that works for every event. It depends on the menu, the format and how many people reach the bar at the same time. What there is, is a way to calculate it:

1. **Find the peak.** At a corporate event it's usually when the reception opens, after the welcome speeches or when the formal part ends. Estimate how many guests will be there at that moment.
2. **Estimate drinks per hour at the peak.** How many each guest orders in that hour, depending on the type of event and the menu. It's your estimate, and it gets better with your own past events.
3. **Measure what one bar station serves per hour.** A beer and a three-ingredient cocktail don't have the same pace. If you don't have the figure, time several real drinks from your menu during the bar trial.
4. **Divide:** drinks per hour at the peak, divided by what one station serves. That gives the **simultaneous stations** you need.
5. **Split them into bars.** Several small bars spread the queue better than one very long one. The number comes from the space and how people move around it.
6. **Add barbacks and relief staff.** If the event lasts many hours, stations have to stay covered while someone eats or takes a break.

An example to see the mechanics, with assumed numbers rather than benchmarks: if at the peak hour you expect 600 drinks and in your trial one station served 100 per hour, you need 6 stations at the same time. If the menu switches to cocktails, that 100 drops and the stations go up.

The menu is the variable that moves the calculation most. Simplifying it (a few drinks, some pre-batched) is often cheaper than adding people. The full event calculation is in [how many staff you need for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event), and the rota in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).

## What to ask before confirming

- **What kind of bars have you worked?** Volume, cocktails, company events.
- **Do you have a valid food handler card?** In Argentina, article 21 of the Argentine Food Code requires it of anyone who is or may be in contact with food; it lasts three years and is valid nationwide. The details are in [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work). Elsewhere, the name and requirements depend on your country or city.
- **How are you getting home?** Events finish late and teardown even later.
- **What tools do you bring?** If you expect them to bring their own, say so in the offer.

## What each side has to provide

Write it in the offer, item by item. This is the usual split, but what counts is what you agree:

| Item | Who provides it (define it) |
|---|---|
| Drinks and supplies (fruit, syrups, garnish) | Organiser, caterer or bar supplier |
| Ice and restocking during service | Organiser or supplier, with a named person responsible |
| Physical bar, glassware, disposables | Venue, caterer or bar supplier |
| Bar tools | To be agreed: organiser or bartender |
| Water, electricity and a place to wash up | Venue |
| Clothing | Bartender, according to what the offer asks |
| Menu and service standards | Organiser |

One rule to write in the brief: in Argentina, Law 24,788 bans the sale of alcoholic drinks to under-18s nationwide. If there may be minors at your event (employees' children, for example), the bar has to know how to handle it.

## Common mistakes

- **Calculating by guests rather than by peak.** Five hundred guests spread over four hours are not five hundred in the first half hour.
- **Not hiring barbacks.** The bartender goes off to fetch ice and the bar stops serving at the worst moment.
- **A cocktail menu with beer-level staffing.** The menu sets the pace; if the menu changes, redo the calculation.
- **Calling staff for opening time.** With no setup time, the first guest waits while the fruit gets cut.
- **Leaving no margin.** The bar is one of the roles where a missing person shows straight away. If it happens, read [what to do when event staff don't show up](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do).

A quinceañera party (in Argentina, the "fiesta de 15" that marks a girl's fifteenth birthday) is set up much like a wedding, but it has one difference that changes almost everything: most of the guests are minors. That shapes how the door is controlled, what the bar serves and, above all, how guests leave at the end. This guide goes through each role and what to agree with the venue and the family before the date.

## First: what the venue brings and what the family provides

Before you count people, ask what the venue includes. If you hire waiters on your own without asking, you end up with twice as many people in the room and half of them with nothing to do.

Make a simple list with three columns: what the venue provides, what the family provides and what the production provides. Every role in this guide goes into one of the three. Whatever isn't in any column is what gets forgotten.

And name a single family member as the contact on the day. Not the birthday girl or her mother, who will be at the party: someone trusted who can answer "is this boy invited?" or "who is this girl leaving with?" without missing what is happening on the dance floor.

## Reception and guest control

Reception is the first filter and, at a quinceañera, the most important one.

- **A closed guest list.** The family hands it over a few days before, with first and last names. If guests arrive in groups (the school class, for example), it helps to have them grouped on the list.
- **What to do with someone not on the list.** Reception doesn't decide: they call the family contact. That has to be written into the role's offer.
- **Arrival pace.** Quinceañera guests tend to arrive in waves. If you want to know how many reception points you need, the calculation by pace is in [how many staff you need for an event](/en/blog/how-many-staff-for-an-event).

If you are also putting someone on the door for access control, set out their role in the same brief.

## Waiters

Table service works as at any other party: welcome canapés, starter, main course, dessert and a sweet table. What changes is the rhythm of the dance floor: young guests get up early and rarely come back to the table, which affects when plates are cleared and when things are topped up.

Agree with the venue who is in charge of service. If the waiters belong to the venue and you add extra staff, they must answer to a single floor manager. [Event waiter jobs](/en/blog/event-waiter-jobs) covers what is expected in the role, which is useful when writing the offer.

## An alcohol-free bar for minors

There is no leeway here: in Argentina, Law 24,788 bans the sale of alcoholic drinks to anyone under 18 throughout the national territory.

In practice, at a quinceañera this is handled in one of two ways, decided by the family with the venue:

1. **An alcohol-free bar for everyone.** Mocktails, soft drinks, juices and water. It is the simplest to control.
2. **An alcohol-free bar for minors and a separate bar for adults.** Physically separate or with different opening times, and with a written rule about who is served and how age is checked if in doubt.

What doesn't work is leaving the decision to the bartender in the moment. The rule goes into the bar brief before the event. [Hiring bartenders for a corporate event](/en/blog/hiring-bartenders-for-a-corporate-event) has more on putting that brief together, and the part about minors applies just the same.

## Cloakroom

At a quinceañera the cloakroom takes jackets, bags and often a change of clothes or shoes for the dance floor. That means bags as well as hangers.

The usual applies: numbered tickets, rails in sections, a sign listing what isn't accepted, and sizing the team for the exit. At a quinceañera the exit is even more concentrated, because many families come to pick up at the same time. All the details are in [how to run an event cloakroom](/en/blog/how-to-run-an-event-cloakroom).

## Coordination with the venue and the family

One coordinator who isn't busy with anything else. Their job is to:

- Hold the event schedule (entrance, waltz, dinner, the party-favour round known locally as the carioca, sweet table, close) and tell each role what is coming next.
- Be the only bridge with the family contact and the venue manager.
- Handle the unexpected: someone feeling unwell, a guest not on the list, a minor who wants to leave early.

If you are unsure whether you need one, the criteria are in [when you need an event staff coordinator](/en/blog/when-you-need-an-event-staff-coordinator).

## Timing and the minors' exit

The most delicate moment of a quinceañera is not the entrance, it is the end.

- **Agree the closing time and the pick-up time** with the family, and put them on the invitation if possible.
- **Decide which door guests are collected from** and have reception there, with the same list from the entrance.
- **Who goes home with whom.** If the family wants each minor to leave with an authorised adult, reception marks on the list who left and with whom. If a minor wants to leave alone and isn't authorised, the family contact is called.
- **Nobody on the staff leaves before the last guest.** Reception and cloakroom are among the last to close, and that has to be in the hours on the offer.

If the party is long, plan relief shifts: [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events) explains how to think about them.

## Common mistakes

- **Hiring waiters without asking what the venue brings.**
- **Not having a guest list**, or having it on the phone of a family member who is dancing.
- **Leaving the alcohol rule to the bar's judgement.**
- **Treating the exit as the end of the job**, when it is the moment that needs the most care.

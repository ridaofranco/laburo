A summer event on the coast has everything any event has, plus three complications: the town is full, the people who know the job have already committed their season, and the weather decides more than in any indoor venue. This guide is for the people hiring: how to build the team, what to sort out if people are travelling and what to have decided beforehand. If you're staff and want to work on the coast, the guide for you is [summer event jobs on the coast](/en/blog/summer-event-jobs-on-the-coast).

In Argentina, the summer season runs from December to February, and seaside towns fill up with events, beach clubs and brand activations.

## A seasonal town is not your town

The first step is accepting that you won't hire the way you would in a big city in a quiet month:

- **The available people are busy.** Bars, beach clubs, hotels and other events are looking for the same profiles in the same weeks.
- **Everything takes longer.** Transport, suppliers and accommodation have to be arranged further ahead.
- **A last-minute replacement is harder.** If someone drops out on a Saturday in January, there's no long list to call. That's why it pays to have a plan ready, like the one in [event staff no-show: what to do](/en/blog/event-staff-no-show-what-to-do).

## Local people or a travelling crew

It isn't one or the other. What helps is deciding post by post.

**Local people**
- They know the town, the access routes and the venue's hours.
- They don't need accommodation or long journeys.
- If a replacement is needed, they're nearby.
- But in high season they're usually working somewhere else, so you have to call them earlier.

**People who travel**
- They let you bring roles you can't find locally (coordination, tech, a team that has worked with you before).
- They arrive with your production's way of doing things.
- But you have to sort out accommodation, transport, meals and travel days, and all of that has to be clear from the offer.

A mix that tends to work: coordination and key roles travel, and the rest of the team is built with people already in the area.

## Accommodation, transport and expenses: spelled out

What causes problems at an event with travelling staff is almost never the shift pay. It's everything else. Before sending the offer, write down:

- **Accommodation.** If production provides it, where it is, from which night to which morning and whether it's shared.
- **Travel.** How people get to the town and back, who buys the ticket and for which date.
- **Local transport.** How to get from the accommodation to the site and back in the early hours.
- **Expenses and meals.** If there are any, what they're for and which days they cover. If there aren't, say that too.
- **Travel days.** Whether they're paid or not.

The amount doesn't have to be the same for everyone, but nobody should find out in town about something that wasn't in the offer. For the payment side, see [how to organise event staff payments](/en/blog/how-to-organise-event-staff-payments).

## Shifts in the heat

On the coast, a daytime event happens in the sun. What to sort out beforehand:

- **Rotate the exposed posts** (access, bars without a roof, wayfinding on the beach) with posts in the shade.
- **Water for staff** throughout the day, at a dedicated point.
- **Shade at every fixed post.**
- **Shifts that aren't one long stretch.** If the event runs all day, split it, as explained in [shift planning for long events](/en/blog/shift-planning-for-long-events).

Argentina's Ministry of Health recommends avoiding direct sun exposure between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., wearing loose, light, light-coloured clothing, and resting at any sign of dizziness or fatigue. It also lists the warning signs of heatstroke: a body temperature of 39 °C or more, red, hot and dry skin, rapid breathing and pulse, confusion or loss of consciousness. Whoever coordinates needs to know them and where the medical service is.

## Rain plan

In summer, a storm can roll in within an hour. Decide beforehand, in writing:

- **Who decides** whether to carry on, pause or call it off, and how the decision reaches the team.
- **Where people take shelter** and which equipment has to be covered or moved first.
- **What happens to the shift** if it's called off halfway. Put it in the offer.
- **What to bring.** If the event is in the open, ask for a raincoat and closed shoes in the call.

Many of these decisions are the same as at a festival: they're developed in [outdoor festival staffing](/en/blog/outdoor-festival-staffing).

## Book early

In high season, experienced people commit early. The order that works:

1. **Confirm the key roles first** (coordination, tech, the travelling team).
2. **Book accommodation** as soon as you know how many people are travelling.
3. **Then the local team**, which can be closed nearer the date, though not at the last minute.

The same logic of booking before diaries fill up is in [booking event staff for December](/en/blog/booking-event-staff-for-december).

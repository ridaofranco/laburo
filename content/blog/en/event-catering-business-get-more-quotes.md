A caterer competes on one very specific question: "How much is it per head?". The problem is that the question can't be answered without knowing the style, the numbers, what's included and the venue. This guide is about making your listing and your replies settle that from the start, so the people who ask you for a price are the ones you suit, and you close more of those who write to you. It doesn't give market prices: everyone knows their own.

## Your service styles, one by one

Whoever organises an event thinks about the style first and the menu second. If your listing only says "event catering", they don't know whether you do what they're looking for. Split it up:

- **Cocktail.** Canapés passed around, food stations, guests standing. Say how many rounds or bites per person your base offer has and how long the service lasts.
- **Banquet or table service.** Starter, main and dessert, with guests seated. Say how many courses and whether it's plated or served from platters at the table.
- **Coffee break.** For conferences, training sessions and company days. Say what's included (hot drinks, water, something sweet, something savoury) and whether it's topped up during the day.
- **Anything else you do:** working lunch, barbecue, cheese table, themed stations, cocktail bar.

On LABURO, add **one service per style**. That way, someone searching "coffee break" or "cocktail" finds yours and reads exactly what interests them. If you also run a bar or a food truck, those go under their own category (**Bars and drinks**, **Food trucks**), because the directory's category filter has to match exactly.

## The sample menu and special options

A sample menu for each style is the best showcase of your style and scale. It doesn't have to be the final one: you make clear it's a reference and that menus are tailored.

What can't be missing:

- **Special options.** Vegetarian, vegan, gluten free, other restrictions. Say which ones you do and whether they're cooked separately. If you can't guarantee something, say so: it's better to lose a booking than to have a problem with a guest.
- **Children's menus**, if you offer them (birthday parties, weddings with families).
- **Drinks:** whether they're included, extra or handled with a bar partner.

## What's included (and what isn't)

This is the part that most confuses people comparing quotes. Two prices per head may be quoting different things. For each style, write down:

- **Crockery and linen:** do you provide it, does the venue, or is it hired separately?
- **Staff:** are waiters, cooks, a dishwasher or a service manager included? How many per number of guests in your base offer?
- **Kitchen equipment:** do you bring ovens, hobs, fridges, or do you need the venue to have a kitchen?
- **Hours of service** included, and what happens if the event runs over.
- **Setup and cleaning** of your area.
- **Transport**, and where you set off from.

If you don't provide waiters, say so clearly: the client will have to find them separately, and if they know beforehand, they can organise it. If you do, explain how more are added when the event grows. Whoever builds that team can read [event waiter jobs](/en/blog/event-waiter-jobs) to see the role from the inside.

## Minimum numbers and notice

- **Minimum guests** per style. If a coffee break for 15 doesn't work for you, say so.
- **The maximum you can cover** on one date, with your team and your kitchen.
- **How much notice** you need to confirm the final headcount, and until when it can change.
- **Deposit and balance:** how much, when and what it secures.

Write it once, paste it into every reply and put it in the service details.

## The food handler card

In Argentina, according to **article 21 of the Argentine Food Code** and ANMAT (the national food and drug agency), anyone who is or may be in contact with food where it is produced, portioned, stored, transported or sold must have a food handler card. It is issued by the health authority of each jurisdiction and is **valid throughout the country**. Elsewhere, the name and requirements depend on your country or city.

If your team's cards are up to date, say so on your listing: it's a question many companies ask before hiring. Everything about how to get it is in [food handler certificate for event work](/en/blog/food-handler-certificate-for-event-work).

## How to answer the request

When someone requests a quote from your listing, it reaches you by email and you see it in your dashboard, under your inquiries. In your inquiry settings you can add the questions you won't quote without: number of guests, style, whether the venue has a kitchen, dietary restrictions, timings.

When you reply:

1. **Confirm the date**, or say you don't have it.
2. **Propose the style** that suits them, with the sample menu.
3. **Break it down**: price per head, what's included and what's extra.
4. **Paste your terms:** minimum, notice, deposit.
5. **If a detail is missing, ask for it** in the same message.

If a production company invites you to a quote request, you get a link to enter your quote line by line, with what's included and what isn't. How to do it well is in [how to reply to an event quote request](/en/blog/how-to-reply-to-an-event-quote-request).

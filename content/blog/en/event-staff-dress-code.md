At an event, what the staff wear isn't a detail. It tells guests who to ask, it keeps the technical crew invisible on stage and it's what a brand pays for when it sets up an activation. That's why almost every offer says something about clothing, and why it's one of the things most worth clarifying before you say yes.

This guide goes through what you can be asked for, what to have ready and what to ask.

## All black

It's the most common code. It means **everything visible is black**: T-shirt or shirt, trousers, socks and shoes. No prints, no logos, no shiny details.

It's mostly requested for roles that need to go unnoticed: technical crew, backstage, setup during the event, and in many cases floor and bar staff. The idea is that guests see the event, not the team.

Typical questions worth asking beforehand:

- **T-shirt or shirt?** A corporate event is not the same as a concert.
- **Are black jeans ok?** Sometimes yes, sometimes no.
- **Black trainers or shoes?** It depends on the event.
- **Long sleeves?** Some venues ask for them even when it's hot.

A tip: if you work regularly, keep a full black outfit just for events, in good condition and not faded. It saves you the "what do I wear?" question every time.

## Brand uniform

At activations, sampling, stands and company events, the brand often wants the team in its clothing: a T-shirt, sweatshirt, cap or vest with the logo. What you need to clarify:

- **What they give you and what you bring.** Often they give you the top and you provide trousers and shoes in a set colour.
- **Your size.** Give it accurately when asked: a uniform that doesn't fit shows in the photos.
- **When and where you collect it.** Sometimes it's handed out at the venue; other times, days before.
- **Whether it has to be returned** at the end and in what condition.
- **Whether it's washed between days** when the event runs for several days, and who does it.

If you work in reception, [corporate event hostess and reception](/en/blog/corporate-event-hostess-and-reception) covers what else those roles require.

## Formal or "floor" attire

At weddings, galas and some corporate events, the venue usually has its own code: a shirt (often white or black), dark trousers, an apron or waistcoat, closed shoes. In event hospitality this is a frequent request; [event waiter jobs](/en/blog/event-waiter-jobs) covers the rest of the role.

Ask whether the apron or waistcoat is provided by the venue or whether you bring it.

## Footwear

Footwear is the most underestimated item and the one people suffer most. You'll be standing or walking for many hours.

- **In setup and technical roles** you may be asked for safety boots. If so, ask whether they're provided or you bring them. More on that in [event setup and teardown: how to work safely](/en/blog/event-setup-and-teardown-work-safely).
- **On the floor and at reception** closed, smart shoes are usually requested.
- **If you're asked to wear heels,** ask for how many hours, whether there's a moment to change and where to leave the other pair.
- **Outdoors** (grass, sand, boards), stable footwear is worth more than pretty footwear.

Try your shoes beforehand. Breaking in new shoes on a ten-hour shift is a bad idea.

## Grooming

Beyond clothes, whoever hires you looks at how you arrive:

- **Clean, ironed clothes**, with no lint or stains.
- **Neat hair**, tied back if the role requires it (bar, catering, kitchen).
- **Short, clean nails** if you handle food or drinks.
- **Minimal accessories.** In technical or setup roles, nothing that can catch.
- **Credential or ID** visible if you're given one.

Grooming isn't about looks: it's about being tidy. What matters is that it's clear you came ready to work.

## What to ask before accepting

The list for any offer:

1. **What's the dress code?** As specific as possible: colour, type of garment, footwear.
2. **Do they give me anything?** Uniform, apron, protective equipment.
3. **When do I collect it, and does it have to be returned?**
4. **Is there somewhere to change** and leave my things at the venue?
5. **Does anything change if the event is outdoors** or if it rains?

If they ask for something you don't have, say so right then. Sometimes an alternative works and sometimes it doesn't, but it's much better to know beforehand than to arrive in something else.

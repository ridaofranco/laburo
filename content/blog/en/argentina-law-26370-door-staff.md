If you work the door at an event in Argentina, or you're the one putting together the access team, there's a national law that's about you: **Law 26,370**, regulated by **Decree 1824/2009**. This guide summarises what both say, without adding anything that isn't in the official text.

> **Note:** this is general information to help you find your way. It isn't legal advice. Before licensing staff or opening a venue, check the rules in force in your jurisdiction and, if necessary, a professional.

## What Law 26,370 is and which rules go with it

The law was passed on **7 May 2008**. Its purpose (article 1) is to set the **rules for licensing staff who carry out admission and stay control of the public**, hired directly or through service companies, and to define their functions.

InfoLEG, Argentina's official legislation database, lists four rules that complement it: **Decree 1824/2009**, which regulates it; two resolutions of the Ministry of Justice (1024/2013 and 1826/2015) on agreements to implement the national register; and Resolution 33/2016 of the Ministry of Security, which creates a register of the right of admission at football matches.

Two definitions:

- **Right of admission and stay** (article 4): the power of the owner of the venue or event to admit or exclude people, **as long as it's based on objective conditions**, not contrary to the Constitution and not involving discriminatory or arbitrary treatment.
- **Admission and stay control** (article 5): the tasks carried out by **employed workers** to ensure those conditions are met.

## Who it applies to

The law applies to those who organise and run **events and musical, artistic and general entertainment shows** in stadiums, clubs, pubs, nightclubs, bars, restaurants and any other public entertainment venue, including **open spaces or the public road** (articles 1 and 2).

The law **invites the provinces and Buenos Aires City to adhere** (article 33). Each jurisdiction defines its enforcement authority, keeps its register and decides which institutions run the courses (articles 34, 35 and 37). That's why the actual procedure depends on where you work.

**Buenos Aires City.** The City adhered in 2011 (Law 4,010) and in 2013 moved the matter into its own Permits Code (Law 4,855, which amended Law 1,913), with Law 26,370 applying to anything not covered. Since those rules currently appear as no longer in force in the City's Official Gazette, check with the City authority which register applies before starting any procedure.

## What the requirements are

Article 7 of the law, with the detail added by the decree:

- Two years of **actual residence** in the country.
- Being **over 18**.
- **Compulsory education** completed. The decree asks for **completed secondary school**, with a certified certificate.
- **Criminal record certificate** from the National Register of Repeat Offenders, issued no more than **60 days** before.
- **Psychological fitness certificate**, from a public authority or a recognised institution.
- **Technical qualification certificate**: according to the decree, obtained by passing the course in article 17.
- Being an **employee**, of the venue owner or of a service company.

The criminal record and psychological fitness certificates are submitted to the register **once a year**.

Article 8 sets out incompatibilities: people on active duty in the armed forces, security forces, police, prison service or intelligence agencies cannot do this work, nor can anyone convicted of offences in this activity or sentenced to more than three years, among other cases.

## Register, card and badge

Whoever meets the requirements is **licensed by the authority of each jurisdiction** (article 12). The decree created the **RENCAP**, a national register with **open access** that brings together what the jurisdictions send.

- **Professional card** (article 13): name, ID number, category, licence number, town and province. According to the decree, it's **valid for one year**, shows the expiry date and is renewed by submitting the criminal record and psychological fitness certificates again.
- **Identification badge** (article 14): first name, surname, photo, licence number and the words "Control de Admisión y Permanencia". It's worn **at chest height, on the left side**, always visible (article 9). It's handed over together with the card.

## Categories and training

Article 15 creates three categories: **controller**, **specialised controller** and **admission and stay control technician**. To move up you need to pass the course and have seniority: three years as a controller to become specialised and five as specialised to become a technician (article 16).

The controller course (article 17) includes, among other topics, human rights, addictions, safety against accidents and disasters, non-violent communication and first aid.

## What they can and can't do

Among their obligations (article 9): equal and respectful treatment, **asking for ID** when age is a requirement and helping the injured while notifying the authorities.

They can **refuse entry or stay** (article 11) for violent behaviour, evident drug use or drunkenness that bothers or endangers others, weapons or fireworks, racist or xenophobic symbols, conduct that disrupts the show, full capacity, closing time reached or under-18s when the law requires it.

They are prohibited (article 10) from working **with weapons**, **without a licence**, **under the influence of alcohol** or drugs, disclosing customers' information and obstructing their political, social and union rights.

Discriminating, not keeping conditions equal or **abandoning people during an emergency** are serious offences (article 21) and lead to the licence being cancelled for five years (article 23). Not wearing the badge visibly is a minor offence (article 22) and can mean suspension of up to one year, a fine or a warning. Two minor offences in a year count as a serious one.

## What people who hire should know

- **Hire licensed staff** and ask them to prove it (articles 26 and 27). The decree says it's proven with the **professional card**.
- **Minimum staffing** (article 28): one controller for every 80 people present at the same time; with more than 200, a specialised one; with more than 400, a technician. In open spaces with no predetermined capacity, the decree requires telling the authority **72 hours** in advance how many tickets are sold.
- **Insurance** for damage to attendees and third parties, a stamped **incident book** and a **notice board** listing the staff (article 29). The decree specifies the board: no more than one metre from the main entrance, lit, in capital letters, in alphabetical order and with the text of Law 23,592 on discriminatory acts.
- **A sign at every entrance** with the admission conditions, the price of the ticket or minimum spend and the prohibitions and restrictions (articles 30 and 32).

If staff commit offences, the owner can also be fined and, for repeat offences, the law allows for closure (article 24). The 2008 amounts are updated according to the decree.

Carry your valid card, keep the badge visible on your chest and apply the same rules to everyone.

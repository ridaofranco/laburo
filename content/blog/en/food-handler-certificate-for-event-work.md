If you work, or want to work, in food and drink at events in Argentina, sooner or later someone will ask whether you have your food handler card ("carnet de manipulador de alimentos"). This guide explains what it is, who needs it, how to get it and how long it lasts, based on what the official sources say and nothing more. If you work outside Argentina, the logic is similar but the name and requirements depend on your country or city.

## What it is and where it comes from

The food handler card is established in **article 21 of the Argentine Food Code** (Código Alimentario Argentino), in the wording given by **Joint Resolution 12/2019** (published in the Official Gazette on 30 April 2019).

What the article says, in short:

- Anyone who works in contact with food or its raw materials must have a food handler card, issued by the competent health authority.
- The **only requirement** to get it is passing a safe food handling course.
- The card is **personal and non-transferable**: it carries your photo, your details, the date of issue and the expiry date.
- It is **valid throughout the country**.
- The employer has to guarantee the conditions for staff to be able to get it.
- Each health authority keeps a public register of recognised trainers and trained food handlers.

## Who needs it at an event

The rule covers anyone who is, or may be, in contact with food in places where it is produced, portioned, stored, transported or sold. Translated into event positions:

| Position | Why the rule applies |
|---|---|
| Cook, kitchen assistant | Prepares and handles food the whole shift |
| Bartender, barback, barista | Makes drinks, cuts fruit, handles ice |
| Waiter, runner | Serves and carries food and drink |
| Dishwasher | Washes and handles crockery and kitchen utensils |
| Sampling or tasting | Hands out food or drinks to the public |

In practice, whoever runs the food and drink at an event (a caterer, an outsourced bar, the venue itself) is the one answerable to the health authority, which is why they usually ask for a valid card when they recruit. If you're going to work in these positions, keeping it up to date saves you from being left out of a call.

## How to get it (national rule)

According to ANMAT (Argentina's food and drug agency) and the text of article 21, the route is the same everywhere in the country:

1. **You take the Safe Food Handling Course**, taught by trainers recognised by the health authority of your jurisdiction. It can be run by a public institution (municipal, provincial or national) or a private one, as long as the trainer is recognised.
2. **The course lasts at least 7 hours** and can be in person or online. It is theoretical and practical.
3. **You sit the final assessment, which is always in person.** It is given by a professional from the health authority or by a trainer registered and authorised by it.
4. **The health authority issues the card.**

If you have a tertiary or university qualification that includes food handling training, you can be exempted from the course, but you still have to pass the assessment.

Health checks are not a national requirement, although provincial health authorities can ask for whichever ones they consider necessary.

## How to get it in Buenos Aires City

In Buenos Aires City (CABA) the procedure is handled by the **Government Control Agency (AGC)** and, according to the official city government page, it is **free**. The steps:

1. **Register as a student** on the AGC food platform (alimentos.agcontrol.gob.ar), with your name exactly as it appears on your national ID (DNI), an email address you use often, a copy of the front and back of your DNI and a recent 4x4 photo.
2. **Choose how to study**: online or in-person course. If you take the online one, you have to go through all the material and book the exam within three months; otherwise you have to watch it again. The in-person course requires full attendance.
3. **Sit the in-person exam** at the AGC offices. You pass with 80% correct answers.
4. **Download the card** from the platform, under "Mis inscripciones" (My registrations), once the exam has been validated.

In the city, renewal can be done online if you joined the register after 2014.

If you live in another province or municipality, the procedure is set by your local health authority. What doesn't change is the national rule: a course of at least 7 hours, an in-person exam, three years' validity and validity throughout the country.

## How long it lasts and how to renew it

- **Validity: three years** from issue.
- **Compulsory renewal** on expiry, with a knowledge exam. The health authority can also ask for a refresher course.
- **If you fail the renewal exam twice in a row**, you have to take and pass the full course again.

A practical tip: write down the expiry date. For whoever is recruiting you, an expired card is the same as not having one.

## National validity: why it matters at events

Since Joint Resolution 12/2019 the card **is valid throughout Argentina**, even if you move or work in another province.

For people who work at events this is especially useful: if you're called for an event outside your city, the card you got in your jurisdiction still counts. If that kind of job interests you, read [staffing an event in another province](/en/blog/staffing-an-event-in-another-province).
